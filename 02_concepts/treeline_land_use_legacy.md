# Land-Use Legacy and Treeline Dynamics

**Summary**: In the Alps and other long-settled mountains, the current forest edge often lies below its climatic potential because of centuries of grazing and wood use; abandonment since the 19th/20th century creates a "land-use gap" that trees can fill. Climate then acts as accelerator or window of opportunity. Studies disagree on the relative weight of the two, and attributing change to either from remote sensing alone is not possible.

## Evidence That Land Use Sets the Starting Point

- Swiss Alps resurvey: at slightly lower elevations treeline advance occurred mainly where land use was present in 1900, whereas current land use had no significant effect; above ~2330 m advance occurred on microsites without dwarf-shrub competition (source: [[frei_2023_treeline_shift]]).
- Southern French Alps: the colonisation front (about 2300 m) was below the climatic treeline of both 1961–1990 and 1991–2020, interpreted as a legacy of pastoral use; population fell sharply 1840–1950 (source: [[nicoud_2025_landuse_larch_expansion]]).
- Eastern Alps simulation: under grassland abandonment forest expands upward within the 21st century irrespective of climate warming because the present edge is mainly land-use constrained (source: [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]]). This is a model scenario assuming full abandonment, not a forecast.
- Pan-European expert synthesis (15 mountain units): land-use history, geomorphology and soils explained most of the variability near the treeline; abandonment may matter more than temperature for grassland diversity loss (source: [[cudlin_2017_drivers_treeline_shift]]).
- Review of Central Austrian treeline ecophysiology: current seedling re-establishment is an invasion of potential habitat after reduced grazing, not an upward migration; the treeline behaves "conservatively" (source: [[wieser_2019_effects_climate_treeline]]).
- Review across taxa: a large part of recent upward woody-plant shift was attributed to land abandonment, with forest-only studies likely to under-detect shifts beyond the forest limit (source: [[vitasse_2021_shifts_plants_alps]]).

## Where Climate Re-enters

- Southern French Alps: the acceleration of larch establishment around 2003 (95 % interval 1995–2010) coincided with a favourable climate window (earlier snow melt-out, fewer cold waves, more heat waves) while sheep stocking remained about constant since the 1950s; the title concludes "climate, not land-use", but the paper itself says abandonment provided the preconditions (source: [[nicoud_2025_landuse_larch_expansion]]).
- Swiss resurvey: the authors judge warming the likelier driver of advance of the uppermost trees (source: [[frei_2023_treeline_shift]]).
- Stand density after abandonment also changes growth responses: larch growth in abandoned, closed stands decoupled from warming after 2000 (source: [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]]) — see [[larch_growth_climate]].

## Dispersal and Establishment Limitation

- Pine shrub spread simulations show slow advance driven by dispersal kernel shape and spatially patterned resistance of resident grassland, so land-cover and vegetation structure above the treeline matter alongside climate (source: [[dullinger_2004_climate_treeline_shifts]]).
- Newly established trees were mostly infilling (79 % of cells) rather than advance (21 %) in the Swiss resurvey (source: [[frei_2023_treeline_shift]]); larch advanced steadily, pine mainly in particular habitats, spruce mostly infilled.

## Critical Notes

- The sources disagree on emphasis (land use vs climate); differences partly reflect regions (Swiss inner Alps, southern French Alps with extensive sheep grazing, simulated Stubai Valley) and methods (resurvey, dendrochronology, model, expert synthesis). No source here separates the two effects causally across regions.
- Land-use records are coarse (maps from 1900/1982, census, stocking since 1959, landowner statements) and collinear with terrain suitability (source: [[frei_2023_treeline_shift]], [[nicoud_2025_landuse_larch_expansion]]).
- Dendrochronological ages come from surviving trees only (survivorship bias) (source: [[nicoud_2025_landuse_larch_expansion]]).
- Implication for remote-sensing work: Landsat- or UAV-derived treeline shifts (see [[treeline_remote_sensing_monitoring]]) record outcome, not cause, so land-use history should be a covariate or stratifier when attributing shifts to climate. Landsat studies in addition detect densification more than displacement (source: [[menegaldo_2025_landsat_treeline_dynamics]]).
