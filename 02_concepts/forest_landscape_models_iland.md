# Forest Landscape Simulation with iLand

**Summary**: iLand is an individual-based, spatially explicit forest landscape model used for the Stubai Valley (Tyrol) to explore long-term responses of forest composition, structure and the upper forest-grassland ecotone to climate, disturbance and land use. The results indicate a tipping point near +2 °C and strong dependence of ecotone expansion on land-use abandonment, but they are not empirically validated for these transitions.

**Sources**: [[albrich_2020_transitions_mountain_forests]], [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]], [[albrich_2019_climate_change_mountain_forests]]

**Last updated**: 2026-10-15

---

## Tipping Points and Hysteresis

- Stepwise warming up to +6 °C and then cooling (1000 simulated years per step, 10 replicates, four precipitation scenarios) shows a transition above ~+2 °C from spruce-dominated stands with many large trees to smaller, mostly broadleaved trees (beech, then oak and Scots pine); *Pinus cembra* is lost above +2 °C (source: [[albrich_2020_transitions_mountain_forests]]).
- Topographic complexity delays and smooths the transition and provides refugia (high elevations, north slopes) but increases irreversibility: complex-topography runs did not return to the starting state under cooling, especially with less precipitation (source: [[albrich_2020_transitions_mountain_forests]]).
- At most 2 % of forest area lost tree cover even at +6 °C, so the result is a change of forest type, not forest loss (source: [[albrich_2020_transitions_mountain_forests]]).

## Ecotone Expansion Under Land-Use and Climate Scenarios

- Simulations 2020–2200 with grassland abandonment, four climate trajectories, wind and bark beetle and management give an upward-moving forest-grassland ecotone that stabilises before 2200 regardless of climate; larch showed the largest elevational shift (+329 to +339 m on south slopes by 2100) (source: [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]]).
- Climate mainly changes species composition and stocking at higher elevations, with stronger shifts on south-facing slopes (source: [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]]).

## Critical Notes

- Equilibrium states over 1000-year steps are statements about attractors, not about timing during this century (source: [[albrich_2020_transitions_mountain_forests]]).
- Not simulated: soil dynamics and nutrient feedbacks, ground-vegetation competition, CO₂ fertilisation, disturbances in the tipping-point study; microclimate and fine-scale (<1 ha) processes in the ecotone study; soil depth data above the forest edge were extrapolated (source: [[albrich_2020_transitions_mountain_forests]], [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]]).
- One valley, one model; the land-use finding follows partly from the scenario assumption of complete abandonment. Compare with observed rates, e.g. ~10 m per decade in [[frei_2023_treeline_shift]] and the dispersal-limited expansion in [[dullinger_2004_climate_treeline_shifts]].
- Implication for mapping: static species or forest-type maps may have a limited validity horizon, but the model gives no timing for this century.
