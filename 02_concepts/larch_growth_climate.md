# Larch and Conifer Growth Along Alpine Elevation Gradients

**Summary**: In dry inner-Alpine valleys (South Tyrol, Vinschgau) conifer growth is limited by water at low and mid elevation and by temperature only near the forest line; growth of *Larix decidua* no longer tracks warming after about 2000 in dense stands, and stand density (a legacy of land use) modulates this decoupling.

**Sources**: [[obojes_2018_water_stress_growth_larch]], [[obojes_2024_growth_limits_elevational]], [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]], [[albrich_2020_transitions_mountain_forests]]

**Last updated**: 2026-10-15

---

## Water Limitation at Low and Mid Elevation

- Along a transect from 1070 to 2250 m in a dry valley, larch sap flow and radial growth were reduced in dry periods up to ~1715 m; maximum annual growth occurred at ~2000 m; in a wet year elevations did not differ (source: [[obojes_2018_water_stress_growth_larch]]).
- Tree-ring growth correlated with precipitation up to ~1715 m and with temperature only above ~2000 m (source: [[obojes_2018_water_stress_growth_larch]]).
- Five conifers (spruce, larch, Scots pine, black pine, Swiss stone pine) at 24 sites (1000–2320 m): all species at low elevation responded to water availability incl. previous autumn; spruce was most drought-sensitive at mid elevation; differences between anisohydric larch and isohydric species were small at low elevation; a dry period since 2003 coincided with growth declines (source: [[obojes_2024_growth_limits_elevational]]).

## Temperature Response Near the Forest Line

- At high elevation the positive temperature correlation of *Pinus cembra* was weaker than expected, whereas larch responded positively to temperature and grew faster in recent decades (source: [[obojes_2024_growth_limits_elevational]]).
- The weak forest-line temperature signal is attributed by the authors to the forest line being depressed by pasture use and to recent warming; this is interpretation, not tested (source: [[obojes_2024_growth_limits_elevational]]).

## Decoupling from Warming and Stand Density

- In seven pairs of subalpine larch stands (~1723 m) May–August temperature promoted growth in open and closed stands, but after 2000 BAI no longer increased with warming; the decline was stronger with higher stand density, and a temperature × July precipitation interaction showed that warm summers help only with enough moisture (source: [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]]).
- Management (mowing, grazing, thinning) in continuously managed larch grasslands reduced competition; abandonment and densification increased it (source: [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]]).
- Related simulations show larch to be among the species increasing at the ecotone under warming in the Stubai Valley (source: [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]]) — see [[forest_landscape_models_iland]].

## Critical Notes

- All three studies are from the Central/Eastern Alps (South Tyrol/Tyrol), mostly dry inner-alpine sites; findings do not transfer to humid outer Alps.
- Tree-ring data only cover surviving trees; ground truth for management history in [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]] rests on landowner reports and 1970s aerial photos; mowing/grazing and thinning are collinear (r = 0.995).
- Space-for-time (elevation) designs assume elevation effects mirror temporal climate change ([[obojes_2018_water_stress_growth_larch]], [[obojes_2024_growth_limits_elevational]]).
- Larch budmoth outbreaks (~9-year cycle) confound growth series and had to be modelled.
- Subalpine stands (~1700 m) are below the treeline, so conclusions for treeline larch are extrapolations; see [[treeline_ecotone_theory]] and [[treeline_land_use_legacy]].
