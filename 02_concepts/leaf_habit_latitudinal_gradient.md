# Leaf Habit and Latitudinal Gradients in Forests

**Summary**: Broad-leaved woody plants shift from evergreen to deciduous leaf habit with increasing latitude in response to climatic seasonality, but the strength of this shift differs between canopy trees and understory shrubs due to canopy buffering of the understory microclimate.

**Sources**: [[jin_2023_drivers_differentiation_evergreen]], [[herraiz_2025_phen_shifts_mediterranean]], [[hiebl_2025_pretraining]]

**Last updated**: 2026-05-05

---

## The Latitudinal Evergreen–Deciduous Gradient

In broad-leaved forests of subtropical eastern Asia, dominance shifts from evergreen to deciduous angiosperm species with increasing latitude — and analogous patterns occur across other subtropical and temperate forest zones globally.

**Primary climatic drivers:**
- **Temperature (MAT)**: Lower MAT → shorter growing seasons, more frequent sub-zero temperatures → deciduous leaf habit minimises metabolic costs (respiration, hydraulic failure risk) in unfavorable seasons
- **Precipitation (AP)**: Lower AP → greater water stress in dry seasons → deciduous leaf habit reduces transpiration loss
- Temperature is the stronger driver; precipitation plays a secondary, independent role (source: [[jin_2023_drivers_differentiation_evergreen]])

**Phylogenetic conservatism:**
- Leaf habit is strongly phylogenetically conserved (Pagel's λ = 0.835, p < 0.001 for 523 angiosperm species in subtropical China)
- Community-level leaf habit composition therefore reflects both evolutionary history and contemporary climate filtering (source: [[jin_2023_drivers_differentiation_evergreen]])

## Tree vs. Shrub Differentiation: The Canopy Buffering Mechanism

A key insight from Jin & Qian (2023): **the rate of leaf habit shift along the temperature gradient is significantly higher in canopy trees than in understory shrubs**.

**Why trees shift more than shrubs:**
- Canopy trees are directly exposed to climatic extremes (temperature, radiation, wind, ice storms)
- Forest canopy buffers the understory microclimate: reduces light and heat extremes, moderates temperature fluctuation → understory shrubs experience a more stable, resource-limited environment
- In stable, dim understory conditions, the **evergreen leaf habit is advantageous**: longer leaf lifespan amortises leaf construction cost in low-resource settings (Givnish 2002)
- Result: shrubs remain more evergreen than predicted from regional climate alone

**Consequences for forest composition:**
- At a given latitude (MAT), understory shrubs are proportionally more evergreen than canopy trees
- The deciduous:evergreen ratio differs systematically between forest strata — pooling growth forms obscures this pattern

## Latitudinal Convergence of Tree and Shrub Responses

**H2 (Jin & Qian 2023)**: At higher latitudes, the differentiation in leaf habit shift between trees and shrubs decreases.

**Mechanism: forest vertical structure simplification**
- At low latitudes: dense, multi-layered canopy → strong canopy buffering → large tree-shrub microclimate contrast
- At high latitudes: forest vertical structure simplifies, interior less foliated (Spicer et al. 2020) → canopy buffering weakens → trees and shrubs experience more similar effective climates
- Result: tree and shrub leaf habit responses converge at higher latitudes / colder climates

**Species range as a predictor of sensitivity:**
- Species with higher maximum distribution latitude (wider ranges extending northward) tolerate harsher climates regardless of growth form
- These wide-ranging species show less differentiation in leaf habit response between trees and shrubs (source: [[jin_2023_drivers_differentiation_evergreen]])
- Species geographical range is thus a functional property — a proxy for climatic niche breadth

## Evergreen Leaf Habit Economics

Why evergreen leaf habit persists in harsh climates (especially as understory shrubs):
- **Long leaf lifespan**: amortises leaf construction cost over multiple years; profitable in stable, low-light environments
- **Resource conservation**: evergreen leaves typically have lower SLA and higher leaf dry matter content (conservative end of the leaf economic spectrum) — well matched to stable, low-resource understory
- **Photosynthetic advantage at low latitudes**: longer photosynthetic season increases annual carbon gain compared to deciduous habit → favored in warm, aseasonal or mild-winter climates

Deciduous leaf habit advantages:
- **Avoidance of freeze damage**: shed leaves before freezing temperatures damage hydraulic systems
- **Reduced dark respiration** in winter: deciduous plants save energy during unfavorable seasons
- **Light capitalisation in early spring**: leaf-out before canopy closure allows understory plants to maximise early-season radiation

## Implications for Climate Change

- Climate warming shifts the deciduous/evergreen boundary toward higher latitudes → evergreen species expected to expand northward and upward in elevation
- Documented examples: evergreen broad-leaved expansion (*Quercus ilex*, *Olea europaea*) at temperate-Mediterranean transition in Italian National Parks (source: [[hiebl_2025_pretraining]])
- Mediterranean evergreen species (*Q. ilex*, *P. halepensis*, *P. nigra*) show positive NDVI trends over 28 years (1994–2021), consistent with expanding ranges under warming (source: [[herraiz_2025_phen_shifts_mediterranean]])
- Growth form matters: canopy trees are likely to respond faster to warming-driven range shifts than understory shrubs, due to canopy buffering that protects shrub understory from immediate climate exposure
- Remote sensing applications: monitoring the deciduous/evergreen ratio in forest canopy vs. understory requires multi-temporal imagery and structural information (e.g., LiDAR) to disentangle the two strata
