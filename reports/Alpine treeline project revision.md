# Rebuilding the Tyrolean treeline proposal on verified Alpine evidence

The proposal "Alpine forest tree line under stress" (*Pinus cembra*, Tyrol, 2026-2029) rests on a sound idea, but it cannot go out as written. **Eight of its Motivation citations are misattributed, mis-dated or do not support the claim they carry**, and its central premise, that drought and snow loss are compounding stressors at the upper Tyrolean treeline, has no verified Alpine support for *P. cembra*. The design problems are as serious. There is one transect per canopy position (n = 1 at site level), and the recruitment aim (O2) has no recruitment measurement. Landsat at 30 m cannot detect a treeline displacement, and Planet at 3 m cannot see juvenile crowns. The named drought years (2017, 2019, 2022) do not match the documented Alpine drought years (2003, 2015, 2018, 2022). The funding route "FWF 1000 Ideas" caps projects at 24 months, so it cannot carry a 3-year project. Year 1 (2026-27) is already running with no funding secured. The best Alpine evidence reframes the project as a test of whether drought has become co-limiting, in a landscape where land-use legacy dominates treeline position. A reframed project is more defensible and still publishable. The sections below follow the order needed for revision: citation errors, claim verdicts, aims, method feasibility, timeline and funding, references to add, and items to leave out.

Evidence base and limits: the underlying notes checked metadata against Crossref and OpenAlex and read abstracts where accessible. Publisher full texts (MDPI, Wiley, Elsevier) were mostly blocked, so verdicts on content rest on abstracts. The searches were limited (about 15 tool calls per note), so "not found" is not proof of absence. Items the notes could not confirm are listed in section 7 and should stay out of the proposal until checked.

## 2. Claim-by-claim verdicts

**"Upward shift of ~0.4 m/yr in the Alps over the last century."** Unsupported. It is not in the cited abstract (Frei et al., which reports **10 m per decade, about 1 m/yr, 1972/73-2012, maximum 42 m per decade** in one Swiss valley) ([Frei 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/f14020412)). No Alps-specific 0.4 m/yr source was found. Reported Alpine rates range from roughly 0.3 to more than 1 m/yr depending on site, definition and method. The South Tyrolean Landsat study found a mean advance of **+32 m (Landsat) and +45 m (orthophoto) over about 38 years** in 15 of 16 areas ([Menegaldo 2025](https://doi.org/10.3897/ved.177152)). An Italian preprint reports about 1.6 m/yr for timberline and 1.1 m/yr for the tree species line, 1954-2017 ([Carrieri 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.30.755293)). Verdict: delete the number, or replace with a defined range citing these studies, and state the definition used (forestline, timberline or tree species line; see [Körner & Hoch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.14593)).

**"Treeline advance was primarily temperature-driven in the 20th century."** Overstated for the Alps. Land abandonment dominated Swiss shifts: above 1650 m only 10% of new forest was true upward shift, 90% was ingrowth, and only 4% rose above the potential treeline ([Gehrig-Fasel 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1654-1103.2007.tb02571.x)). In the Eastern Alps, simulations show the current ecotone is mainly land-use constrained and forest would expand rapidly after abandonment regardless of warming ([Marzini 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10980-025-02070-8)). A counter-example attributes recent larch acceleration in the southern French Alps to climate, not land use ([Nicoud 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2024.178326)). Globally, treelines advanced at 52% of 166 sites, were stable at 47% and receded at 1% ([Harsch 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01355.x)). Verdict: cite both camps and add land-use history as a covariate throughout.

**"Drought is now limiting *P. cembra* growth at subalpine/treeline elevations."** Not supported by verified Alpine treeline evidence. Tyrolean work points to temperature and nutrients. [Oberhuber 2004](https://doi.org/10.1093/treephys/24.3.291) attributes long-term ring-width variation at Patscherkofel to July temperature, with post-1980s divergence tentatively linked to "extreme events or increasing drought stress". A Central Tyrolean treeline experiment found soil warming had no effect while nitrogen addition and understory removal raised growth, and 33% rain exclusion caused no soil-water shortage ([Gruber 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2018.00711)). Three years of rain exclusion did not reduce sap flow in *Picea* and *Larix* saplings at the treeline ([Wieser 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/f10090777)). Drought signals in *P. cembra* appear at the lowest stands of dry inner-Alpine sites ([Vittoz 2008](https://doi.org/10.1657/1523-0430(06-010)[vittoz]2.0.co;2)). Water limitation is documented at low-to-mid elevation in a dry inner-Alpine valley, with *P. cembra*'s temperature response at high elevation smaller than expected ([Obojes 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffgc.2024.1332941)). Verdict: reword to "test whether drought has become co-limiting at the upper treeline"; drought is a hypothesis here, not a premise.

**"Isolated open-ecotone trees are more drought-stressed than closed-stand trees."** No Alpine evidence found. The only facilitation citation is Tibetan (above); the Pyrenean krummholz study concerns *P. uncinata* seedlings ([Batllori 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1466-8238.2009.00464.x)). Gruber 2018 shows understory root competition reduces *P. cembra* growth, which cuts against a simple "neighbours buffer" assumption. Competition for water is plausibly higher in closed forest. Verdict: present as an open hypothesis with two-directional predictions.

**"Reduced snow cover drives frost/dehydration damage."** Supported only for saplings of other species. At 1700 m, snow-free saplings of five species showed more freeze-thaw cycles, higher spring hydraulic conductivity loss and more mortality; *P. cembra* was not tested ([Charra-Vaskou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70926)). The mechanism is winter dehydration and freeze-thaw embolism, a process separable from summer drought ([Mayr 2002](https://doi.org/10.1034/j.1399-3054.2002.1150108.x); [Mayr & Charra-Vaskou 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1399-3054.2007.00942.x)). Deep, long-lasting snow is itself harmful: snow-fungus mortality of *P. cembra* plantations at the treeline ([Barbeito 2013](https://doi.org/10.1657/1938-4246-45.4.455), abstract not read). Also, 1700 m is at or below the Tyrolean timberline (about 1900-2100 m), so it is not the ecotone. Verdict: keep as hypothesis; add the "more snow can hurt" counter-evidence.

**"The two stressors interact synergistically."** Untested. The only indirect support is sequential carry-over (spring hydraulic damage correlating with summer mortality) in saplings ([Charra-Vaskou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70926)). Verdict: replace with "may act sequentially" and drop "synergistic" and "compounding" from the title framing.

**"Simulation modelling demonstrates irreversible critical transitions at +2 °C" (Albrich).** Stretched. The study simulates one Eastern Alpine mountain forest landscape and finds a tipping point toward smaller, broadleaf-dominated forest above about +2 °C mean annual warming; topographic complexity buffers it ([Albrich 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.15118)). It concerns montane/subalpine composition, not treeline position, and the notes could not verify the study's elevation range. Verdict: reword as above, and do not use it to argue that the "window for treeline expansion is narrowing".

**"The classical assumption that treeline is uniformly temperature-limited (Körner 2012)."** Not verified; Körner discusses local modifiers. The Mongolian example (Klinge et al. 2018, *Biogeosciences* 15:1319-1333, taken from the proposal; not verified in the notes) is non-Alpine. Verdict: reword to "the globally dominant thermal limitation" and drop the Mongolian lower-treeline argument or move it to a footnote. Treeline temperature work worth citing: [Körner & Paulsen 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2699.2003.01043.x) and [Paulsen & Körner 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00035-014-0124-0).

**"No direct evidence on carbon vs growth limitation in conifers."** False for carbon. *Pinus cembra* at the Swiss treeline has mobile carbon pools that increase with altitude, indicating sink limitation ([Hoch 2002](https://doi.org/10.1034/j.1600-0706.2002.980301.x)), and non-structural carbohydrates were not depleted at any of 13 regions ([Hoch & Körner 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1466-8238.2011.00731.x)). The Dietrich & Zeidler 2026 DELLA viewpoint ([10.1111/nph.71385](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.71385), *New Phytol* 251:2337-2343) is correct that its hormonal mechanism has no direct conifer test yet. Verdict: restate as "carbon limitation is already rejected for *P. cembra* at the treeline; the hormonal alternative is untested". This also means the compound-stress argument cannot lean on carbon depletion, though an NSC effect after drought is untested rather than refuted.

**"Canopy facilitation drives seedling survival at alpine treelines" (Camarero 2024).** Tibetan; see table. Verdict: remove for Alpine claims.

**"Treeline at 1900-2400 m; closed forest at ~1800 m."** No peer-reviewed Tyrol-specific source found. The uppermost tree-size *P. cembra* in Eastern Tyrol reach about 2500 m ([Körner 2024, EGU abstract](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu24-20484)), but these are isolated individuals, not the forest line. A broader Alpine sample reports ecotones from 1100-2800 m ([Kruse, EGU abstract](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu24-10296)). Verdict: do not generalise; say which ecotone line is meant and source it from a Tyrolean inventory.

**"Data for Natura 2000 habitat reporting."** Habitat code 9420 is correct (Annex I, non-priority; [EUNIS](https://eunis.eea.europa.eu/habitats/205)), but it is a forest type, and the treeline ecotone partly lies outside it. A 3-site study cannot feed Article 17 reporting, which operates at national/biogeographic scale in 6-year cycles. Verdict: reword to "contributes structure/function information on pressures to habitat 9420 in Tyrol".

## 3. Critique of aims O1-O3 with suggested rewording

**None of O1-O3 is formally testable as written.** No hypotheses are stated, and the design is three transects with position, elevation (about 300-600 m apart) and likely aspect confounded. Position differences are inseparable from site, so site-level n is 1; trees are subsamples. The title phrase "under stress" and Paper 3's "stagnation under drought" presuppose both an outcome and a cause.

**O1 (photosynthetic stress across the open-closed gradient).** Three problems. First, the design confounds canopy buffering with snow redistribution, wind, soil depth, tree age and genotype. Second, SIF depends on canopy structure (LAI, shadow, snow/grass background), so sparse crowns and closed stands are not radiometrically comparable without structure normalisation; this is expert judgement, not a sourced finding. Third, "spring frost" appears in the aim but no leaf- or shoot-level frost damage measurement exists. Suggested rewording: "Test whether *Pinus cembra* at the Tyrolean treeline shows stronger physiological drought responses (predawn water potential, UAV-derived stress indices, ring width) in open than in closed positions, after controlling for elevation, aspect and canopy structure, across at least six paired sites." State the null and an a priori drought threshold (for example SPEI-3 below -1).

**O2 (snow deficit, soil frost and seedling recruitment).** The aim asks about recruitment, but no work package measures it: there is no seedling census, seed rain, cache or germination measurement. *P. cembra* is dispersed almost exclusively by spotted nutcrackers (caches about 5 km from harvest sites; [Waldwissen](https://waldwissen.net/en/forest-ecology/forest-fauna/birds/spotted-nutcracker-and-swiss-pine)), so recruitment is spatially patchy and cache sites may not match establishment microsites. GLORIA plots do not fill the gap (section 4). The aim also rests on a mechanism demonstrated in five other species. Suggested rewording: "Quantify the microsite conditions (snow-cover duration, soil freeze-thaw frequency, moisture) associated with *P. cembra* seedling and sapling presence and survival in permanent census plots across the ecotone." This needs a recruitment work package with permanent belt or 2 x 2 m plots, annual census and microsite classification. If that is not affordable, drop the word "recruitment".

**O3 (40-year treeline trajectory, deep learning crowns, SDM under CMIP6).** This aim bundles three incompatible scales. Landsat can only support forestline and densification trends; crown segmentation needs drone imagery; and a correlative SDM assumes equilibrium that treelines violate, so habitat suitability is a proxy for potential, not realised, treeline ([Elith 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2041-210x.2010.00036.x); Li et al. 2026, *J Environ Manage* 411:130173, taken from the proposal and not verified in the notes, whose own paper cautions that HSI is only a proxy). Suggested rewording: "Reconstruct 1984-present change in the upper forest line in Tyrol from Landsat and aerial orthophoto archives, and test its association with climate indices and land-use/browsing covariates before projecting climatic suitability under CMIP6 scenarios to 2050."

**Cross-cutting fixes.** Add formal hypotheses H1-H3 with nulls and pre-registration. Replace "dual-stressor regime" with "exposure to low snow and summer drought". Add a single sentence acknowledging land-use legacy (grazing, burning, browsing, avalanche) as a competing explanation. A defensible novelty claim: "To our knowledge, the first study to combine tree-level UAV-based physiological indices with juvenile dendrochronology and long-term satellite treeline records along a canopy-position gradient at the *P. cembra* treeline in Tyrol." Back it with a documented Scopus/Web of Science search; the notes' own search was too limited to establish "first", and Innsbruck's Patscherkofel group ([Gruber 2009](https://doi.org/10.1051/forest/2009038); [Wieser 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/treephys/tpv009)) has run integrated treeline work for decades.

## 4. Method feasibility per work package

**WP1, drone SIF and hyperspectral.** Not robust as a monthly monitoring design. SIF is a small far-red signal requiring sub-nm spectral resolution (roughly 0.3-1 nm FWHM); typical push-broom hyperspectral cameras (several nm) are inadequate, and the proposal names no instrument. This FWHM figure is expert judgement. Known technical issues include sensor etaloning and platform motion ([Bendig 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2019.2956194); note the journal is *IEEE TGRS*, not *Remote Sensing*), and steep terrain and wind worsen the latter. A review of non-imaging UAV-SIF retrieval is available ([Vargas 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12101624)). No UAV-SIF study on small conifers or in mountain terrain was found, and no Alpine treeline SIF study at all. PRI is a weak "stress" indicator in evergreen conifers because irradiance and downregulation dominate it ([Gamon & Bond 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2013.03.032)), so the PRI fallback is not a safe fallback. Flight count: snow often covers sites until May or June, so April-May flights see snow, not trees (expert judgement); a realistic number is **3-5 successful campaigns per year**, not seven. Redesign: use multispectral, red-edge and thermal UAV payloads as the primary indicators, with SIF as a small pilot or fixed-point field spectrometer; add predawn water potential and gas exchange as the ground truth that actually identifies drought. Add a permit work package (drone registration and insurance, Tiroler Naturschutzgesetz, landowner and Natura 2000 consent, wildlife disturbance) and two-person alpine safety protocols; the notes could not confirm protected-area drone bans, so check them.

**WP1, dendrochronology.** Feasible for adults (50 per transect), infeasible for the stated juvenile design. Naturally fallen or thinned trees under 10 cm DBH are rare at the treeline; 20-40-year stems have narrow rings, possible false or missing rings and short series, so COFECHA crossdating and century-scale pointer-year analysis cannot work on juveniles. Age-dependent responses in *L. decidua* and *P. cembra* are documented ([Carrer & Urbinati 2004](https://doi.org/10.1890/02-0478)). The EPS >= 0.85 threshold derives from [Wigley 1984](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0450(1984)023%3C0201:OTAVOC%3E2.0.CO;2). Redesign: use juveniles only for climate sensitivity over their short span, with size-class isolation as in the High Tatras spruce study (Märker et al. 2025, *Trees* 39:7, taken from the proposal; DOI not verified in the notes); use non-destructive cores from larger saplings; consider wood anatomy ([Lopez-Saez 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2022.158605)). Check sample size: that study needed at least 10 trees per size-class-year, with more than 500 recommended. Sacrificial sampling in Natura 2000 or protected areas needs permits.

**WP1, microclimate, phenocam and SMAP.** In-situ loggers and webcams are feasible; webcam snow cover and melt-out date are workable, snow depth only with calibrated markers ([Portenier 2020](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-14-1409-2020); [Filippa 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2016.01.006) for Alpine phenology processing). SMAP at 9 km is regional context at best, since a pixel spans huge elevation, aspect and snow variability; no Alpine SMAP freeze-thaw validation was found. Drop SMAP as a validation tool, or label it clearly as regional context. Use soil loggers at 5-10 cm, which also operationalises the freeze-thaw cycling metric from the Charra-Vaskou paper.

**WP2, Landsat forest-line mapping.** Landsat cannot measure treeline displacement. At 0.2-0.5 m/yr, 40 years gives 8-20 m, less than one 30 m pixel; even at 1.1-1.6 m/yr the shift (44-64 m) sits at the edge of detectability once geolocation error (about 10-15 m) and mixed pixels are included. Baglioni et al. never measure displacement: they detect Mann-Kendall greenness/wetness trend hotspots in buffers along a forestline taken from the 10 m Copernicus Tree Cover Density layer, with no independent accuracy assessment of the line and GAMs of trend against elevation not significant ([Baglioni 2025](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-22-4349-2025)). The line is also circular if reused for validation. Landsat 7 SLC-off handling is not discussed there and must be stated explicitly. Redesign: describe Landsat as "greening/densification trend detection near the forestline"; measure line displacement with historical orthophotos (1950s onward) and deep learning in the style of [Nguyen 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2022.113217); apply topographic correction and snow masking; add land-use and browsing layers. Multi-scale, multi-sensor design is the field's recommendation ([Garbarino 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/rse2.351)). The Tyrol-adjacent precedent that actually uses Landsat plus machine learning is [Menegaldo 2025](https://doi.org/10.3897/ved.177152).

**WP2, GLORIA as ground reference.** Misdescribed. GLORIA is a summit (alpine-nival) design recording vascular plant cover in 1 m2 quadrats, summit-area abundance, photos and soil temperature; the method page lists no tree density ([GLORIA](https://gloria.ac.at/methods/multi-summits)). The resurvey interval in Austria is not confirmed (the 5-year cycle was found only for another programme), and a "final GLORIA survey in Year 3" is outside project control. Reposition GLORIA as regional context; for tree validation use the Austrian Forest Inventory, Tyrolean aerial archives or own plots.

**WP2, Sentinel-2 anomaly detection.** At 10 m, a treeline tree island is a mixed pixel dominated by grass, rock and snow, so interannual phenology and snow-melt timing swamp the stress signal. Restrict to pixels of at least 50% tree cover (suggested threshold, not literature-derived), use red-edge indices with a modelled seasonal baseline, and treat 2018/2019/2022 impacts as hypotheses. No verified Austrian subalpine Sentinel-2 drought-anomaly paper exists to cite.

**WP2, Planet crown segmentation.** Infeasible for juveniles. Even 5 cm drone imagery detects only **52% of trees up to 130 cm** at Italian Alpine treelines (overall 67%; F1 0.76, falling to 0.68 under spatially independent validation) ([Carrieri 2025](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-22-6393-2025)). 3 m pixels cannot see crowns under 1 m, and the proposal's mitigation ("focus on juveniles of at least 0.5 m") contradicts itself. Species mapping with 0.31-1.24 m WorldView-3 reached only 44-47% accuracy for multi-class problems ([Sindewald 2025](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-22-6509-2025)), and the SAM paper has no treeline data and shows off-the-shelf SAM does not beat a trained Mask R-CNN ([Teng 2025](https://doi.org/10.52202/085713-0290)). Redesign: drone orthomosaics at 5 cm or better for crown work, Planet only as a stand-level context layer.

**WP2, SDM and projections.** Use observation-based snow inputs (SNOWGRID, [Olefs 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos11121330); SPARTACUS, [Hiebl & Frei 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00704-015-1411-4)) rather than 1 km CHELSA ([Karger 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2017.122)) for local snow and temperature, because topography creates 1-2 K local variation ([Scherrer & Körner 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2699.2010.02407.x); [Randin 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2486.2008.01766.x)). The EURO-CORDEX Alpine evaluation ([Frei 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-12-1-2018)) should accompany any use of its snow output. Restrict to 2050, report extrapolation with the area of applicability ([Meyer & Pebesma 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210x.13650)), and include seed-source distance, land-use legacy and microtopography as predictors. The transects supply no SDM presence data, so name the source (Austrian Forest Inventory, Tyrolean inventory). Dispersal and invasibility limit Alpine treeline expansion as much as temperature ([Dullinger 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0022-0477.2004.00872.x)).

## 5. Timeline and funding issues

**Calendar.** Today is 13 October 2026; "Year 1 (2026-2027)" is under way with no funding secured. Application, decision and contracting take months (an assumption; check the programme), so a realistic start is 2027 at the earliest. Relabel Years 1-3 relative to project start and update "1984-2025" to the last full year at analysis time.

**Drought years.** The proposal names 2017, 2019 and 2022. Documented Alpine drought events are **2003, 2015 and 2018**, with 2022 also widely reported; 2018 was climatically more extreme than 2003 and hit forests in Austria, Germany and Switzerland ([Schuldt 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.baae.2020.04.003); [NHESS drought impact inventory](https://nhess.copernicus.org/preprints/nhess-2021-24/nhess-2021-24-manuscript-version3.pdf), not read in full). The notes found no source for 2017 or 2019 as Tyrolean drought years, and 2018 is absent from the proposal. One 2025 multi-country analysis reportedly found the Alpine zone least affected, possibly due to altitude (summary only; unverified). Fix: select events a priori from site-specific SPEI or ERA5-Land soil moisture rather than naming years. Sentinel-2 starts 2017, so 2018 is available for satellite comparison but predates any project SIF data. Ring-width records can retrospectively cover 2003, 2015, 2018 and 2022 only if trees are old enough.

**Three field seasons and no guaranteed drought.** The design must work in a normal year. Frame the study as a monitoring baseline, not "drought response", unless an event occurs.

**Papers.** Five papers in three years is not realistic: Paper 1 (SIF) needs a first full season, Papers 4 and 5 depend on year-3 data and a validated SDM. Consolidate to three core papers (physiology plus dendrochronology; historical treeline from orthophotos and Landsat; scenario suitability) plus one data or methods paper, with Paper 4 optional. Neutralise titles: Paper 3 "Forty years of treeline ecotone change in the Tyrolean Alps from Landsat time series: patterns and climatic and land-use correlates"; Paper 5 "Climate- and snow-driven scenarios of *P. cembra* treeline suitability in the Eastern Alps".

**Funding.** FWF 1000 Ideas provides about €50,000-175,000 for 6-24 months, about 20 projects per round, PI salary capped at 50% and no doctoral positions ([FWF](https://fwf.ac.at/en/funding/portfolio/projects/1000-ideas); figures came via search summary, one aggregator said €150k, so confirm on the call text). It cannot fund this design. The realistic fit is the FWF Stand-Alone Project programme (programme page not re-fetched, so verify terms). "Forest4Climate" (Horizon Europe) and an "OeAD bilateral Alps cooperation" scheme could not be found and should be verified or deleted. "BiodivClima" is probably BiodivClim, a Biodiversa ERA-NET Cofund call that ran 2019-2024 and is closed ([TACR](https://tacr.gov.cz/en/biodivclim/)); check current Biodiversa+ calls. Euregio Science Fund and Interreg Alpine Space were not verified. Planet SuperDove access is commercial; check licensing and budget. No budget, staffing or site list is in the proposal, so feasibility was assessed qualitatively. Obtain letters of support from Land Tirol, landowners (ÖBf, Agrargemeinschaften) and the GLORIA coordinators; no data agreements exist in the proposal, and the roles of Land Tirol and Bundesforste were not verified.

## What this changes

The notes converge on a more modest but stronger project. The Tyrolean treeline literature does not support a drought-driven narrative at the upper margin: temperature and nutrients limit *P. cembra*, saplings showed no drought response in rain-exclusion experiments, and carbon limitation has already been tested and rejected for this species, so the compound-stressor premise must be recast as a testable hypothesis rather than the project's starting assumption. The real scientific gap is narrower and more defensible: whether drought has recently become co-limiting, how winter snow loss affects *P. cembra* (untested), and how both operate against a land-use legacy that still dominates where the Tyrolean ecotone sits.

Practically, the proposal should shrink its remote-sensing ambitions to what the pixel sizes allow (forestline trends from Landsat, line displacement from orthophotos, crowns from drones), grow its field replication (six paired sites or an honest case-study framing), add the missing recruitment measurement, and move to a funding scheme and calendar that exist. Resolving the Lévesque and Körner chapter-DOI discrepancies, and running a documented novelty search, are the two checks the notes could not complete.
