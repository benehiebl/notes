# Anchoring a Tyrolean forest-line proposal on airborne laser

**The best-supported design is a three-work-package project in which repeat airborne laser scanning (ALS), not WorldView stereo, carries the 3D change signal, and in which the third package attributes forest-line change to its drivers.** The documented gaps are real but narrower than the proposal brief assumes. No peer-reviewed Landsat forest-line change study exists for Austrian Tyrol (the nearest is one valley in South Tyrol, +32 m by Landsat versus +45 m by orthophoto), no satellite-stereo treeline volume study turned up in five targeted searches, and no study tests Sentinel-2 anomalies against tree rings at an Alpine treeline. Three feasibility limits shape everything else. Landsat's 30 m pixel is only marginally larger than the 8-64 m of displacement that 0.2-1.6 m/yr would produce in 40 years. Stereo DSMs (digital surface models) have per-pixel errors of about 1-4 m over mountain forest, which exceeds the sub-2 m sapling growth at the forest line. And Land Tirol already holds two statewide ALS epochs (first 2006-2010, second 2017-2023) that outperform stereo for canopy height change, while the WorldView archive available through ESA ends in December 2020. WP2 should be framed as a test of whether stressors leave detectable Sentinel-2 and ring-width signatures, with a null result pre-registered as an acceptable outcome, because the strongest Alpine evidence points to weak or absent browning at high elevation in 2018. WP3 should be a spatially explicit, uncertainty-aware driver attribution, with species-resolved ecotone change as the runner-up. Every number below is abstract- or summary-level; the notes read almost no publisher full texts, so "not found" means "not found in a limited search".

## What is verified, what is weakened, and what is only hypothesis

The notes tested six candidate gaps (G1-G6). The ranking below reflects the strength of the negative searches and what the nearest counter-evidence says; it is my synthesis of the notes' verdict table, not a literature-derived score.

The wording matters. Gap G2 must say "satellite stereo", not "VHR", because the French Alpine work already fuses very-high-resolution aerial imagery with Landsat. Claims of "first" need a documented Scopus or Web of Science search, which none of the notes performed.

On stressors, the picture is thinner than the brief's list suggests. Documented near the Tyrolean treeline are thermal and nutrient limitation, an unexplained post-mid-1980s break of the July-temperature link at Patscherkofel ([Oberhuber et al. 2008, Trees 22:31](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00468-007-0166-7)), winter frost-drought embolism in *Picea* but not in *P. cembra* at the same sites ([Mayr et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.011452)), snow-removal damage in saplings at 1700 m [[charra_2025_snow_treeline]], and rain-exclusion effects in larch and spruce at a subalpine treeline forest ([Oberleitner et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.14369)). Not documented for adult trees at the Tyrolean treeline are climate-driven browning or mortality in 2003, 2015, 2018 or 2022, frost rings, freeze-thaw embolism in *P. cembra* or *Larix*, Herpotrichia, and any interaction among stressors. The snow premise is itself unproven at treeline elevation. Station snow depth fell 8.4% per decade but the stations are scarce above 2000 m [[matiu_2021_snow_depth_trends]], and the Austrian 1 km model found a 42-day loss of snow cover duration that is significant only below about 2000 m [[olefs_2020_snow_depth_changes]]. "Reduced snow" at 1900-2300 m is an exposure assumption, not a measured trend.

## Landsat can show forest-line change, but only with a stated detection limit

WP1's Landsat component is defensible if it is framed as a forest-line product with an explicit minimum detectable shift of roughly one 30 m pixel plus geolocation error. At the published shift rates of 0.2-1.6 m/yr, 40 years yields 8-64 m, so only the upper half of that range is resolvable; this is arithmetic from the earlier project notes, not a measurement. The one empirical benchmark is Menegaldo et al., where Random Forest on Landsat 5 (1985) and Landsat 8 (2022) gave a **+32 m** mean advance against **+45 m** from manual orthophoto delineation in 15 of 16 advancing areas ([Menegaldo et al. 2025](https://ved.arphahub.com/article_preview.php?id=177152); [[menegaldo_2025_landsat_treeline_dynamics]]). The roughly 13 m Landsat underestimate is the bias to beat, with the caveat that it comes from one valley and that the reported 98% accuracy is the plugin's internal figure, not a line-position error.

Four sensor problems need to be designed in rather than discovered later. USGS Collection 2 gives Landsat 8/9 a geolocation error of about 17 m CE90, but the notes retrieved no figure for Landsat 4/5 TM in mountain terrain, and TM scenes form the baseline epoch ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/land-resources/nli/landsat/landsat-collection-2)). Harmonized Landsat Sentinel-2 (HLS v2.0) starts in April 2013 and has no Landsat 5 component, so the 1984-2012 TM/ETM+/OLI harmonisation must be built by the project; the notes mention Roy et al. 2016 transforms but did not check them [verify]. Landsat 7 scan-line gaps after 2003 forced the Swiss study to exclude that sensor. Topographic shadow produced the largest between-sensor differences (above 20% relative) in the HLS comparison, and no Alpine topographic-correction benchmark for TM was found. Because the Bayle artefact was shown on non-forest pixels above 2400 m, its size near the Tyrolean forest line at roughly 1900-2200 m is unquantified; the notes treat a smaller effect there as an inference. The safer design therefore classifies forest fraction per year and fits a time-series model robust to sampling density, instead of trending NDVImax. The notes found no treeline paper using LandTrendr, CCDC or BFAST, so applying them is methodology transfer, not precedent.

The definition also needs fixing. Körner and Hoch restrict "treeline" to thermally limited, globally comparable forest edges ([J. Biogeogr. 50:838](https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.14593)), and the Stubai iLand simulation places the current Tyrolean edge near 2000 m as mainly set by land use, with expansion after abandonment regardless of warming [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]]. The proposal should say "forest line" (the notes suggest closed canopy of at least 5 m height and 30% cover as an operational definition) and report sensitivity to the thresholds, since Nguyen's work shows boundary position depends heavily on them [[nguyen_2022_forest_mapping_explainable]]. Continental canopy products do not help at this scale: the 30 m annual European canopy height series has an RMSE of up to 4 m [[turubanove_2023_canopy_landsat]] and the 10 m GEDI-Sentinel-2 product has an aRMSE of 7.3 m for a single 2020 snapshot [[lang_2024_canopy_height]], both comparable to the entire krummholz-to-tree height range.

## Stereo volume change falls below its own noise; two ALS epochs fix this

The WorldView component is the weakest part of the brief. No peer-reviewed WorldView, Pléiades or SPOT stereo study of treeline canopy-height or volume change was found. The closest evidence is Pléiades canopy height models over mountain forest in Ticino and Slovenia, where the direction and angle of stereo geometry mattered and a 19 degree across-track angle degraded results ([Piermattei et al. 2018, Remote Sens. 10:1542](https://doi.org/10.3390/RS10101542)). One search summary quoted sigma-MAD values of about 0.8-3.8 m, but the same summary wrongly mentioned Salzburg, so those numbers need checking against the paper [verify]. The most pointed warning comes from a boreal-alpine ecotone in Norway: airborne laser had smaller residuals than aerial or UAV photogrammetry, photogrammetry smoothed small solitary trees, and the authors cautioned that small systematic differences could undermine monitoring of subtle, slow change ([Næsset et al. 2021, Remote Sens. 13:2469](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13132469)). A repeat-ALS treeline study in Norway measured 316 trees with mean growth of 0.19 m and could estimate height change only for domains of at least 1.5 ha ([Næsset et al. 2019, Remote Sens. 11:1804](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11151804)).

The detection arithmetic is the notes' own inference. A per-pixel level of detection is roughly 1.96 times the square root of the summed squared errors, so with about 1 m error per DSM the limit is about 2.8 m, far above sapling growth. Spatial averaging could rescue an aggregate signal only if errors are uncorrelated, but stereo errors depend on slope and geometry and are spatially correlated. WorldView DSM differencing can therefore plausibly quantify gap formation or loss (storm, bark beetle), growth of tall closed stands below the forest line, and mean height change of tall dense krummholz or alder stands, but not sapling establishment. A defensible detection limit needs an empirical stable-terrain error model (off-vegetation NMAD binned by slope and aspect) with autocorrelation-corrected uncertainty; the co-registration method of Nuth and Kääb ([Cryosphere 5:271](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-5-271-2011)) is the standard reference and the ALS terrain model can anchor the vertical axis. Volume here means canopy envelope volume (height integral), not stem volume or biomass, which would need allometry for *P. cembra*, *Larix*, *Picea*, *Pinus mugo* and *Alnus viridis* that no note reviewed.

The data situation reverses the logic of the design. Land Tirol's first statewide scan ran 2006-2010 and the second 2017-2023, the latter with at least 8 ground points per square metre; digital terrain and surface models are free at 50 cm, though they are mosaics of flights from different years, so per-tile flight dates are essential ([Land Tirol laser data](https://tirol.gv.at/sicherheit/geoinformation/geodaten-tiris/laserscandaten)). That gives a 3D change interval of roughly 7-17 years depending on tile, at accuracy far better than stereo. Whether the surface model (not just the terrain model) is available for the first epoch is not confirmed. The WorldView archive offered through ESA runs 2009-02-07 to 2020-12-01, so a WorldView record starting in 2005 is not supported by anything in the notes, and whether Tyrol footprints or stereo pairs exist has not been checked ([ESA](https://dragon3.esa.int/eogateway/catalog/worldview-esa-archive)). No current Austrian national WorldView or Pléiades licence was found either. The recommended restructuring is a tiered one: ALS epoch differencing as the 3D core, WorldView stereo as a feasibility and extension test with a published detection limit, and historical aerial stereo as the only route to pre-2006 3D, which is unverified for Tyrol. Land Tirol's orthophoto archive, a 20 cm product updated every three to four years with some historical imagery, is the likely validation source for the Landsat line, but its earliest years are unknown and should be requested ([Land Tirol orthophotos](https://tirol.gv.at/sicherheit/geoinformation/geodaten-tiris/orthofotos)).

## WP2 stressors: design for event contrasts, expect a possible null

Sentinel-2 has been validated for closed-canopy disturbance, not for sparse treeline canopies. The Austria-wide phenology and disturbance study detected 83.4% of about 1500 field disturbances, but only with two baseline years and no evaluation of Alpine snow or shadow [[löw_2020_phenology_austria_forest]]. The most relevant direct evidence is Swiss: in 2018 the August NDWI fell at least 10% in about 4.3% of forest, conifers were more damaged, fewer severe declines occurred at high elevation, and many alpine pixels showed NDWI increases ([Sturm et al. 2022, Glob Change Biol 28:2956](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.16136)). Producer's accuracy for damaged forest was only about 18%, and the composites were August only. A null Sentinel-2 anomaly at the Tyrolean forest line in 2018 or 2022 is therefore a plausible, informative result, not a failure. Pixel purity is a further problem: tree islands and krummholz are mixed with grass, rock and shadow at 10 m, and tree coordinates in steep terrain have errors of the same order as the pixel. The 50% tree-cover threshold in the draft proposal has no literature basis and needs sensitivity analysis. Larch is the best candidate for linkage because it is deciduous and gives a strong phenology amplitude, so any anomaly baseline must be stratified by larch fraction; no Sentinel-2 study separating *Larix* from evergreen conifers at the forest line was found.

The remote-sensing-to-ring link is the main methodological risk. A study of 180 spruce trees in six lowland plots found no significant correlation between NDVI and annual ring width ([D'Andrea et al. 2022, Forests 13:1417](https://doi.org/10.3390/f13091417)); the sensor was not confirmed as Sentinel-2, and no Sentinel-2 plus ring study at an Alpine treeline was found. With only the 2015-2025 overlap, about ten seasons, a per-site correlation needs |r| above about 0.63 to reach p < 0.05 with 8 degrees of freedom (the notes' arithmetic), so the design should use multi-site mixed models and event-year contrasts for 2018 and 2022 against reference years, with Landsat extending the baseline to 2003. Satellite data describe canopy exposure and phenology; rings record stem carbon allocation. The notes recommend 20-30 cored trees per site, with density-based proxies (blue intensity or maximum latewood density) because ring width diverges from temperature at some sites. No blue-intensity series of Tyrolean *P. cembra* was found, and the Patscherkofel divergence ends in 1999 data, so extending it through 2003, 2015, 2018 and 2022 is both feasible and novel. The existing Obergurgl and Stubai chronologies in the international tree-ring database all end before 1976, so fresh coring is mandatory. Snow melt-out dates from Sentinel-2 snow products ([Gascoin et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-11-493-2019)) could provide a new 10 m record, but ERA5-Land snow bias grows with elevation in the Alps and its raw use for treeline climate-growth work is not advised without checking against stations.

For compound stress the evidence is one sapling experiment: spring loss of conductivity predicted summer mortality, with adjusted efficiency 0.895 against 0.604 for a species-and-treatment-only model, in five species at 1700 m and without *P. cembra* [[charra_2025_snow_treeline]]. Interactions in adult trees are untested, so the lagged snow-by-summer-drought interaction tested on larch, where carry-over is hypothesised to be strongest, is a legitimate but exploratory hypothesis.

## WP3 should attribute drivers, with species-resolved change as runner-up

The notes scored seven candidates (my judgement, not literature-derived). Driver attribution (A) scored 20 of 25, species-resolved change (B) 19, cross-sensor harmonisation (F) 22 on raw convenience, topographic microclimate (D) and ecosystem consequences (E) 16 each, iLand projection (C) 12 and paleo context (G) 10. F ranks first by raw score but reads as WP1 methodology to a funding panel, so it should become a cross-cutting validation and uncertainty task. D, E and the volume-to-carbon translation fold into A as predictor engineering and a "so-what" deliverable. C needs process-modelling expertise the team lacks and is viable only as a hindcast subtask with collaborators. G adds no new gap for this team.

Attribution is the right WP3 because it closes the gap that the earlier reports left open and that no source resolves: whether land-use legacy, demographic lag and warming can be separated quantitatively. The underlying question is classic. Land use dominates Swiss ingrowth (Gehrig-Fasel 2007), a pan-European expert-based comparison finds temperature alone insufficient [[cudlin_2017_drivers_treeline_shift]], and Dischma advance is microsite and 1900 land-use controlled [[frei_2023_treeline_shift]]. The novelty therefore lies in a Tyrol-wide, multi-driver decomposition with propagated uncertainty and an explicit area of applicability, not in "land use matters more than climate". The wording must stay observational, as "attribution with bounds" using partial dependence, SHAP, spatial block cross-validation and counterfactual scenarios. The brief's contradiction to carry into the proposal text is that the land-use-dominant literature conflicts with Nicoud et al., who tie a French Alpine larch wave around 2003 to a climate window with 19th-century abandonment as precondition ([[nicoud_2025_landuse_larch_expansion]]; one region, 628 trees). Data are the bottleneck: Austria's ungulate browsing monitoring (WEM) exists at district and federal-state level, reporting strong game influence at 41.8% of Tyrol survey points in 2019-2021, but it samples forest regeneration areas rather than the ecotone and its point-level release is unknown ([waldwissen](https://www.waldwissen.net/de/waldwirtschaft/schadensmanagement/wildschaeden/wildeinflussmonitoring-2019-2021)). No Tyrol-wide alm-abandonment time series, avalanche-track layer or ownership dataset was found; these require Year 1 requests. Browsing evidence near treelines points in opposite directions, with sapling numbers rising despite browsing in the Swiss National Park ([Fluri et al. 2023](https://camargue.unibas.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/camargue/Fluri_et_al_2023_Forests.pdf)) and unpalatable spruce favoured in Paneveggio ([D'Aprile et al. 2020](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/handle/2318/1765505/689631/2020_daprileetal_Forests.pdf)).

The runner-up, species-resolved change, is hampered by time and labels. AlphaEarth and Tessera embeddings only exist from about 2017 [[brown_2025_alphaearth]], so a species-resolved forest-line displacement would span roughly 2017-2025 and any displacement would be below pixel size. Shifts in the species composition of the ecotone belt, say the share of larch versus *P. cembra* versus spruce per hectare cell, are more plausible than line movement, using Tyrolean S2 species work [[kollert_2021_tree_species]] and foundation-model species fractions [[ball_2026_foundation_models]]. Whether the BFW Austria-wide species map separates *P. cembra* at the forest line was not confirmed, so a "first species-level treeline map for Tyrol" claim is risky. The notes also warn of competing work from EPFL and WSL, an Italian UAV-seedling project (OLYMPUS), an FWF project on alder at the treeline (P34706) and the Innsbruck physiology group.

## Contradictions, [verify] items and corrections to earlier reports

Several source conflicts must be handled explicitly rather than smoothed over. Oberleitner et al. 2022 found drought reduced growth from the start in trees under rain exclusion (6 trees per species per treatment, 3 years), whereas Wieser et al. 2019 found three years of top-soil rain exclusion hardly changed sap flow in treeline saplings ([Wieser et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/f10090777)); the two differ in age class, soil layer and outcome variable, and neither should be cited alone. Obojes et al. attribute a weak forest-line temperature signal in *P. cembra* to a pasture-depressed forest line, but that interpretation is untested ([Obojes et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffgc.2024.1332941)). The Stubai simulation and the Swiss ingrowth data say land use sets the edge; the French larch wave says climate accelerated recruitment.

The notes also corrected earlier outputs. The earlier review called the study area of Marzini et al. 2025 unconfirmed; the wiki note shows it is the Stubai Valley, with the forest edge near 2000 m, simulated in iLand [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]]. The earlier reports said Carrieri 2025 detected 52% of trees up to 130 cm; the wiki note gives 70% for conifers above 50 cm (F1 0.76), 86% above 2 m and a leave-one-site-out recall of 0.56 [[carrieri_2025_hr_imagery_treeline]]. Both can be true for different height classes, so quote one threshold at a time (the WP2 note still repeats the 52% figure, so the notes themselves disagree). The earlier reports said Nguyen 2024 could not be matched externally; the wiki holds a full note ([[nguyen_2024_treeline_monitoring]]), and its DOI is unconfirmed [verify]. The Oberhuber attribution for the Patscherkofel divergence should cite both 2004 and 2008, because only the 2008 paper's abstract was read. Bayle et al. have a treeline follow-up, "Alpine greening deciphered by forest stand and structure dynamics in advancing treelines of the southwestern European Alps" (DOI 10.1002/rse2.430, journal assignment unconfirmed), which attributes greening hotspots mainly to forest dynamics, strongest where trees established in the mid-1980s [verify].

Items still marked [verify] include Piermattei's sigma-MAD figures, the Landsat 5 TM mountain geolocation error, the Roy et al. transforms, the HLS between-sensor DOI, the Loghin et al. WorldView-3 small-object numbers, Kupferschmid 2019, Merges 2020, Graf 2024, Wallentin 2008, Braunisch 2016, the BFW 2024 species-map DOI (MDPI Remote Sens. 16:2887), the Oberleitner site elevation, and the Märker 2025 sign convention for SPEI correlations. The ITRDB series AUST002 is labelled "PICE" in its header but was listed as *P. cembra* in a web summary; check the species before use. The Charra-Vaskou study appears as 2025 in the wiki filename but 2026 in the notes' text. "Larix decemlineata" matches no organism found (*Leptinotarsa decemlineata* is the Colorado potato beetle), so ask the user which agent was meant; the larch budmoth is the one biotic stressor with a century-scale ring signature, but no Tyrolean outbreak record for 2015-2025 was found and no satellite detection of it exists. The unsupported 0.4 m/yr motivation claim and the arbitrary 50% cover threshold should stay out of the proposal. Conference abstracts (EGU24-9480, EGU26-9249, IMC 2025) are context, not evidence.

## Conclusion

The proposal's weakest assumption is a technology one: that satellite stereo is the way to see treeline volume change. The notes show stereo noise exceeds the signal at the forest line, while Tyrol's own two ALS epochs sit unused in the same archive. Making ALS the core and stereo an openly bounded test turns a vulnerable aim into a defensible, measurable one, and it makes the Tyrolean laser record a novel result in itself, since no Eastern Alps repeat-ALS forest-line study was found.

A second shift is in how to treat negative results. Both the Landsat line (one pixel of detectable shift) and the Sentinel-2 stress layer (possibly no 2018 signal at altitude) have credible failure modes, so each should be written as a calibrated detection-limit statement with a pre-specified acceptable null. Combined with a driver-attribution WP3 that reports where its models extrapolate, the project can promise rigorous bounds instead of effects, which is the claim the evidence can actually support. First actions are data requests to Land Tirol (orthophoto and ALS dates, surface-model availability, licence), a catalogue query for WorldView stereo footprints in 2009-2020, and the Year 1 WEM and alm-cadastre requests.
