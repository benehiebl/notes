# Literature review v2: evergreen broad-leaved change in Italy

**Scope.** This report supports the paper idea in `03_papers/04_evergreen_distribution_change.md` ("Distribution changes of evergreen broad-leaved species in Italy": 40 years of annual 30 m Landsat leaf-type (LTC) and dominant-tree-species (DTS) cover maps, trajectory and hotspot analysis, drivers temperature / drought / land use, future prediction). It is a complete, standalone replacement of version 1. It rests on five round-2 researcher notes (ecology, remote sensing, trajectory and uncertainty, Italian context and drivers, and a check of the user's wiki PDFs) plus the version-1 inventory of the TRACEVE reference list (not redone). Round 2 read at least abstracts for most papers (v1 read almost none) and read the user's own PDFs for the wiki papers. The TRACEVE proposal is a proposal, not evidence.

**Access limits (honest).**
- Not reachable from the research machine: ScienceDirect, ResearchGate, Springer, Wiley, Science, OUP and the Cloudflare-protected institutional repositories (iris.unito.it, iris.univpm.it, iris.uniroma1.it). Springer abstracts are also elided in OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and CrossRef.
- OpenAlex hit its free daily budget (HTTP 429); Semantic Scholar was rate-limited after a few calls.
- Google Scholar worked only for a few batches (about 14 queries in one note, 3 in the remote-sensing note, 3 dieback queries in the Italy note), then returned 429 or a captcha. It returns titles and about 125-character snippets only. Italian-language and Italian-journal coverage (Forest@, iForest, L'Italia Forestale e Montana, Annali di Botanica) was therefore weak.
- No full text of any Springer or Wiley paper was read. Full texts were read only for the user's wiki PDFs, the open-access Berger (2008) dissertation, and the INFC chapter in the wiki book.
- During the synthesis, bibliographic metadata of most non-wiki papers cited here were re-checked against the CrossRef API ("CrossRef-checked"). This confirms authors, volume and pages, not content.

**Verification legend (use these tags exactly; do not read a weaker tag as a stronger one).**
- **[W]** = a note exists in the user's wiki (`01_notes/`). **[PDF-CHECKED]** = the round-2 wiki-PDF check read the user's PDF against the note.
- **[ABSTRACT-READ]** = abstract text fetched directly from an API or publisher/repository page.
- **[ABSTRACT-VIA-SEARCH]** / **[SEARCH-SUMMARY]** = only a search tool's paraphrase of a repository abstract (weaker; the two names are used by different researchers for the same status).
- **[ABSTRACT-PARAPHRASE]** = abstract only via a summarising fetch tool, not verbatim (weaker). **[SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]** = only a Scholar snippet or a machine-generated Semantic Scholar summary (weakest content tag).
- **[FULLTEXT-PARTLY-READ]** = some full text read. **[METADATA-ONLY]** = bibliographic record only, no abstract. **[still unverified]** = a content statement nobody has checked.
- "NOT IN WIKI - add PDF" = no note in `01_notes/`. Wiki papers carry a `[[citekey]]` link in addition to the full reference.

## What changed from version 1

**Promoted into the must-read list**
- Delzon et al. 2013: the abstract shows it is an inventory study (1880-2010) giving the only observed Q. ilex colonisation rate (22-57 m/yr maximum), not a genetic study.
- Padial-Iglesias et al. 2022 (new find): 30 m maps, three dates 1987-2002-2017, broadleaf-evergreen drivers analysed. Closest design twin for the ecology-plus-remote-sensing question.
- Hermosilla et al. 2024 (replaces Hermosilla 2018 as the design analogue): 37 species, 1984-2022, 86.1 % overall accuracy (OA).
- Thapa et al. 2020 (was a title-level extra): weak species-level but strong leaf-type back-casting results directly decide how far LTC vs DTS can be hindcast.
- Bogaert et al. 2022 (was an extra): the hidden Markov model (HMM) paper whose limits (300 m, about six classes) are now known.

**Demoted or moved out of the top 8**
- Garbarino et al. 2020: the 0.6 %/yr rate is for all forest, its reference base is unclear and the abstract was never read verbatim. Conditional read once the post-print is obtained (see "Still to verify via uibk institutional access").
- Pollastrini et al. 2019: still the best evergreen-vs-deciduous crown-loss paper, but the wiki already covers dieback with Seddaiu 2025, Puletti 2019 and Satta 2025. It becomes the first "short read".
- Atkins et al. 2018: abstract confirms a single watershed and winter NDVI / shrub cover, not a species map; the leaf-off principle is already in the wiki (Chastain 2007).
- Hermosilla et al. 2018: OA 70.3 % for one validation year only (2005), land-cover classes, not species.
- Olofsson et al. 2014: still the standard, but only a paraphrase of its abstract was obtained; moved to the short-read tier with Pontius & Lippitt 2006 (abstract read).

**Dropped or downgraded as sources for specific claims**
- Walther 2005 is not a Ticino paper (it is Ilex aquifolium in Scandinavia); Hickler 2012 does not report evergreen expansion or CO2; Zellweger 2020 is about understorey plants; Balzter 2000 is a stationary grassland Markov paper, not a forest/climate forecasting anchor; Colangelo 2018 is Q. cerris / Q. pubescens, not holm oak.

**Claims corrected**

Carnicer 2011 is southern European defoliation 1987-2007 with Iberian mortality. Peñuelas & Boada "three times" is isolated vs continental beech stands, not a time trend. Abercrombie & Friedl used MODIS 500 m; Bogaert 2022 used 300 m and about six classes. Bonannella 2022 covers 16 species in six 4-year windows. Francini 2024 is the Netherlands. Mombrini 2025 does not show SPEI steeper than SPI. Gasparini numbers replaced (forest 9,085,186 ha, +3.7 %; wooded land 11,054,458 ha, +5.6 %). Details in sections 1, 2 and 6.

- **Seven papers deserve a deep read; an eighth (Bogaert 2022) is a justified but weaker slot.** I would not pad beyond eight. A further six are "short reads" (abstract plus one section, under an hour each).

## 2) Where the literature stands per theme

### 2.1 Ecology and drivers of EVE spread

**What is established**
- The laurophyllisation narrative rests on a small Ticino / Insubria cluster, half of it grey literature: Gianoni, Carraro & Klötzli 1988 (ETH report; reference taken from the Berger 2008 dissertation, article not found online), Klötzli & Walther (eds.) 1999, Carraro et al. 1999 / 2001, Walther 2000 / 2002, Berger & Walther 2006, Berger et al. 2007, Grund et al. 2005, Conedera 2018.
- **Climate hypothesis**: milder winters relax the cold limit. Evidence: Walther et al. 2005 (holly, northern margin in Scandinavia; "a shift in the northern margin ... in concert with gradually increasing winter temperatures") [ABSTRACT-READ]; Walther et al. 2007 (*Trachycarpus*: spread into southern Swiss forests "driven by changes in winter temperature and growing season length and not by delayed population expansion") [ABSTRACT-READ]; Berger et al. 2007 (milder winters "consistent with the northward expansion of potential ranges"; bioclimatic models, i.e. potential not observed area) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Carraro et al. 1999 ("milder winters have a strong effect") [ABSTRACT-READ]. Walther 2002 is still [METADATA-ONLY].
- **Counter-evidence, from the wiki PDFs [W][PDF-CHECKED]**: Conedera 2018 (propagule pressure dominant, climate secondary, land-use change); Alessi 2019 (climate "secondary" to historical processes); Berger & Walther 2006 (22 sites, Lago d'Orta to Lago di Garda; distribution tracks precipitation and bedrock). **The literature disagrees on climate vs land use vs propagule pressure; Walther 2007's "not by delayed population expansion" is directly qualified by Conedera's propagule-pressure result.** The paper should state this disagreement, not choose a winner silently.
- **Q. ilex is a different EVE from the laurophylls**: in Insubria it sits on dry, shallow calcareous sites in the east (Berger & Walther 2006: it "profits on shallow calcareous soils from reduced competition from deciduous species") while laurophylls track moist, shaded sites [W][PDF-CHECKED]. One "EVE" class conflates these niches.
- **Non-equilibrium**: native laurophylls fill less than half of their potential range (Alessi 2019), so further spread is plausible. Field evidence for one species: *Laurus nobilis* in 16 central-Italian sites; three population structures (stable, dynamic, regressive), the first two most frequent, "suggesting a potential increase"; the authors name forest cover and age after rural abandonment as key factors, call the species "apparently late-successional" and frame expansion as recovery of a potential niche after land-use change, **not a climate-driven range shift** (Alessi et al. 2021) [ABSTRACT-READ].
- **Biome-shift precedents**: Peñuelas & Boada 2003 (Montseny); Peñuelas et al. 2007 (overlapping: holm oak recruitment "more than three times higher" than beech at the lower beech limit) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Cruz-Alonso et al. 2019 (Spanish old fields: recruitment highest for the shrub, then the evergreen oak; shrubs speed up *Q. ilex* colonisation) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Beffa et al. 2025 (Holocene, central Italy: *Fagus* declined and *Q. ilex* expanded after 8,200 cal yr BP; palaeo context only) [ABSTRACT-READ].
- **Observed rates (the only ones found)**: Delzon 2013 (*Q. ilex* 22-57 m/yr, France); Perring et al. 2020 (ivy occurrence +14 % per site on average, 1,814 resurveyed plots, 40 sites, median 38 years) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Kucharski et al. 2019 (fruiting ivy stands almost 10-fold since the mid-1970s, central Poland) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Abrahamczyk et al. 2024 (*P. laurocerasus* in 19 of 24 transects near Bonn, mostly established since 2000) [ABSTRACT-READ]. Neither is an EVE area-change rate.
- **Two-sided picture at the dry edge**: Garcia-Fayos et al. 2020 (aridity "drastically reduced" *Q. ilex* recruitment at the lower precipitation limit, 17 plots of 5.3 ha, Iberian System) [ABSTRACT-READ].

**What is weak**
- **No peer-reviewed spatial quantification of EVE or Q. ilex change in Italy was found** (see the limits in section 5). The only rate-like numbers for Italy are for all forest.
- CO2 is a plausible but **untested** driver: Niinemets, Flexas & Peñuelas 2011 (hypothesis review; "an additional explanation for the current apparent expansion of evergreen sclerophylls") and Soh et al. 2019 (evergreen water-use efficiency rose faster than deciduous over about 25 years of CO2 rise; [ABSTRACT-VIA-SEARCH], iWUE not range expansion). A correlative trajectory analysis cannot separate CO2 from warming.
- Hickler et al. 2012: the abstract reports 31-42 % of Europe projected into a different vegetation type by 2085 and widespread forest-to-shrubland shifts in southern Europe from drought; it does **not** state evergreen expansion or a CO2 result [ABSTRACT-READ, truncated].
- Zellweger 2020 concerns understorey plant thermophilization and microclimate (two Comments, two Responses; contested); "canopy buffers winter lows for EVEs" is your hypothesis, not its finding. Bertrand 2011 and Lenoir 2008 likewise concern herbs.

### 2.3 Time-series change, trajectories and uncertainty

- **Algorithms are built for spectral series, not categorical maps**: LandTrendr (Kennedy et al. 2010; [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]), CCDC (Zhu & Woodcock 2014; [ABSTRACT-PARAPHRASE]: change detection producer 98 %, user 86 %, temporal 80 % in one New England scene), BFAST (Verbesselt 2010; [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]). Applied to a per-pixel EVE-fraction series they inherit the map error (inference). Pasquarella et al. 2022 compares LandTrendr and CCDC specifications and outputs [ABSTRACT-READ]; Cohen et al. 2017 shows seven algorithms agree on undisturbed forest but "little agreement about which pixels were affected" [ABSTRACT-READ]; Forkel 2013 warns that breakpoint over-estimation can give "wrong or even opposite trend estimates" [ABSTRACT-READ]. **Plan a sensitivity test across algorithms.**
- **Temporal consistency of annual maps**: Hermosilla 2018 / 2024; Abercrombie & Friedl 2016 (HMM post-processing of MODIS 500 m labels); Bogaert 2022; Gong et al. 2017 (spatio-temporal HMM on SVM maps, no accuracy in the abstract) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Tsendbazar 2021 (annual-map validation, stability of accuracy) [ABSTRACT-READ]. Independent yearly classifications create flip-flops that inflate gross change; an HMM for **smoothing** is not an HMM for **forecasting**; keep them apart. Hauser et al. 2020 (mangroves) is a small tropical example of temporal gap-filling [ABSTRACT-READ].
- **Map error vs real change**: Pontius & Lippitt 2006 (77 % accuracy threshold in their case) and Olofsson 2014 (short reads). Intensity analysis (Aldwaik & Pontius 2012; [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]: tests stationarity at interval, category and transition level) fits the question "does evergreen gain target specific deciduous classes?".
- **Trend tests**: Yue et al. 2002 (serial correlation alters the Mann-Kendall variance; pre-whitening "leads to potentially inaccurate assessments") [ABSTRACT-READ]; Neeti & Eastman 2011 (contextual Mann-Kendall; mean annual NDVI 1982-2003; about 11 % of the area significant, 16 % with the contextual test) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Hamed & Rao 1998 [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]; Getis & Ord 1992 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Aubard 2019 is the only verified Mann-Kendall / contextual Mann-Kendall / Theil-Sen use on *Q. ilex*, and it is on a spectral index in stable cover. A dieback followed by resprouting is not monotonic.
- **Trajectory typologies with climate drivers**: Viana-Soto et al. 2020 (LandTrendr, K-means into four recovery trajectory classes, GWR; recovery "highly sensitive to post-fire climate", Spanish pine forests) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Giannetti 2020 (Mediterranean coppice clearcuts, GFC producer's accuracy 0.00-0.38) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Khoury & Coomes 2020 (drought-tolerant species show weak NDVI covariance with SPEI) [ABSTRACT-READ]; Thom & Seidl 2021 (BRT drivers of Alpine forest change; legacies and climate) [ABSTRACT-READ].
- **No observation-density paper for classified maps** was found; Bayle 2024 remains the main reference.
- **No verified paper applies Mann-Kendall / Theil-Sen / Getis-Ord hotspots to categorical annual species maps, and none uses an HMM with climate covariates for forest composition** (hotspot hits were low-quality regional land-use studies). This is a novel extension that the user must justify and hindcast-validate (section 5).

### 2.5 Space-for-time and prediction

- Blois 2013 (72 % as accurate; poor when temporal variation was small relative to spatial) and Damgaard 2019 ("problematic, especially in nonstationary environments") [ABSTRACT-READ]. Alessi 2019 already shows non-equilibrium, which weakens a space-for-time forecast.
- **Balzter 2000 is not the anchor v1 claimed**: it is a stationary Markov-chain assessment of 22 grassland applications (annual time step preferable; rank order of dominants the most reliable prediction) [ABSTRACT-READ]. McClintock et al. 2020 (HMMs for state dynamics in ecology, mostly animal movement) is the better generic reference [ABSTRACT-READ].
- Future suitability projections are mixed or negative for holm oak: [[noce_2023_altitude_shift_tree_italy]] [W][PDF-CHECKED] (MaxEnt, INFC2005 presences, n = 708 for *Q. ilex*, 2021-2050; holm-oak suitability change RCP 4.5 / 8.5: Northern-NW Apennines -58.9 / -29.4 %, Central Apennines -49.2 / -25.3 %, Southern Apennines -11.3 / -31.1 %, Central-Eastern Alps -38.6 / -1.8 %, Western Alps -29.4 / +29.2 % from a mean suitability of only 0.05); [[bricca_2026_topo_diversity]] [W] (diversity of warm broadleaved-evergreen forest habitats might fall by 2100 with summer drought, cold habitats gain as winter frost eases); Pignatti 2011 (old GCM; "more Mediterranean species on lower mountains, hills and lowlands") [ABSTRACT-READ].
- **Practical consequence**: use time-for-time modelling (fit on an early period, predict the later one as hindcast); present any space-for-time projection as a sensitivity run.

### 5.2 Methodological implications (evidence-backed)

- **Space-for-time substitution has limits**: about 72 % as accurate as time-for-time and poor when temporal variation is small relative to spatial (Blois 2013); problematic in non-stationary environments (Damgaard 2019); the laurophylls are out of equilibrium (Alessi 2019). Use time-for-time fits and hindcasts; show space-for-time as sensitivity only.
- **Label bias in plot data**: vegetation-database plots over-represent warm lowland sites with evergreen stands and can miss evergreen warm-temperate forests under random or systematic sampling (Alessi 2023 [[alessi_2023_sampling_approaches]]). Training and reference labels from such data bias class prevalence.
- **Leaf type (LTC) vs species (DTS)**: Thapa 2020 shows species-level calibration is poor to moderate (R2adj 0.39-0.78) while broadleaf / conifer grouping reaches 0.72-0.91; Hiebl 2025 shows EVE regression overestimates cover and fails out of distribution in conifer stands. Treat LTC as the primary hindcast target; report DTS trends only where early-year accuracy supports them.
- **Area-adjusted change estimation**: differencing annual maps is dominated by classification error unless corrected. Pontius & Lippitt 2006 give the test of whether map error alone could explain the change (in their case below 77 % accuracy); Olofsson 2014 gives the adjusted-area workflow; Hermosilla 2018 reached only 70.3 % OA for one year; reference labels for the 1980s are the bottleneck (INFC plots carry large standard errors by forest type). Plan the reference sample now, or limit claims to relative patterns.
- **Temporal consistency**: Bogaert 2022 and Abercrombie & Friedl 2016 show unfiltered annual series overestimate change, but both work at 300-500 m with few classes; Hermosilla-type rules may erase real slow change. Test with and without filtering.
- **Landsat consistency**: observation density, TM / ETM+ / OLI differences and label transfer into the 1980s all create spurious trends; Bayle 2024 shows this only for alpine non-forest summer NDVI, so transfer to a winter-dependent EVE class is an inference. Report observation counts per year and run a density-controlled subsampling experiment.
- **Mann-Kendall / hotspots and HMM-with-covariates have no precedent on categorical species maps**: no verified paper applies Mann-Kendall, Theil-Sen or Getis-Ord hotspots to categorical annual species maps, and none uses an HMM with climate covariates for forest composition. Aubard 2019 (Portuguese oak NDVI) and Viana-Soto 2020 (trajectory classes with GWR) are the nearest. This is a **novel extension** that must be justified, hindcast-validated, and kept separate from the HMM used for smoothing.
- **Heterogeneous target class**: sclerophylls (Q. ilex, Q. suber, Phillyrea), laurophylls (*Laurus*, *Ilex*, *Buxus*) and aliens (*Prunus laurocerasus*, *Trachycarpus*, *Ligustrum lucidum*) differ in drivers and spectral behaviour; Q. ilex and laurophylls occupy different niches. Stratify driver claims by group; expect understory spread to be underestimated.
- **Collinearity**: warming and abandonment both date from about 1950, so a correlation design needs explicit land-use covariates and spatial-autocorrelation-aware models (Dormann 2007; Beale 2010); correlation is not causation. Padial-Iglesias 2022 and Thom & Seidl 2021 are the only verified BRT driver analyses and neither states spatial cross-validation.
- **Other**: deciduous-oak drought stress (Colangelo 2018, Seddaiu on Basilicata) can raise the apparent evergreen share without evergreen spread; pixel mixing at 30 m; Italy-wide cross-checks should use the INFC holm-oak area and growing stock with the caveat that INFC samples are not independent.
