# Vihervaara et al. 2017 — Essential Biodiversity Variables and Remote Sensing for National Monitoring

## Title and Authors
**How Essential Biodiversity Variables and remote sensing can help national biodiversity monitoring**
Petteri Vihervaara, Ari-Pekka Auvinen, Laura Mononen et al. — *Global Ecology and Conservation*, 2017

## Quick Overview
- **Why is it relevant?** Provides the conceptual framework linking remote sensing capabilities to the internationally standardised EBV framework for biodiversity monitoring, relevant for understanding how RS-based forest mapping contributes to broader biodiversity assessment goals.
- **What was done?** Assessed how Finland's existing national biodiversity indicators correspond to GEO BON's 22 candidate EBVs, identified gaps, and systematically identified which EBVs could most benefit from available RS applications.
- **What is the main outcome?** Four EBVs (ecosystem extent/distribution, ecosystem structure, phenology, community composition) could substantially benefit from RS; three new EBV candidates are proposed to better capture ecosystem function via RS.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept
Essential Biodiversity Variables (EBVs) are a standardised set of variables proposed by GEO BON to harmonise biodiversity monitoring globally. The study tests whether Finland's national biodiversity monitoring scheme — one of the most advanced in Europe — covers EBVs adequately, and identifies where RS could fill monitoring gaps cost-effectively.

## Technical Approach
- **Scope:** Finland as case study (well-developed national biodiversity indicators)
- **Framework:** 44 national biodiversity state indicators vs. GEO BON 22 EBV candidates
- **Cross-tabulation:** Each indicator assigned to primary and secondary EBV subclasses; experts evaluate correspondence strength
- **Gap analysis:** Identifies EBV subclasses not covered by existing indicators
- **RS opportunity assessment:** Reviews available RS applications that could address identified gaps

## Distinctive Features
- National-scale EBV gap analysis applied to one of the most advanced biodiversity monitoring systems globally
- Explicitly connects RS capabilities to standardised international monitoring framework (GEO BON, CBD Aichi Targets)
- Proposes three new EBV candidates focused on ecosystem function measurable via RS
- Published as RS monitoring technology was rapidly advancing (Sentinel-2 just launched, UAVs emerging)

## Key Findings
- Finland's indicators cover many but not all EBV subclasses; gaps in community composition and ecosystem function
- **Four EBVs with strong RS potential:**
  1. Ecosystem extent and distribution (land cover change detection)
  2. Ecosystem structure (vegetation height, canopy cover from LiDAR/RS)
  3. Phenology (time series vegetation indices)
  4. Community composition (species mapping where resolution allows)
- Shore habitats and water quality monitoring as specific Finnish RS opportunities
- New EBV candidates proposed: ecosystem functional types, primary productivity, spectral diversity (related to species diversity via SVH)

## Advantages and Limitations
- **Advantages:** Practical policy relevance; bridges monitoring theory and RS practice; Finland data high quality
- **Limitations:** Finland-specific; RS technology has advanced substantially since 2017; EBV framework has evolved (now ~30 EBVs)

## Conclusion
RS can substantially improve national biodiversity monitoring by filling gaps in EBVs related to ecosystem structure, extent, phenology, and community composition. This framework provides a rationale for investing in RS-based forest monitoring as a contribution to international biodiversity commitments. The spectral diversity/SVH link to community composition EBV is directly relevant to the wiki's research context.
