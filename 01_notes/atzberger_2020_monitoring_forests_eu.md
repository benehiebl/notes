# Atzberger et al. 2020 — Monitoring of Forests through Remote Sensing (EU Final Report)

## Title and Authors
**Monitoring of Forests through Remote Sensing — Final Report**
Clement Atzberger, Gunter Zeug, Pierre Defourny, Luiz Aragão, Lars Hammarström, Markus Immitzer — European Commission, ENV.D.1/ETU/2018/0022MV, October 2020

## Quick Overview
- **Why is it relevant?** Comprehensive EU-commissioned review of RS methods for forest monitoring across all major domains (phenology, illegal logging, pests/diseases, fire, biodiversity, carbon stock), providing a policy-relevant synthesis of the entire field.
- **What was done?** Stocktaking and review of existing RS evidence for forest monitoring across 6 thematic domains, synthesizing current capabilities, limitations, and recommendations for EU-level forest monitoring systems.
- **What is the main outcome?** RS can contribute substantially to all six forest monitoring themes; phenology and ecosystem structure monitoring are most mature; pest/disease early detection and biodiversity monitoring have the largest gaps; multi-sensor time series approaches are recommended across all themes.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept
The EU needs consistent forest monitoring data to support biodiversity (Natura 2000, Habitats Directive), climate (LULUCF carbon accounting), environmental (Forest Europe), and policy (Green Deal) reporting. This report evaluates how RS technologies can support or replace field-based monitoring, identifying the state-of-the-art and critical gaps per theme.

## Technical Approach
Systematic review structured around 6 thematic domains:
1. **Phenology** — optical time series, vegetation indices, curve fitting methods
2. **Illegal logging** — land use change detection, SAR, radar backscatter change
3. **Pests and diseases** — bark beetle, Phytophthora, defoliation monitoring via RS
4. **Fire** — burned area mapping, post-fire assessment
5. **Biodiversity** — species diversity estimation, forest type mapping, structural diversity
6. **Forest structure/carbon** — canopy height, biomass, AGB from SAR, LiDAR, optical

Each theme reviewed for: in-situ methods, RS approaches, validation, operational readiness

## Distinctive Features
- EU policy context: directly informs European Forest Monitoring systems (Copernicus Land Service, JRC)
- Multi-thematic coverage enables cross-domain synthesis
- Evaluates current operational products (CORINE, Copernicus Forest Cover) and their gaps
- Provides tiered recommendations: immediately applicable, near-term feasible, research needed

## Key Findings
- **Phenology:** Landsat/MODIS/Sentinel-2 time series are mature and operational; main challenge is data density in cloudy regions
- **Illegal logging:** SAR (Sentinel-1) provides all-weather detection; Landsat tracks slow-onset change
- **Pests/diseases:** Currently detectable only post-severe-damage; early detection remains a key gap
- **Biodiversity:** Forest type mapping advancing rapidly; species-level diversity monitoring still immature
- **Carbon/structure:** LiDAR (GEDI + ALS) combined with Sentinel-1 is the most promising near-term approach for AGB
- **Cross-cutting:** Dense multi-sensor time series (S1+S2+Landsat) needed; cloud computing platforms (GEE) enable operational scale

## Advantages and Limitations
- **Advantages:** Authoritative EU policy document; comprehensive multi-theme scope; practical policy recommendations
- **Limitations:** Rapid technology evolution means parts are already partially dated; focuses on European temperate forests; not peer-reviewed journal publication

## Conclusion
RS can substantially improve EU forest monitoring across all six themes. Phenology and forest structure/carbon are most mature. Pest/disease early warning and species-level biodiversity monitoring remain the largest gaps requiring targeted research. The combination of Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, and Landsat time series on cloud platforms is the recommended operational approach.
