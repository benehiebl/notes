## Title and Authors of the Paper

- "Vulnerabilità delle foreste mediterranee a eventi climatici estremi di ondate di calore e siccità: il caso del disseccamento del leccio (*Quercus ilex* L.) in Italia" (Italian original, English title and abstract provided)
- Seddaiu S, Ruiu PA, Piras G, Scanu B, Satta GGA, Lentini A, La Mela Veca DS, Sidoti A, Castellaneta M, Colangelo M, Moretti N, Ripullone F, Tarasco E, Gravano E, Mocci EM, Delle Donne AG, Iacopetti G, Bettini D, Bussotti F, Pollastrini M
- *Forest@* 22: 56–65 (2025); doi: 10.3832/efor4934-022 (online 2025-10-14). Article type: "Articoli Scientifici", but in practice a regional overview/perspective, not an experiment.

## Quick Overview

- **Why is it relevant?** Holm oak is the key evergreen broad-leaved species of Mediterranean Italy, and this paper documents that its nominal drought tolerance failed in 2017 and 2024, which directly matters for any narrative of evergreen expansion.
- **What was done?** The authors compile regional field observations (Basilicata, Puglia, Sardinia, Sicily, Tuscany), ICP Forests defoliation data for *Q. ilex* (2001–2024) and 33 citizen reports from the new SilvaCuore web app into an overview of the 2024 dieback and its causes.
- **What is the main outcome?** Dieback is widespread, linked to hot droughts (2017, 2022, 2024) and often co-occurring with *Phytophthora* and other opportunistic pathogens, but extent, severity and causal weights remain poorly quantified, so the authors call for a coordinated monitoring network.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept

- Goal: document and analyse the most relevant *Q. ilex* dieback cases in central and southern Italy and define research and monitoring priorities.
- Concept: dieback is a complex (oak-decline) syndrome in which hot droughts act as predisposing/triggering factor and biotic agents (*Phytophthora* spp., Botryosphaeriaceae, *Biscogniauxia mediterranea*, xylophagous insects) contribute, with local site conditions (soil depth, substrate, exposure) causing patchy patterns.
- Sclerophylly is not a specific drought adaptation; high vapour pressure deficit (VPD) causes xylem cavitation that is hard to repair (the authors cite Salleo et al. 1997 and Bussotti & Pollastrini 2020; neither is in the wiki, so needs verification).

## Technical Approach

- **Narrative synthesis** of literature plus regional expert observations; no systematic search protocol.
- **ICP Forests Level I / CON.ECO.FOR.**: ~250 *Q. ilex* trees in 11 permanent plots (of 261 national plots); mean defoliation and the share of dead or extremely defoliated (>85 %) trees, 2001–2024; trend test Mann–Kendall (p < 0.05).
- **Regional reports** with symptoms (leaf browning, shoot and branch desiccation, epicormic shoots, root necrosis), pathogen findings and, for Sardinia and Basilicata, satellite-based estimates (EVI, NDVI, NGRDI change versus the previous year).
- **SilvaCuore**: web application with GPS-tagged, photo-documented citizen reports stored in Firebase and validated remotely by the team, with field checks for important cases.

## Distinctive Features

- Covers the 2024 event across five regions in one document and ties it back to the 2012 and 2017 events in Tuscany.
- Introduces SilvaCuore as a citizen-science tool for forest health in Italy.
- Explicitly points to the representation gap of Mediterranean evergreen forests in ICP Forests (about 10 % of the forest area, 11 of 261 plots).

## Experimental Setup and Results

- **ICP Forests**: mean defoliation of *Q. ilex* decreased 2001–2016 and increased afterwards with peaks in the hot-dry years 2017, 2022 and 2025 (significant trends, Mann–Kendall); mortality is sporadic, but including trees with >85 % defoliation raises the share in the latest year; the authors stress that resprouting makes true death hard to diagnose.
- **2017 reference**: for central-southern Italy no rain for >3 months and temperatures up to 8 °C above average; in Basilicata ~50,000 of 350,000 ha of forest were in high stress state by satellite analysis, particularly deciduous species.
- **Sardinia 2024**: change in EVI/NDVI/NGRDI suggests ~110,000 ha affected; strong heterogeneity even under equal climate; ~25 % holm oak mortality reported in already declining areas; partial recovery by new shoots after the August–September rains.
- **Sicily 2024**: damage mainly on volcanic, water-poor soils (Etna, Iblei); local dieback incidence of 40–60 % in some stands; preliminary survey found no pathogens/insects, so a hydric-thermal cause is suspected.
- **Tuscany**: coastal and sea-facing slopes over ~130 km hit in 2017; shoots resprouted in 2018–2019 later desiccated and died mostly in 2021–2022; association with *B. mediterranea*; structural conversion from high forest to shrub-like forms.
- **Puglia/Basilicata**: documented Botryosphaeriaceae and *Phytophthora* findings, summarised from other studies.
- **SilvaCuore**: 33 holm oak reports from five regions so far.

## Advantages and Limitations

- **Advantages**: broad regional coverage, multiple evidence lines (field, ICP Forests, satellite, citizen reports), clear research agenda; valuable Italian-language context and species coverage (cork oak, shrubs).
- **Limitations** (critical reading):
  - Observations are explicitly empirical and non-systematic; no common protocol or sampling frame across regions, so extent and severity numbers (110,000 ha, 25 %, 40–60 %) are not comparable and not validated here.
  - ICP Forests subset is tiny (11 plots, ~250 trees) and the authors themselves consider it unrepresentative; the Mann–Kendall trend is for a handful of plots.
  - Causal attribution (abiotic vs *Phytophthora*) is unresolved and region-specific (e.g. Sicily "no pathogens" vs Puglia/Tuscany pathogens); other sources report that most oak-decline studies attribute decline to pathogens ([[seddaiu_2025_mediterranean_dieback]] citing Marques et al. 2025).
  - The report on the Tuscany chronology contains an apparent inconsistency (events "from summer 2022" subsequently "extended during summer 2017"); the ICP Forests series is said to peak in 2025 although the article reports data until 2024. Check against the original before citing exact years.
  - SilvaCuore reports are self-selected, so they carry spatial reporting bias and cannot be used as unbiased ground truth.
  - Includes 2024 events only preliminarily; recovery after later rains is documented only anecdotally.

## Conclusion

- Hot-drought-driven dieback of holm oak is a real, recurrent and large-scale phenomenon in Italy since at least 2017, with strong site-dependence and probable biotic interaction.
- It is a good citable overview for context and for the monitoring gap, but not a quantitative source; extent figures should be cited as regional estimates from other studies.
- For this wiki it challenges the simple "evergreen expansion" picture in [[evergreen_broadleaved_expansion]]: the main evergreen tree of the Mediterranean is also a casualty of the same climate trend.
