## 1. Title and Authors

**Temporal increase in the extent of Sardinian pine formations**
Calvia et al. (2022), *Rendiconti Lincei. Scienze Fisiche e Naturali* 33:489–499.

## 2. Quick Overview

- **Why is it relevant?** Provides baseline distribution data and recovery trends for native Mediterranean pine formations in Sardinia — directly relevant to Italian forest mapping and EVE/pine expansion narratives.
- **What was done?** Diachronic aerial photo analysis (1954, 1977, 1998, 2019) combined with field surveys, phyto-toponym mapping, and local interviews to quantify changes in native Sardinian pine formation extent.
- **What is the main outcome?** All three native pine species expanded substantially since 1954, with *P. pinaster* up +1043%, *P. halepensis* +235%, and *P. pinea* +27%, driven by post-abandonment natural regeneration.

## 3. Main Goal and Fundamental Concept

The study aims to document the present distribution and quantify the temporal trajectory of native *Pinus halepensis*, *P. pinaster*, and *P. pinea* formations in Sardinia. The core hypothesis is that historical overexploitation and fire suppression reduced these forests below their ecological potential, and that subsequent land abandonment has allowed substantial natural recovery — a recovery not yet quantified or mapped systematically.

## 4. Technical Approach

- **Current distribution mapping**: 60 field excursions (2017–2019) with GPS-delimited perimeters; canopy cover categorised as <1%, 1–35%, >35%
- **Diachronic analysis**: Manual interpretation of georeferenced aerial photographs from 1954, 1977, 1998, 2019 (Sardegna Geoportale); formation extents calculated for each time step
- **Phyto-toponyms**: Sardinian dictionaries + IGMI 1:25,000 maps + Geoportale to identify pine-related place names as proxies for historical distribution
- **Interviews**: 30 people (foresters, shepherds, guides) aged 40–90, queried on local pine history
- All mapping done in QGIS

## 5. Distinctive Features

- **Multi-source triangulation**: Aerial photos + toponymy + oral history + field surveys — none used in isolation
- **Phyto-toponym analysis**: Unusual methodology for reconstructing pre-photographic distribution; 36 toponyms identified, mostly now geographically displaced from extant formations, implying historical range loss
- **Habitat Directive integration**: Results explicitly mapped to EU habitats 2270* (dune pines) and 9540 (Mediterranean pine forests); 1100 ha in 9540, 128 ha in 2270* priority habitat

## 6. Experimental Setup and Results

| Species | 1954 extent (ha) | 2019 extent (ha) | Change |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|--------|
| *P. halepensis* | 222 | 770 | +235% |
| *P. pinaster* | 39.5 | 454 | +1043% |
| *P. pinea* | 43 | 58 | +27% |

- ~90% of formations fall within protected areas (SACs, Natura 2000)
- *P. halepensis*: SW Sardinia, 4864 ha total (all cover classes); almost entirely within SACs
- *P. pinaster*: NE Sardinia, 5907 ha total; ~20% outside any protected area
- *P. pinea*: SW Sardinia, 582 ha total; only 33% within protected areas despite all natural forests being within SAC ITB042247

## 7. Advantages and Limitations

**Strengths**
- First systematic, island-wide distribution map for three native Sardinian pine species
- Long temporal window (65 years across 4 time steps)
- Conservation-relevant framing (Habitat Directive, SAC coverage)

**Critical Limitations**
- **Low initial extents inflate percentages**: *P. pinaster* started at 39.5 ha, making +1043% impressive in relative terms but modest in absolute terms (454 ha)
- **No remote sensing classification**: Purely manual aerial photo interpretation — no quantified accuracy assessment, no error bounds on extent estimates
- **Canopy cover threshold (>35%)** is operationally defined without ecological justification
- **No climate attribution**: Recovery attributed qualitatively to land abandonment and fire suppression; no quantitative analysis of climate drivers
- **Temporal resolution coarse**: Only 4 snapshots over 65 years; no annual trend analysis
- **Toponym attribution is uncertain**: Dialect terms conflated with personal names; subjective filtering noted
- **Not transferable at scale**: Methods are time-intensive and non-replicable with automated satellite data

## 8. Conclusion

Calvia et al. (2022) provide useful baseline distribution maps and trend data for three native Sardinian pine species, demonstrating that land abandonment and fire protection drove substantial natural forest recovery since the mid-20th century. The paper is most valuable as a floristic reference and conservation audit for Sardinia, and as a diachronic use case for vegetation change detection via aerial photography. However, the lack of statistical uncertainty, the methodological reliance on manual interpretation, and the limited absolute extents involved constrain the paper's generalisability. For the wiki's purposes, the main utility is contextual: Sardinian pine forests as a distinct category from EVE species, and the general principle that Mediterranean forests are expanding under land abandonment.
