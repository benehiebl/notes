## Quick Overview

- **Why is it relevant?** Italy's third national forest inventory provides the most comprehensive, statistically rigorous dataset on the extent, structure, composition, and carbon content of Italian forests, serving as ground truth for remote sensing studies and as the national reference for forest policy and international reporting.
- **What was done?** A three-phase national sampling survey was conducted (field data collected 2017–2020, reference year 2015), measuring >50 qualitative and quantitative forest variables across all 21 Italian regions.
- **What is the main outcome?** Italian forests cover 10,980,000 hectares (~37% of national territory), an increase of ~520,000 ha compared to the INFC2005 inventory, reflecting continued forest expansion into mountain and hilly areas.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept

The Italian National Forest Inventory (INFC) is a permanent sample survey that provides national forest statistics for Italy. Its objectives span three domains:
1. **Productive function**: growing stock volume, biomass, annual increment, wood availability
2. **Carbon accounting**: aboveground and belowground biomass, deadwood, litter, and soil carbon pools (for UNFCCC/Kyoto Protocol reporting)
3. **Ecosystem characterisation**: biodiversity, forest health, structure, management, protective functions, ownership

INFC2015 is the third Italian NFI. The reference year is 2015 (to allow comparison with INFC2005), though field surveys were conducted November 2017 – early 2020. Processing and final statistics were produced in 2021.

## Survey History

| Inventory | Period | Key Features |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| IFNI85 | 1983–1987 | First Italian NFI; 3 km × 3 km grid; single field phase; sampling design and definitions not fully comparable with later surveys |
| INFC2005 | 2002–2007 | Second NFI; introduced 1 km × 1 km grid and three-phase design; adopted FAO-UNECE forest definition; expanded carbon pool measurements |
| INFC2015 | 2013–2020 | Third NFI; same methodology as INFC2005 (enabling temporal comparison); management transferred from State Forestry Corps to Carabinieri (CUFA) |

## Sampling Design

Three-phase design on a systematic 1 km × 1 km national sampling grid:
- **Phase 1 (photointerpretation)**: All grid points classified by land use and land cover from aerial/satellite imagery — determines which points fall within the inventory domain (Forest or Other Wooded Land)
- **Phase 2 (second photointerpretation)**: Subset of forest/OWL points further classified for forest type, canopy cover, and forest category
- **Phase 3 (field measurement)**: Subset visited on the ground; circular sample plots (AdS) of varying radii (AdS2, AdS4, AdS13, AdS25 — radii in meters) for different tree size classes; all trees and shrubs above threshold diameters measured

Sampling intensity was stratified by region to ensure reliable estimates at both national and regional scales. Results are always reported by region and for Italy as a whole.

## Variables Measured (>50 total)

**Qualitative variables:**
- Forest type, silvicultural system, stand origin (natural vs planted)
- Development stage, ownership, protection constraints
- Management methods, forest health indicators, terrain instability

**Quantitative variables:**
- Tree count, diameter at breast height (DBH = d₁.₃₀), height
- Growing stock volume (total and by species, diameter class, forest type)
- Annual increment in volume and biomass
- Deadwood volume and biomass (standing dead trees, lying deadwood, stumps)
- Aboveground biomass and carbon content
- Forest soil carbon

## Key Results (INFC2015)

- **Total forest area**: 10,980,000 ha (~37% of Italian territory)
- **Change from INFC2005**: +~520,000 ha; forests continue expanding into mountain and hilly areas as agricultural land is abandoned
- Full statistics by forest type, region, species, management, and carbon pool available at www.inventarioforestale.org

## International Reporting Uses

INFC data feeds into:
- **SoEF** (State of Europe's Forests, Forest Europe)
- **FAO Global Forest Resources Assessment** (FRA)
- **UNFCCC/Kyoto Protocol** national greenhouse gas inventories (carbon pools)
- Italian national reports on forests, natural capital, and biodiversity (CBD)
- SDG monitoring (Goal 15)

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages:**
- Only comprehensive, statistically rigorous national-scale dataset for Italian forests
- Three-phase design allows efficient estimation across large areas
- Consistent methodology with INFC2005 enables 10-year temporal change analysis
- Data fully open access; all estimates publicly available by region
- Covers qualitative and quantitative variables far beyond wood volume

**Limitations:**
- Field data collected over 2017–2020 but reference year is 2015 — temporal mismatch
- 1 km × 1 km sampling grid means fine-scale spatial predictions require upscaling (e.g., via remote sensing wall-to-wall mapping)
- Plot-level data are anonymised/not fully georeferenced for public use — limits spatial modelling applications
- NFI cycle length (nominally ≥5 years) means change data lag actual forest dynamics

## Conclusion

Gasparini et al. (2022) present the complete methods and results of Italy's third national forest inventory, which documents 10.98 million hectares of Italian forest and provides the nation's primary reference dataset for forest area, growing stock, biomass, carbon stocks, biodiversity, and forest health. For remote sensing research in Italy, INFC2015 is the definitive ground reference for forest attributes at regional to national scale, and its forest type classification (broadleaved deciduous, broadleaved evergreen, Mediterranean coniferous, temperate coniferous) aligns with the habitat types used in related studies such as Bricca et al. (2026) and Amico et al. (2025).
