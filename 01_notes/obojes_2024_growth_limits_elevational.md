## Title and Authors of the Paper

- "Tree growth at the limits: the response of multiple conifers to opposing climatic constraints along an elevational gradient in the Alps"
- Nikolaus Obojes, Serena Buscarini, Armin Konrad Meurer, Erich Tasser, Walter Oberhuber, Stefan Mayr, Ulrike Tappeiner
- *Frontiers in Forests and Global Change* 7: 1332941 (2024); doi: 10.3389/ffgc.2024.1332941

## Quick Overview

- **Why is it relevant?** Warming is expected to increase growth near the forest line but to reduce it where water is limited; this study compares five conifers along one gradient to see where each constraint dominates.
- **What was done?** Tree-ring widths and basal area increment of *Picea abies*, *Larix decidua*, *Pinus sylvestris*, *Pinus nigra* and *Pinus cembra* were analysed at 24 sites between 1000 and 2320 m a.s.l. in the Vinschgau/Val Venosta valley, one of the driest regions of the Alps (mean annual precipitation 682 mm at 1310 m a.s.l.).
- **What is the main outcome?** Water availability limited growth of all species at low elevation (including the previous autumn), spruce was most drought-sensitive at mid elevation, and near the forest line temperature responses were weaker than expected for *P. cembra*, whereas larch responded positively to temperature and grew faster in recent decades.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept

- Goal: estimate species-specific growth responses to climate along an elevational gradient and use them as a space-for-time analogue for future conditions.
- Hypotheses: water availability limits growth of all conifers at low elevation; temperature limits growth near the forest line; anisohydric larch should differ from isohydric spruce and pines.

## Technical Approach

- **Sites**: 24 sites (three to nine per species) from 1000 to 2320 m a.s.l., north and south slopes, sampling 10–16 trees per site (cores sampled in 2014 and 2017); elevation belts low <1200 m, mid 1200–1800 m, high 1800–2200 m, forest line >2200 m.
- **Forest history context**: many low and mid-elevation stands were planted between the 1890s and 1960s on formerly pastured slopes for erosion control (so tree ages are similar), black pine was introduced 50–130 years ago.
- **Data**: ring width (total, earlywood, latewood) converted to basal area increment; climate from nearby station (temperature, precipitation) and SPEI at 1, 3 and 12 months.
- **Analysis**: climate–growth correlations and response functions (seasonal windows from previous spring to current summer), principal component gradient analysis (PCGA) of chronologies, comparison across species and elevations.

## Distinctive Features

- Five conifers sampled over the same, very dry gradient, which allows species comparisons.
- Considers carry-over effects (previous autumn and winter water supply).
- Explicit use as space-for-time analogue for future conditions of larger Alpine regions.

## Experimental Setup and Results

- At low elevation all species responded significantly to water availability, including previous autumn precipitation; differences between anisohydric larch and isohydric spruce/pines were surprisingly small.
- At mid elevation, correlations with precipitation and SPEI were highest for *P. abies*, which is the species assumed to be most drought-sensitive.
- At high elevation the positive temperature correlation of *P. cembra* was smaller than expected; larch responded positively to temperature and grew faster in recent decades.
- Most stable and favourable growth in recent decades occurred at about 2000 m a.s.l.; a dry period since 2003 (2002 driest year since 1860s per SPEI12) coincided with growth declines at low and mid elevations.
- Weak temperature correlations at the actual forest line are attributed to its depression by historic and current pasture use and to recent warming.

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages**
- Broad species and elevation coverage within one climatic regime.
- Considers multi-season climate windows and drought indices.

**Limitations**
- Space-for-time design in a single dry valley; reforested stands of similar age and site-specific management confound elevation and age effects.
- Tree-ring data record only surviving trees and short chronologies at some sites (sampling depth ≥4–5 trees), with mixed sampling years (2014 and 2017).
- Limited replication per species and elevation; *P. sylvestris* and *P. nigra* are only present at lower elevations, so species and elevation are partly confounded.
- Temperature at the forest line interpreted in the presence of grazing history, which is not quantified.
- Statements about future composition shifts are inferences, not tests.

## Conclusion

- In inner-alpine dry regions water availability limits growth at low and also at mid elevation, and temperature is less limiting near the forest line than expected.
- The forest line is expected to rise with land abandonment and warming, and species-specific responses will change forest-line composition. Related: [[obojes_2018_water_stress_growth_larch]], [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]], [[wieser_2019_effects_climate_treeline]].
