# Torres et al. 2021 — Remote Sensing for Forest Health Assessment: A Systematic Review

## Title and Authors
**The Role of Remote Sensing for the Assessment and Monitoring of Forest Health: A Systematic Evidence Synthesis**
Pablo Torres, Marina Rodes-Blanco, Alba Viana-Soto, Hector Nieto, Mariano García — *Forests*, 2021

## Quick Overview
- **Why is it relevant?** Systematic review (PRISMA protocol) of 107 forest health RS papers (2015–2020), providing a comprehensive view of methods, sensors, and stress factors most relevant to the research wiki context.
- **What was done?** Applied PRISMA methodology to review 107 journal papers on RS-based forest health assessment (2015–2020), systematically extracting information on sensors, methods, study areas, stress factors, and RS approaches.
- **What is the main outcome?** Satellite-borne multispectral sensors (especially Landsat) dominate the literature; most studies assess a specific stress factor rather than taking an "early warning" approach; North America and Europe are the primary study regions.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept
Forests face increasing stresses from climate change, pests, fire, and drought. Remote sensing offers efficient large-area health assessment but the breadth of approaches is overwhelming. This study applies rigorous PRISMA systematic review methodology — rare in this domain — to objectively characterise the state of the art and identify gaps, including the underrepresented "early warning" perspective.

## Technical Approach
- **Protocol:** PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses)
- **Search:** Web of Science, keywords: remote sensing + forest/vegetation/tree + health/decline/dieback/stress/mortality
- **Period:** 2015–2020
- **Final selection:** 107 papers after filtering
- **Variables extracted (20):** Sensor, platform, spatial resolution, temporal resolution, study area, stress factor, RS method, validation approach, among others

## Distinctive Features
- First PRISMA-based systematic review of RS forest health literature
- Distinguishes reactive (post-disturbance assessment) from proactive (early warning) approaches
- Identifies geographic and methodological gaps objectively
- Covers bark beetles, drought, wildfire, disease, windthrow stress factors across diverse ecosystems

## Key Findings
- Publications increased consistently 2015–2020
- Most papers cover North America and Europe (less tropics, Asia, Africa)
- Satellite-borne multispectral sensors dominate; Landsat most common
- Most studies assess impact of a specific stressor (drought, bark beetle, fire) after it has occurred
- Very few studies address **early warning** (detecting stress before visible damage)
- Spectral indices (NDVI, red-edge VIs) and time series change detection are most common methods
- Machine learning integration is growing but still a minority approach

## Advantages and Limitations
- **Advantages:** PRISMA provides selection objectivity; systematic variable extraction enables meta-synthesis; identifies clear research gaps
- **Limitations:** Limited to 2015–2020 and Web of Science; rapid field evolution means the review is already partially dated

## Conclusion
RS-based forest health assessment is predominantly reactive and sensor-specific (Landsat/multispectral). The gap between early warning capacity and current practice is substantial — most studies detect damage after it has occurred. Advancing towards predictive, early-warning frameworks using time series and physiological indicators (e.g., red-edge, thermal) is the field's most needed step.
