## Title and Authors of the Paper

- "Treeline remote sensing: from tracking treeline shifts to multi-dimensional monitoring of ecotonal change"
- Matteo Garbarino, Donato Morresi, Nicolò Anselmetto, Peter J. Weisberg
- *Remote Sensing in Ecology and Conservation* (2023); doi: 10.1002/rse2.351

## Quick Overview

- **Why is it relevant?** It is the main systematic overview of how remote sensing has been used for treelines, linking sensors and methods to the ecological metrics they can measure.
- **What was done?** A systematic review and meta-analysis of 372 treeline remote-sensing papers (from 700 initial hits), with co-occurrence mapping, cluster analysis and non-metric multidimensional scaling relating RS techniques to treeline metrics.
- **What is the main outcome?** Treeline RS is dominated by pixel-based satellite classification, greenness (NDVI) trends and photointerpretation of aerial images; the authors argue for a multiscale, multi-sensor, question-oriented framework that moves from tracking the treeline position to monitoring multidimensional ecotone change.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept

- Goal: relate RS methods to key ecological metrics of treelines and identify knowledge gaps and promising directions.
- Concept: a treeline is not just a line (1D) but an ecotone with pattern, structure and species dimensions (up to 3D), and drivers act at different scales (climate at regional scale; microtopography, facilitation and competition at tree scale).
- Therefore, no single sensor suffices: the sensor must match the question and scale.

## Technical Approach

- **Search**: keyword search (treeline, timberline, forestline, treeline ecotone, alpine treeline AND remote sensing, satellite, aerial, LiDAR, UAV, image) with 700 initial papers; 292 off-topic and 36 review papers removed, giving 372 papers.
- **Classification**: papers grouped as treeline dynamics (46 %), static mapping (28 %) and a remaining group; extracted geography, scale, sensors, analysis technique, RS-derived metric, drivers and non-RS data.
- **Statistics**: co-occurrence mapping of keywords (six clusters), Mann–Whitney tests of spatiotemporal resolution between categories, cluster analysis on RS attributes (four clusters) and NMS (Gower distance) against treeline metrics.

## Distinctive Features

- Question-oriented rather than technology-oriented review.
- Proposes a conceptual multiscale model linking drivers, treeline attributes and RS data sources by scale (Fig. 7).
- Highlights UAV-based sensing as the bridge between plots and coarse satellites.

## Experimental Setup and Results

- Of treeline papers, only about 10 % use RS to describe and measure the ecotone; 94 % were research articles.
- Elevational treelines were studied more often than latitudinal ones (76 % vs 21 %); within elevational studies, upper treelines made up 96 %.
- Studies of treeline dynamics use coarser spatial resolution (mean about 186 m vs 44 m for static studies) over longer periods (about 49 vs 1 year); temporal extent ranged from 1 to 130 years (mean 26.9).
- Most common methods were pixel-based classification of satellite images and photointerpretation; object-based classification was 11 % and sub-pixel only 1 %.
- Four clusters of studies: long-term aerial (average 43.6 years, photointerpretation and GIS), long-term oblique photography (average 75.3 years), satellite/greening time series, and high-resolution mapping with LiDAR (45 %) or UAV for tree height and crown attributes.
- Recent dynamics are often quantified with greenness trends from satellite imagery; recent short-term studies delineate trees from UAV or LiDAR.

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages**
- Large, transparent sample with defined search and exclusion steps.
- Clear typology of sensors and metrics, usable for planning a monitoring design.

**Limitations**
- Based on bibliographic search terms; grey literature and non-English studies may be missed, and publication bias favours "positive" shift results.
- Counts papers, not effect sizes; the review does not synthesise actual shift rates or accuracies.
- Cluster results depend on the chosen attribute matrix.
- Published in 2023, so recent deep-learning, foundation-model and time-series methods are barely covered.
- Greenness-based dynamics are noted as often being detached from direct treeline position or tree demography.

## Conclusion

- RS can measure treelines in several dimensions, but each platform covers only part of the scale range; satellites for long, coarse monitoring, UAV/LiDAR for tree-scale structure, and field or dendro data for processes.
- Future work should combine sensors and disciplines and focus on tree-scale demography, species interactions and microsites.
