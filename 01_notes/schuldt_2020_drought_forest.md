# Schuldt et al. 2020 — Impact of the 2018 Summer Drought on Central European Forests

## Title and Authors
**A first assessment of the impact of the extreme 2018 summer drought on Central European forests**
Bernhard Schuldt, Allan Buras, Matthias Arend, Yann Vitasse et al. (28 authors) — *Basic and Applied Ecology* 45: 86–103 (2020).

## Quick Overview
- **Why is it relevant?** Establishes the 2018 hotter drought as the most damaging recent forest event in the DACH region and frames the urgency for combined ground + RS monitoring — context for [[wegler_2026_canopy_cover_loss]], [[grünig_2026_climate_change_disturbances_forest]], [[turubanove_2023_canopy_landsat]].
- **What was done?** Opinion paper synthesising climatic, physiological, ground-based, and MODIS-NDVI evidence for forest impacts of 2018 drought across Austria, Germany, Switzerland (DACH).
- **What is the main outcome?** 2018 was climatically more extreme than 2003 (MGT +3.3 °C vs 1961–1990 normal; CWB –238 mm); unprecedented hydraulic-failure-driven mortality; legacy effects continue in 2019; calls for a pan-European tree-mortality monitoring network.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept
The 2003 summer drought was previously the benchmark "hotter drought" for Europe (most severe in 500 yr). The 2018 event was more extreme and more spatially uniform. The paper documents the physiological, ecological, and economic consequences across temperate Central European forests and argues for a step-change in monitoring infrastructure.

## Technical Approach
- Climate analysis (DACH region April–October): mean growing-season air temperature (MGT), mean precipitation (MGP), mean vapour pressure deficit (MVPD), climatic water balance (CWB) from CRU TS 4.03.
- Physiological evidence: foliar water potentials below xylem hydraulic-failure thresholds for many species; widespread leaf discoloration; premature senescence (esp. *Fagus sylvatica*).
- Ground monitoring: ICP Forests Level I 16 × 16 km grid (continuous since 1984), BMEL crown condition surveys, individual research site observations.
- RS: MODIS NDVI 2000–2018, quantile-based ranking to compare 2018 with 2003 stratified by coniferous/deciduous forest.
- Discussion of pan-European monitoring gap: ground networks are spatially limited; RS lacks species attribution.

## Distinctive Features
- Combines physiology (xylem hydraulic failure thresholds), ecology (species-level mortality observations), and RS (MODIS NDVI quantiles) in a single integrated assessment.
- Explicit comparison with 2003 in both climatic anomaly magnitude and forest impact.
- Documents drought-legacy effects extending into the next year (2019 lacking leaf flushing in surviving beech trees).
- Identifies hotter drought as a distinct ecological class — not just any drought, but high-VPD drought.

## Experimental Setup and Results

**Climatic severity (DACH region 2018)**
- MGT: highest on record; +3.3 °C vs 1961–1990 long-term mean; +1.2 °C vs 2003
- MGP: equally low as 1911, 1947, 1976, 2003 record dry years
- MVPD: highest ever recorded
- CWB: −238 mm (2003: −200 mm) — second most negative on record (after 1976)

**Species-level impacts**
- *Fagus sylvatica*: premature leaf senescence from late July 2018; many individuals failed to flush in 2019 → defoliation; widespread mortality
- *Picea abies*, *Pinus sylvestris*: needle discoloration in subsequent months; bark-beetle outbreaks amplified mortality
- Many species crossed xylem hydraulic-failure thresholds

**MODIS NDVI quantile analysis**
- Area of deciduous forest in lowest NDVI quantiles 2018: ~11,200 km² vs ~5,600 km² in 2003 (≈ 2×)
- Spatial distribution more uniform than 2003
- Confirms field-based reports of greater impact

**Drought legacy**
- Reduced physiological recovery → vulnerability to secondary stressors (bark beetles, fungal pathogens)
- Mortality continued for several years post-event

## Advantages and Limitations
- **Advantages**: Cross-disciplinary synthesis (ecophysiology + ecology + RS); explicit climatic + physiological + spectral evidence chain; identifies legacy effects critical for projecting future climate impacts.
- **Limitations**: Opinion paper, not exhaustive empirical study; species attribution from RS limited to broad coniferous/deciduous split; spatial resolution of MODIS (250 m) inadequate for stand-level inferences; pan-European data sparse outside DACH.

## Conclusion
**2018 was climatically more extreme than 2003 and ecologically more damaging**. Many temperate European tree species — especially *Fagus sylvatica* and *Picea abies* — are more vulnerable to hotter droughts than previously appreciated, with mortality continuing for years through drought-legacy effects. The combination of warming + VPD increases + recurring droughts may push Central European forests toward a substantial ecological and economic transition. Urgent need for pan-European tree-mortality monitoring with combined ground + high-resolution RS support.
