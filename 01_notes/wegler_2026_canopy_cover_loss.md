# Wegler et al. 2026 — Tree Species-Specific Forest Canopy Cover Loss in Germany 2018–2024

## Title and Authors
**Tree species–specific forest canopy cover loss in Germany (2018–2024): A national-scale remote sensing assessment**
Marco Wegler, Frank Thonfeld, Patrick Kacic, Sarah Asam, Claudia Kuenzer — *Forest Ecology and Management*, 2026

## Quick Overview
- **Why is it relevant?** First national-scale, species-specific quantification of forest canopy cover loss in Germany, demonstrating how combining tree species maps with disturbance detection enables species-resolved disturbance attribution at national scale.
- **What was done?** Combined a stabilised dominant tree species map (2016 baseline) with a monthly 10 m Forest Canopy Cover Loss (FCCL) dataset (S2+Landsat, 2018–2024) to quantify species-specific FCCL across Germany's ~350,000 km² forest.
- **What is the main outcome?** Total FCCL = 8,763 km² (2018–2024); spruce dominated with 4,497 km² (51.3% of total), losing 18.6% of initial spruce area; peak FCCL in 2020–2021 with late-summer seasonal pattern; deciduous species (beech, oak) <300 km² each.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept
Climate-driven drought and bark beetle outbreaks since 2018 have caused unprecedented forest loss in Germany. Understanding which tree species are most affected, where, and when is essential for adaptive management. By combining a species map with a disturbance time series at species-specific level, this study attributes disturbance to species and reveals contrasting vulnerability patterns between coniferous and deciduous species.

## Technical Approach
- **Tree species baseline:** Stabilised Dominant Tree Species Map for 2016 — majority vote across annual Wegler et al. 2025 classifications (2016–2024) filtered with forest structure data; F1 > 0.90 for nine pure-species classes
- **FCCL dataset:** Monthly 10 m canopy cover loss maps from S2+Landsat time series (Thonfeld et al. 2026); detects natural + anthropogenic disturbances including winter months
- **Spatial aggregation:** FCCL pixels × species map → species-specific loss at district, state, national scales
- **Temporal aggregation:** Monthly, annual, cumulative

## Distinctive Features
- First Germany-wide, species-specific, monthly resolved canopy cover loss 2018–2024 at 10 m
- Stabilised multi-year species baseline reduces year-to-year classification noise for robust species attribution
- Covers both natural (bark beetle, drought) and anthropogenic (harvesting) disturbances
- Spatial hotspot analysis reveals geographic concentration of spruce loss in central low mountain ranges (Harz, Mittelgebirge)

## Experimental Setup and Results
- **Total FCCL 2018–2024:** 8,763 km²
- **Spruce:** 4,497 km² (51.3%); 18.6% of initial spruce area — dominant loss species
- **Pine:** 1,893 km² (21.6%); 7.4% relative loss
- **Beech:** < 300 km²; ~1% relative loss
- **Oak:** < 300 km²; ~1% relative loss
- **Peak years:** 2020–2021; consistent late-summer peak (bark beetle + drought phenology)
- **Spatial hotspots:** Central low mountain ranges (Harz, Thuringian Forest) for spruce FCCL

## Advantages and Limitations
- **Advantages:** Novel species × disturbance temporal resolution; national coverage; monthly granularity captures seasonal dynamics; directly policy-relevant
- **Limitations:** Species attribution quality dependent on 2016 baseline accuracy; FCCL may not distinguish mortality from harvesting at all sites; disturbance agent not differentiated

## Conclusion
Germany's 2018–2024 forest crisis was overwhelmingly a spruce crisis: 51% of canopy loss originated from spruce forests, which lost nearly 1/5 of their initial area. Deciduous species (beech, oak) showed substantially lower relative losses. The combination of multi-year species maps with monthly disturbance products enables species-resolved national forest health monitoring — a model for climate adaptation management and policy.
