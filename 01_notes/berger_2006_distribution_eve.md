# Berger & Walther 2006 — Distribution of Evergreen Broad-leaved Woody Species in Insubria

## Title and Authors
**Distribution of evergreen broad-leaved woody species in Insubria in relation to bedrock and precipitation**
Silje Berger & Gian-Reto Walther — *Botanica Helvetica* 116: 65–77 (2006).

## Quick Overview
- **Why is it relevant?** Documents how the laurophyllous (evergreen broad-leaved) species expanding into deciduous lowland forests of the southern Alps partition along environmental gradients at their northern range margin — informs RS-based monitoring of EVE expansion.
- **What was done?** Surveyed 22 EVE-rich forest sites between Lago d'Orta and Lago di Garda; recorded species cover and maximum height; related composition and size to the W→E precipitation gradient and siliceous vs calcareous bedrock.
- **What is the main outcome?** EVE species partition into three groups (W-restricted, E-restricted, region-wide); precipitation and bedrock structure local distribution within an area where winter temperatures permit survival; species at the range margin show narrower ecological breadth than in their core ranges.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept
Rising winter temperatures since the 1970s allowed many introduced and a few native evergreen broad-leaved species to naturalise in the deciduous chestnut forests around the Insubrian lakes. Within this newly suitable thermal envelope, local distribution is uneven. The paper asks whether the W→E precipitation gradient (≈2000 → <1000 mm yr⁻¹) and the corresponding W siliceous / E calcareous bedrock contrast can explain why specific EVE species occur in some lakes but not others.

## Technical Approach
- 22 study sites below 500 m a.s.l. between Lago d'Orta and Lago di Garda, selected as the most EVE-rich stands per area.
- 10 × 10 m plots with Braun-Blanquet cover values recorded separately in herb (<0.5 m), shrub (0.5–7 m), and tree (>7 m) layers.
- Max height of up to five tallest individuals per species measured (woody-stem height for the palm *Trachycarpus fortunei*).
- Species means relative to precipitation class and bedrock type.
- No formal modelling; an ecogram synthesises preferences against the two-axis gradient.

## Distinctive Features
- Targeted sampling of EVE-rich stands rather than random grid sampling, justified by the rarity of EVE species at the time and the wish to attribute presence to environment rather than dispersal opportunity.
- Combines exotic ornamentals (*Cinnamomum glanduliferum*, *Trachycarpus fortunei*, *Prunus laurocerasus*, *Ligustrum lucidum*) with natives (*Hedera helix*, *Ilex aquifolium*, *Laurus nobilis*, *Quercus ilex*, *Viburnum tinus*).
- Provides an explicit ecogram of EVE species along precipitation × bedrock axes.

## Experimental Setup and Results
Three EVE distribution types observed:
1. **W-restricted (high precipitation, siliceous)**: *Cinnamomum glanduliferum*, *Trachycarpus fortunei*, *Prunus laurocerasus*. Sizes decrease W→E with rainfall.
2. **E-restricted (lower precipitation, calcareous)**: *Quercus ilex*, *Viburnum tinus*. Restricted to dry, calcareous sites with reduced competition from deciduous species.
3. **Region-wide**: *Laurus nobilis*, *Ligustrum lucidum* — broader amplitude; *L. nobilis* size is constant across all sites.

Mechanisms inferred:
- W EVE species need humid summers; struggle near their precipitation minimum in the east.
- E sclerophyllous Mediterranean species (*Q. ilex*, *V. tinus*) thrive on shallow calcareous soils due to release from competition with faster-growing deciduous and laurophyllous species.
- Calcareous → reduced humus accumulation, lower water storage capacity, higher drought stress.

## Advantages and Limitations
- **Advantages**: Synthesises long-fragmented evidence on the Insubrian EVE invasion under a unifying gradient framework; pairs natives and exotics in a single analysis.
- **Limitations**: Small sample (n=22); no quantitative niche modelling; targeted sampling precludes inference about presence/absence beyond the EVE-rich subset; bedrock and precipitation are confounded in the W→E gradient.

## Conclusion
Within a region where rising winter temperatures have already opened the laurophyllous niche, **precipitation and bedrock explain the spatial structure of EVE expansion**. At their northern range margins species become more sensitive to local environment than in their core ranges. The W→E precipitation gradient combined with siliceous→calcareous bedrock transition produces a sharper laurophyllous–sclerophyllous distinction than expected from temperature alone. The paper is a foundational reference for thinking about EVE niche partitioning in modern RS-based mapping efforts.
