## Title and Authors of the Paper

- "Changes in Snow Depth, Snow Cover Duration, and Potential Snowmaking Conditions in Austria, 1961–2020—A Model Based Approach"
- Marc Olefs, Roland Koch, Wolfgang Schöner, Thomas Marke
- *Atmosphere* 11(12): 1330 (2020)

## Quick Overview

- **Why is it relevant?** Station snow records are sparse at high elevation, so a spatially complete, validated reconstruction of past snow conditions in the Austrian Alps is needed, including above the stations.
- **What was done?** The physically based snow cover model SNOWGRID-CL, driven by 1 × 1 km gridded observation-based temperature, precipitation and evapotranspiration, was run for 1961/62–2019/20 and validated against homogenised long-term snow observations; potential snowmaking hours were derived from wet-bulb temperature.
- **What is the main outcome?** Mean seasonal (November–April) snow depth decreased by 0.15 m, snow cover duration by 42 days and snowmaking potential by 85 h (26 %) on average over Austria, with strong altitude dependence and shifts in the 1970s and late 1980s.

## Main Goal and Fundamental Concept

- Goal: derive daily gridded natural snow conditions and snowmaking potential for Austria at 1 km and quantify their long-term changes.
- Concept: long-term snow trends reflect temperature and precipitation changes in a complex way; a model with consistent driving data avoids station network inhomogeneities and fills elevation gaps.

## Technical Approach

- **Model**: SNOWGRID-CL, a distributed, physically based snow model (one-layer or multi-layer snowpack with accumulation, compaction, melt and rain/snow partitioning), with a snowmaking module based on wet-bulb temperature thresholds.
- **Driving data**: gridded observation-based datasets of air temperature, precipitation and evapotranspiration at 1 × 1 km, designed to represent variability in complex terrain.
- **Runs**: 1961/62–2019/20 daily simulations; regionalisation and trend analysis (Mann–Kendall, Sen slope), shift-point and cluster analysis.
- **Validation**: homogenised long-term snow depth observations (HZB and other station data), spatial trend patterns and running trend analyses at 85 stations.

## Distinctive Features

- Model-based reconstruction at 1 km resolution across all altitudes, instead of station-only trends.
- Validation against homogenised station series, including trend detection skill.
- Adds technical-snow potential as an additional variable.

## Experimental Setup and Results

- Austria-wide mean decrease (1961/62–2019/20, Nov–Apr): snow depth −0.15 m, snow cover duration −42 days, snowmaking hours −85 h (26 %).
- Relative changes depend strongly on altitude: −75 % to −5 % for snow depth and −55 % to −10 % (no change above 2000 m a.s.l.) for snow cover duration; changes are statistically significant below about 2000 m a.s.l.
- Shift-point analysis identified major shifts in snow depth in the 1970s and in the late 1980s.
- Validation: very good agreement of spatial trend patterns with observations, and a percentage correct of 86 % for the running Mann–Kendall trend test at 85 stations.

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages**
- Spatially complete and consistent; usable for high elevations that lack stations.
- Validated against homogenised data.

**Limitations**
- Model output depends on the driving gridded precipitation, which is uncertain at high elevation (undercatch, wind redistribution), and the snow model does not represent wind transport, avalanches or forest effects at 1 km.
- The results are for Austria only, and above 2000 m no significant trends were detected, so the treeline zone (about 1800–2300 m) lies close to the transition and is affected by uncertainty.
- Snowmaking results are not applicable to individual ski resorts (authors' caveat).
- No ecological interpretation of snow change.

## Conclusion

- Austrian snow depth and duration decreased substantially, mostly below about 2000 m, in two step-like shifts.
- The product could serve as a snow covariate for treeline and vegetation analyses, but the weak signals above 2000 m and the 1 km grid limit its use at the treeline. Compare [[matiu_2021_snow_depth_trends]] (stations, Alpine-wide), and [[snow_cover_treeline]].
