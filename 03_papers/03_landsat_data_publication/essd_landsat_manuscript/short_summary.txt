Climate warming and forest management alterations are changing the forest composition in Italy. However, maps covering these dynamics in space and time are lacking. Using 39 years of Landsat satellite images and deep learning, we mapped Dominant Tree Species and evergreen, deciduous, and coniferous Leaf Type Cover annually from 1987 to 2025 at 30m resolution. This dataset enables tracking long-term natural dynamics in forests for biodiversity or disturbance monitoring.

Consistent long-term maps of forest leaf type cover and dominant tree species at high spatial resolution are currently unavailable for Italy. The study presents annual 30 m resolution maps of continuous evergeen broad-leaved, deciduous, and coniferous leaf type cover (LTC) fractions and dominant tree species (DTS) class probabilities for all forested pixels in Italy spanning 1987–2025, derived from the Landsat Collection-2 Level-2 archive (Landsat 4/5/7/8/9). Time Series Transformer models are trained via a three-stage workflow combining contrastive pretraining with supervised fine-tuning on Italian forest plot observations, forest inventories, and a synthetically generated training dataset to predict both tasks. Pixel-level uncertainty estimates are provided as the standard deviation of a five-seed ensemble for both outputs. A three-year observation pooling strategy is applied to increase cloud-free observation density and mitigate inter-annual variability. Evaluation of the time series maps are based on hold-out datasets across different spatio-temporal scales and a comparison with historical orthophotos. The final products achieve an average MAE of 13.5% cover with an R² of 0.75 for LTC mapping and an overall accuracy of 0.69 across all species-related DTS classes, with most class confusions occuring within specific leaf types. The 39-year time series enables historical reconstruction of forest composition dynamics and long-term ecological change detection at national scale and relevant time scales for climate-driven species range shifts.
