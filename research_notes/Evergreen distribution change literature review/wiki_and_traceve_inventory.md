## Q1. Which notes/concept pages in the wiki are relevant to the paper?

### Takeaway
About 55 wiki notes are usable. The ecology of EVE spread is covered by only a handful of papers (Berger 2006, Conedera 2018, Alessi 2019, Chelli 2017, Herraiz 2025, Jin 2023, Fang 2016), most are Insubria/Spain/subtropical-China, and there is no note on Italian-scale EVE change, on Walther's original laurophyllisation papers, on Mediterranean-core sclerophyll expansion (Delzon, Hernandez, Penuelas appear only in TRACEVE), or on HMM/trajectory methods. Remote-sensing, Landsat-bias and disturbance coverage is much better.

### Cited Findings

#### A. EVE ecology, drivers of spread (Intro / Drivers / Discussion)

- **berger_2006_distribution_eve** (core). Berger S, Walther G-R (2006) Distribution of evergreen broad-leaved woody species in Insubria in relation to bedrock and precipitation. *Botanica Helvetica* 116:65-77. DOI: (not in note). Wiki says: 22 EVE-rich sites between Lago d'Orta and Garda; species partition into W-restricted (humid, siliceous: Cinnamomum, Trachycarpus, Prunus laurocerasus), E-restricted (dry, calcareous: Q. ilex, Viburnum tinus) and region-wide (Laurus, Ligustrum); rising winter temperatures since 1970s permit survival. Section: Intro, Drivers (precipitation/bedrock stratification). Reason: only site-level evidence for sclerophyll vs laurophyll niche separation, which RQ6 of 03_tree_species_cover_landsat relies on. Caveat (wiki): targeted sampling of EVE-rich stands only, no modelling, 22 sites.
- **conedera_2018_drivers_evergreen** (core). Conedera M, Wohlgemuth T, Tanadini M, Pezzatti GB (2018) Drivers of broadleaved evergreen species spread into deciduous forests in the southern Swiss Alps. *Regional Environmental Change* 18:425-436. DOI: (not in note; TRACEVE gives 10.1007/s10113-017-1212-7, citing it as 2017). Wiki says: 200 plots around Lago Maggiore, tobit regression; propagule pressure (distance to garden) is the dominant driver for 3 of 4 species, winter temperature secondary; spread driven more by land-use change (abandoned coppice, gardens) than warming. Section: Intro, Drivers, Discussion. Reason: directly challenges a "climate-only" framing and is the key counterweight for the driver analysis. Caveat: one region, four species, cross-sectional.
- **alessi_2019_refugia** (core). Alessi N, Tesitel J, Zerbe S, Spada F, Agrillo E, Wellstein C (2019) Ancient refugia and present-day habitat suitability of native laurophylls in Italy. *Journal of Vegetation Science*. DOI: 10.1111/jvs.12743 (given in note). Wiki says: 17,087 Italian plots, 11 native laurophylls, most occupy <50% of potential range (concept page says 9 of 11); dispersal limitation from central-Apennine refugia explains this better than climate; high potential for further expansion. Section: Intro, Drivers, Discussion (future habitat). Reason: only Italy-wide EVE distribution analysis; co-author overlap with the user's group. Caveat: native laurophylls only (not sclerophylls); note gives volume/pages as "accepted article, ~2019"; the note's "<50%" vs concept page's "9 of 11" should be checked against the PDF.
- **chelli_2017_climate** (core). Chelli S, Wellstein C, Campetella G, Canullo R, Tonin R, Zerbe S, Gerdol R (2017) Climate change response of vegetation across climatic zones in Italy. *Climate Research* 71:249-262. DOI: (not in note; TRACEVE gives 10.3354/cr01443). Wiki says: review of 90 papers/238 sites; warming enhances growth but drought overrides this even outside the Mediterranean; gaps for sub-Mediterranean/Mediterranean community change and land-use x climate. Section: Intro, Drivers. Reason: Italian climatic-zone framing; also identifies the knowledge gap the paper fills.
- **herraiz_2025_phen_shifts_mediterranean** (core). Herraiz AD, Salazar-Zarzosa P, Acosta-Munoz C, Hernandez-Clemente R, Villar R (2025) Aridity-induced phenological shifts and greening trends in Mediterranean forest species: insights from 28 years of Landsat data in southern Spain. *Ecological Indicators* 171:113115. ### Inferences
- The TRACEVE list is a ready-made source for the ecology and Landsat-methods sections; the proposal is 2023, so everything after ~2022 on EVE (e.g. Q. ilex dieback, Italian EVE trends) is absent and must come from the wiki or new searches.
- Several in-text claims in the proposal's state of research are attributed to local grey literature (Gianoni 1988, Klotzli & Walther 1999, Carraro 1999); I suggest citing Walther 2002 and Conedera 2018 instead where possible.
- Tolnai 2016, Roy 2016, Wulder 2016 and Masek 2020 would provide primary-source support for the Landsat observation-density and sensor-continuity argument that now rests on Bayle 2024 alone.
- The Zellweger and Forzieri entries are "Response to Comment" items; the correct primary papers should be cited if their claim is used.
