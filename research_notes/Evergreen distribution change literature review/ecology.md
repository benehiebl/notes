# Ecology literature: spread of evergreen broad-leaved species (EVEs) into deciduous/temperate forests (Italy, southern Alps, Europe)

Method and honesty note: bibliographic data for every entry below was checked against CrossRef (api.crossref.org) in this session. Content statements are of three kinds: (a) from abstracts/web snippets retrieved this session (cited), (b) from my background knowledge of the paper, NOT re-read in full (marked [from memory, not re-read]), (c) explicit gaps. No full texts were read. Italy-specific EVE literature is thin; this is a finding in itself.

## Q1. Which 15-25 papers are most important (ranked), what do they say, and how good are they?

### Takeaway
The core "laurophyllisation" narrative rests on a small Walther/Carraro/Klötzli/Gianoni (Ticino/Insubria) cluster, which the wiki's Conedera 2018 and Berger 2006 already partly cover. New and genuinely useful additions are: (i) the original Walther framing papers (2002, 2005, 2007), (ii) Italian/Mediterranean expansion evidence (Alessi 2021 Laurus; Delzon 2013 Q. ilex; Peñuelas & Boada 2003), (iii) land-abandonment/forest-expansion context for Italy and Europe (Garbarino 2020; Palmero-Iniesta 2021), (iv) thermophilization/microclimate/lag framing (Bertrand 2011; Zellweger 2020), and (v) the counter-narrative of aridity-driven regeneration failure/dieback (Garcia-Fayos 2020; Carnicer 2011). I found NO peer-reviewed wall-to-wall spatial quantification of EVE change in Italy, which supports the paper's stated gap (to be checked by remote-sensing researcher).

### Cited Findings

**A. Papers already in the wiki (evaluate only; not re-verified here, no DOIs checked, based on wiki concept page [evergreen_broadleaved_expansion.md])**
- berger_2006_distribution_eve: Insubrian EVE distribution partitions by precipitation and bedrock. Regional (Ticino/Lake Maggiore/Como), presence data; descriptive. Cite in Intro/Methods for species-environment partitioning. Worth reading once.
- conedera_2018_drivers_evergreen: propagule pressure > climate for 3 of 4 species; stand maturity after coppice abandonment. Strongest causal paper in the set but limited to one region (southern Switzerland) and 4 species; the key citation for "climate is permissive, not the trigger". Read in depth.
- alessi_2019_refugia (J Veg Sci 30:564-574, DOI 10.1111/jvs.12743, CrossRef-verified): 11 native Italian laurophylls, most below 50% of potential range (non-equilibrium) [Springer/MUNI snippets confirm this]. Core Italian citation; SDM-based so "potential" depends on model assumptions. Read in depth.
- chelli_2017_climate, fang_2016_eve_mosaics, jin_2023_drivers_differentiation_evergreen, fady_2025_native_trees_mediterranean, dyderski_2025_species_shift: peripheral/contextual (Italian climatic-zone review; Chinese subtropical stand mosaics; global drivers; Mediterranean tree range shifts; species shift projections). Cite for context only; fang_2016 is of limited relevance to European temperate-forest invasion (different system, soil P).

**B. New papers, ranked by importance for the planned paper**

1. Walther, G.-R., Berger, S., Sykes, M. T. (2005). An ecological 'footprint' of climate change. Proceedings of the Royal Society B 272(1571): 1427-1432. DOI 10.1098/rspb.2005.3119 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory, not re-read]: uses Ticino/Insubria EVEs to argue that winter-temperature-limited evergreen species are indicators of warming, with frost-day/coldest-month thresholds linked to occurrence; the "classic" climate-forcing statement.
 - Scope: Insubrian region, species-presence data, climate thresholds.
 - Critique: correlative; Conedera 2018 later showed climate alone does not explain timing (similar winters in 1915-25 and 1950-60 without invasion, per wiki). Cite as the origin of the climate-driven hypothesis and then contrast. Read in depth (Intro, Discussion).

2. Walther, G.-R. (2002). Weakening of climatic constraints with global warming and its consequences for evergreen broad-leaved species. Folia Geobotanica 37(1): 129-139. DOI 10.1007/BF02803195 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: conceptual/synthesis: winter cold limits EVEs; warming relaxes this constraint, predicting spread in Europe and other regions (eastern N. America, Japan).
 - Critique: review-type, not a quantitative test; period-appropriate but predates drought-dieback evidence. Cite for the mechanism "relaxed frost limit" and the biome-shift framing. Worth reading.

3. Carraro, G., Gianoni, P., Mossi, R. (1999). Climatic influence on vegetation changes: a verification on regional scale of the laurophyllisation. In: Klötzli F., Walther G.-R. (eds) Recent Shifts in Vegetation Boundaries of Deciduous Forests, Especially Due to General Global Warming. Birkhäuser, Basel, pp. 31-51. DOI 10.1007/978-3-0348-8722-9_3 (CrossRef-verified chapter; conference volume).
 - Finding [from memory]: regional forest-plot verification of laurophyllisation in southern Switzerland (relevés across decades); original empirical source for the expansion rather than the later syntheses.
 - Critique: book chapter, not strictly peer-reviewed journal; access may be hard. Cite for the observational baseline (with Gianoni et al. 1988, which I could not find/verify [unverified]). Only skim.

4. Alessi, N., Wellstein, C., Spada, F., Zerbe, S. (2021). Population structure of Laurus nobilis L. in Central Italian forests: evidence for its ongoing expansion. Rendiconti Lincei. Scienze Fisiche e Naturali 32(2): 365-376. DOI 10.1007/s12210-021-00981-7 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding (abstract snippets, [bia.unibz.it](https://bia.unibz.it/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Population-structure-of-Laurus-nobilis-L/991006491296701241)): 16 sites; stable and dynamic population structures most common, interpreted as expansion; tied to growing forest cover/age after rural abandonment; ungulate browsing linked to declining structures; also lists warming, land-use change, CO2 as suggested drivers.
 - Critique: one species, 16 sites, structure-as-proxy-for-trend (space-for-time). Best Italian field evidence for expansion and for land abandonment as driver. Read in depth; cite in Intro/Discussion.

5. Peñuelas, J., Boada, M. (2003). A global change-induced biome shift in the Montseny mountains (NE Spain). Global Change Biology 9(2): 131-140. DOI 10.1046/j.1365-2486.2003.00566.x (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: combined forest-inventory, photograph and climate analysis shows upward shift of Mediterranean/holm-oak-type vegetation and retreat of beech since the mid-20th century, attributed jointly to warming/drought and land-use change.
 - Critique: single massif; confounded climate vs abandonment (the authors discuss both). The best Mediterranean precedent for a "biome shift" framing. Read in depth (biome-shift framing, drivers).

6. Delzon, S., Urli, M., Samalens, J.-C., Lamy, J.-B., Lischke, H., Sin, F., et al. (2013). Field evidence of colonisation by Holm oak, at the northern margin of its distribution range, during the Anthropocene period. PLoS ONE 8(11): e80443. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0080443 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory; numbers not re-checked]: genetic/ecological field evidence of Q. ilex colonisation of the northern (Atlantic France) margin consistent with warming-linked range expansion plus human history.
 - Critique: Atlantic France not Italy; mixes anthropogenic planting/history with climate. Cite as evidence of poleward Q. ilex spread; skim.

7. Garbarino, M., Morresi, D., Urbinati, C., Malandra, F., Motta, R., Sibona, E. M., Vitali, A., Weisberg, P. J. (2020). Contrasting land use legacy effects on forest landscape dynamics in the Italian Alps and the Apennines. Landscape Ecology 35: 2679-2694. DOI 10.1007/s10980-020-01013-9 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding (web snippet): forest expansion dominant landscape transition in both ranges, ~0.6 % per year overall; strongest where recent agricultural abandonment coincides with favourable climate; more extensive at lower Apennine elevations.
 - Critique: all forest, not EVE-specific; but the key Italian land-use driver reference and supplies expected change rates and elevation/abandonment covariates. Read in depth for the Methods covariate choice.

8. Palmero-Iniesta, M., Pino, J., Pesquer, L., Espelta, J. M. (2021). Recent forest area increase in Europe: expanding and regenerating forests differ in their regional patterns, drivers and productivity trends. European Journal of Forest Research 140(4): 793-805. DOI 10.1007/s10342-021-01366-z (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: Europe-wide 1990-2012 forest-area increase split into expansion (new forest) and regeneration (previously cut), with distinct regional drivers; Mediterranean/southern Europe dominated by abandonment-driven expansion.
 - Critique: CORINE-based, coarse (25 ha units), no leaf-habit/species. Cite for Europe-wide context of land abandonment. Skim.

9. Bertrand, R., Lenoir, J., Piedallu, C., et al. (2011). Changes in plant community composition lag behind climate warming in lowland forests. Nature 479(7374): 517-520. DOI 10.1038/nature10548 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: community temperature index (thermophilization) in French forests lags warming, especially in lowlands, indicating non-equilibrium.
 - Critique: understorey herbs, France; no EVEs. Cite for lag/non-equilibrium framing (complements Alessi 2019). Skim.

10. Zellweger, F., De Frenne, P., Lenoir, J., et al. (2020). Forest microclimate dynamics drive plant responses to warming. Science 368(6492): 772-775. DOI 10.1126/science.aba6880 (CrossRef-verified; note two published Comments, doi 10.1126/science.abd3850 and abd9920, challenge details).
 - Finding [from memory]: across European forests, understorey thermophilization is buffered by canopy cover; canopy closure/maturity cools the understorey in summer (and can buffer winter lows).
 - Critique: summer-temperature focus; EVE winter-minimum link is by inference (my inference: canopy closure may buffer winter frost, relevant to Conedera's "forest maturity" driver; not tested in the paper). Cite in Discussion of stand maturity; skim.

11. Fehr, V., Burga, C. A. (2016). The spread of Trachycarpus fortunei in southern Ticino... Palms 60(3): 125-136 [exact title unverified; no DOI found in CrossRef; found at palms.org PDF: https://palms.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Vol60n3p125-136.pdf].
 - Finding (web snippet): field study 2013-2014 of EVEs spreading into deciduous forests of southern Ticino/northern Lago Maggiore; windmill palm the most conspicuous; weighs warming and human activity over 40 years. WSL (2023) expects strong spread near settlements, slow spread in forests far from settlements, limit around 900 m ([WSL](https://wsl.ch/en/news/the-ticino-palm-an-inconvenient-beauty-under-scrutiny)).
 - Critique: trade journal (International Palm Society); weak peer-review standing. Use only if no better source; [unverified] metadata. Skim.

12. Walther, G.-R., Gritti, E. S., Berger, S., Hickler, T., Tang, Z., Sykes, M. T. (2007). Palms tracking climate change. Global Ecology and Biogeography 16(6): 801-809. DOI 10.1111/j.1466-8238.2007.00328.x (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: palm distributions worldwide are bounded by cold-temperature thresholds; model projects northward shifts, using Trachycarpus as European example.
 - Critique: coarse global climate envelope; supports native vs alien EVE thermal-limit argument. Skim.

13. Garcia-Fayos, P., Monleon, V. J., Espigares, T., Nicolau, J. M., Bochet, E. (2020). Increasing aridity threatens the sexual regeneration of Quercus ilex (holm oak) in Mediterranean ecosystems. PLOS ONE 15(10): e0239755. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0239755 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding (snippet): aridity increase over recent decades has drastically reduced Q. ilex recruitment at the dry edge (Iberian System), adults persist but numbers will decline.
 - Critique: Spain, dry edge; supports "expansion in the north vs decline in the south" two-sided framing. Read for Discussion.

14. Carnicer, J., Coll, M., Ninyerola, M., Pons, X., Sánchez, G., Peñuelas, J. (2011). Widespread crown condition decline, food web disruption, and amplified tree mortality with increased climate change-type drought. PNAS 108(4): 1474-1478. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1010070108 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: Catalan forest inventory shows Q. ilex and Pinus decline with drought, with dieback amplified by pests.
 - Critique: Catalonia; short period. Cite for dieback side of the paper; complements wiki's seddaiu_2025 and puletti_2019.

15. Hickler, T., Vohland, K., Feehan, J., et al. (2012; online 2011). Projecting the future distribution of European potential natural vegetation zones with a generalized, tree species-based dynamic vegetation model. Global Ecology and Biogeography 21(1): 50-63. DOI 10.1111/j.1466-8238.2010.00613.x (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: LPJ-GUESS projects a northward expansion of Mediterranean/evergreen vegetation zones and CO2 physiological effects.
 - Critique: process model, coarse; useful for the CO2/drought driver argument and future projections. Skim.

16. Peñuelas, J., Ogaya, R., Boada, M., Jump, A. S. (2007). Migration, invasion and decline: changes in recruitment and forest structure in a warming-linked shift of European beech forest in Catalonia. Ecography 30(6): 829-837. DOI 10.1111/j.2007.0906-7590.05247.x (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: follow-up on Montseny; beech decline at lower edge and recruitment upward.
 - Critique: same site as #5; redundant if #5 is cited.

17. Rusterholz, H.-P., Schneuwly, J., Baur, B. (2018). Invasion of the alien shrub Prunus laurocerasus in suburban deciduous forests: effects on native vegetation and soil properties. Acta Oecologica 92: 44-51. DOI 10.1016/j.actao.2018.08.004 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding (EPPO summary snippet): invaded plots had fewer native species in ground and shrub layer and lower soil moisture; effects grow with plant age.
 - Critique: Basel region, 12 sites; impacts not drivers. Cite for ecological consequences of alien EVE spread.

18. Abrahamczyk, S., Otto, A., Böhnert, T., Weigend, M. (2024). Naturalization of Prunus laurocerasus in a forest in Germany. Biological Invasions 26 [pages/issue not retrieved; CrossRef-verified title/authors/volume]. DOI 10.1007/s10530-024-03325-2.
 - Finding (web snippet): found on 19 of 24 transects, most plants <20 years old, established since 2000; high fruit set and seedlings; authors suggest warmer winters may have helped (reported by media; verify in paper).
 - Critique: Germany, single forest; shows the phenomenon is pan-European and recent. Skim.

19. Metcalfe, D. J. (2005). Hedera helix L. (Biological Flora of the British Isles). Journal of Ecology 93(3): 632-648. DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2745.2005.01021.x (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: ivy autecology: shade tolerance, frost sensitivity of leaves/seedlings, bird dispersal, response to climate.
 - Critique: species monograph; good source for the Hedera trait basis (shade tolerance, frost limits). Skim for Introduction.

20. Lenoir, J., Gégout, J.-C., Marquet, P. A., de Ruffray, P., Brisse, H. (2008). A significant upward shift in plant species optimum elevation during the 20th century. Science 320(5884): 1768-1771. DOI 10.1126/science.1156831 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: French forest plant optima shifted ~29 m per decade upslope.
 - Critique: no EVEs, but standard citation for elevational shift; marginal.

21. Vitasse, Y., Ursenbacher, S., Klein, G., et al. (2021). Phenological and elevational shifts of plants, animals and fungi under climate change in the European Alps. Biological Reviews 96(5): 1816-1835. DOI 10.1111/brv.12727 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: review of Alpine shifts; broad context only. Marginal for this paper.

22. Walther, G.-R., Carraro, G., Klötzli, F. (2001). Evergreen broad-leaved species as indicators for climate change. In: Walther G.-R., Burga C.A., Edwards P.J. (eds) "Fingerprints" of Climate Change. Springer, pp. 151-162. DOI 10.1007/978-1-4419-8692-4_10 (CrossRef-verified).
 - Finding [from memory]: accessible synthesis of Ticino laurophyllisation as climate indicator. Book chapter; overlaps with #1-3. Skim.

### Inferences
- Narrative for the paper: warming relaxes frost limit (Walther 2002/2005) -> but realised expansion is controlled by propagule pressure and stand maturity (Conedera 2018) and by land abandonment (Garbarino 2020; Alessi 2021; Palmero-Iniesta 2021) -> native laurophylls remain out of equilibrium (Alessi 2019) -> but at dry southern edges aridity reduces regeneration and causes dieback (Garcia-Fayos 2020; Carnicer 2011). This supports the paper's two-sided design (gain and dieback zones).
- Because land abandonment and warming are collinear since ~1950, the 40-year Landsat trajectory design cannot separate them without explicit land-use covariates (my inference).

### Gaps
- Klötzli & Walther (eds., 1999) book itself and Gianoni et al. 1988: not retrievable/verified in CrossRef search [unverified].
- "Mediterranization" literature (Hernández/Peñuelas as requested): no CrossRef match found for a paper by that name; not retrieved. Hofgaard: no relevant match found.
- No Italian paper found that quantifies Q. ilex or EVE expansion spatially over decades; searches for northern Italy (Euganean Hills, Garda, Lake Como) returned only Alessi 2019/2021.
- Corsica Q. ilex reforestation 1975-2000 (snippet only) not identified/verified.
- Alien-vs-native EVE comparison (Ligustrum lucidum, Cinnamomum) in Italy: no verified peer-reviewed sources found.
- Full texts not read for any item; all [from memory] statements need verification before citing.

## Q2. Drivers, rates, non-equilibrium, native vs alien, biome-shift framing: which papers cover which?

### Takeaway
Climate (winter minimum): Walther 2002, 2005, 2007. Propagule pressure/forest maturity: Conedera 2018 (wiki), Alessi 2021, Zellweger 2020 (microclimate, inference). Land abandonment: Garbarino 2020, Palmero-Iniesta 2021, Peñuelas & Boada 2003. CO2: only indirectly (Hickler 2012; Alessi 2021 lists it as suggested). Drought/dieback: Garcia-Fayos 2020, Carnicer 2011. Non-equilibrium/refugia: Alessi 2019, Bertrand 2011. Native vs alien: Berger 2006, Rusterholz 2018, Abrahamczyk 2024, Fehr & Burga 2016. Biome shift: Peñuelas & Boada 2003, Walther 2002.

### Cited Findings
- Observed expansion rates for EVEs: none quantitatively verified; Italy-wide forest expansion ~0.6 %/yr (all forest) — [Garbarino 2020 snippet](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10980-020-01013-9) [URL from DOI; snippet via web search].
- Q. ilex dry-edge regeneration decline — [Garcia-Fayos 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0239755).
- Laurus expansion linked to abandonment, browsing limits — [Alessi 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12210-021-00981-7).
- Holocene/paleo context: evergreen oak expansion with climate warming in Tuscany/peri-Adriatic (web snippet, J. Biogeography 2009; paper not identified or verified [unverified]).

### Inferences
- No source tests CO2 fertilisation of EVEs directly; CO2 should be treated as untested.

### Gaps
- Quantitative EVE expansion rates in Italy are missing in the verified literature.
