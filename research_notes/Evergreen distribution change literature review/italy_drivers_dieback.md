## Q1. Primary literature on Q. ilex dieback and hot-drought mortality in Italy (2012, 2017, 2022, 2024)

### Takeaway
Holm oak dieback in Italy is documented mainly for Tuscany (2012, 2017 events), Sardinia and southern Italy, by a small cluster of groups (Bussotti/Pollastrini/Alderotti in Florence; Colangelo/Gentilesca/Ripullone in Basilicata; Sardinian forest services). Evidence points to drought/heat as trigger, with pathogens (Phytophthora, Botryosphaeriaceae) and intraspecific/site differences as modifiers. I found no peer-reviewed paper quantifying 2022 holm oak mortality in Italy and only the Seddaiu (Forest@) overview plus the Satta preprint for 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Pollastrini M, Puletti N, Selvi F, Iacopetti G, Bussotti F (2019). Widespread crown defoliation after a drought and heat wave in the forests of Tuscany (Central Italy) and their recovery - a case study from summer 2017. *Frontiers in Forests and Global Change* 2: 74. DOI 10.3389/ffgc.2019.00074. Deciduous species (Fagus, Q. pubescens, Q. cerris) shed leaves but kept apical buds and restored canopy in 2018; Q. ilex and Phillyrea latifolia suffered total/partial crown desiccation and branch dieback and only partly resprouted. Scale: case study of representative forest communities in central Italy, event identified with SPI and ESA soil moisture data. Assessment: DEEP READ; the most direct Italian evidence for evergreen vs deciduous drought response; case study not wall-to-wall. Cite in INTRO and DISC. — [CrossRef + OpenAlex abstract](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffgc.2019.00074)
- Alderotti F, Sillo F, Brilli F, Bussotti F, et al. (2023). Quercus ilex L. dieback is genetically determined: evidence provided by dendrochronology, delta13C and SSR genotyping. *Science of the Total Environment* 904: 166809. DOI 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.166809. Two southern Tuscany stands (declining vs non-declining) differ in ring-width responses, delta13C (more conservative water use at non-declining stand) and SSR genotype; both the 2012 and 2017 droughts are analysed. Assessment: DEEP READ; shows two close stands can differ strongly, i.e. dieback hotspots are patchy and partly genetic; n=2 stands, so the "genetically determined" title overreaches (note: my reading, based on abstract only). Cite in DISC for patchiness of dieback. — [OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.166809)
- Seddaiu S, Ruiu PA, Piras G, Scanu B, Satta GGA, et al. (2025). Vulnerabilita delle foreste mediterranee a eventi climatici estremi ... il caso del disseccamento del leccio in Italia / The vulnerability of Mediterranean forests to extreme heat and drought events: the case of holm oak dieback in Italy. *Forest@* 22: 56-65. DOI 10.3832/efor4934-022. Already in wiki (seddaiu_2025_quercus_dieback); CrossRef confirms the record. Assessment: only national-scale overview covering 2017 and 2024 events; it is a perspective/overview, not an experiment. Use as the anchor INTRO citation but pair with primary papers. — [CrossRef](https://doi.org/10.3832/efor4934-022)
- Gentilesca T, Camarero JJ, Colangelo M, Nole A, Ripullone F (2017). Drought-induced oak decline in the western Mediterranean region: an overview on current evidences, mechanisms and management options to improve forest resilience. *iForest* 10: 796-806. DOI 10.3832/ifor2317-010. Review of oak decline cases in the Mediterranean (focus Italy and Spain): drought is main trigger, with warming, pathogens and high stand density as aggravators; growth decline precedes death. Assessment: worth citing (citations ~180), but focus is mostly deciduous / ring-porous oaks, not Q. ilex specifically; use in INTRO/DISC for mechanisms and management. — [OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.3832/ifor2317-010)
- Colangelo M, Camarero JJ, Borghetti M, Gentilesca T, Oliva J, Redondo MA, Ripullone F (2018). Drought and Phytophthora are associated with the decline of oak species in southern Italy. *Frontiers in Plant Science* 9: 1595. DOI 10.3389/fpls.2018.01595. Compares Q. cerris and Q. pubescens in southern Italy: Q. cerris suffers more, growth of declining trees falls from ~2000 onward as drought/warming intensified, and Phytophthora present. Caution: deciduous oaks, NOT Q. ilex (the full author list beyond the first four is from memory and should be checked: CrossRef printed only four names). Assessment: cite for decline in the deciduous matrix that evergreens might replace; do not cite for holm oak. — [OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2018.01595)
- Frisullo S, Lima G, Magnano di San Lio G, Camele I, Melissano L, Puglisi I, Pane A, et al. (2018). Phytophthora cinnamomi involved in the decline of holm oak (Quercus ilex) stands in southern Italy. *Forest Science* 64(3): 290-298. DOI 10.1093/forsci/fxx010. P. cinnamomi consistently associated with holm oak decline in 7 municipalities of Lecce (Apulia); considered primary causal agent there. Assessment: regional pathogen case, no drought analysis; cite in DISC as non-climatic cause that could confound drought attribution. — [OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.1093/forsci/fxx010)
- Bussotti F, Papitto G, Di Martino D, Cocciufa C, Cindolo C, Cenni E, Bettini D, et al. (2021). Defoliation, recovery and increasing mortality in Italian forests: levels, patterns and possible consequences for forest multifunctionality. *Forests* 12(11): 1476. DOI 10.3390/f12111476. 24-year ICP Forests Level I assessment, 253 plots: national crown condition stable overall but more extremely defoliated and dead trees; extreme defoliation in severe summer-drought years, mortality in following years; deciduous species recover, Quercus species showed progressively decreasing defoliation overall; Castanea and Pinus critical. Assessment: useful national baseline; note it does not single out a holm oak decline at national scale (a nuance against assuming a uniform dieback). Cite in INTRO/DISC. — [OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.3390/f12111476)
- Alderotti F, Bussotti F, Gori A, Ferrini F, et al. (2025). Moderately and severely defoliated Quercus ilex L. trees exhibit hydraulic dysfunction and carbon depletion: physiological implications for Mediterranean forest monitoring. *Tree Physiology* 45: tpaf033. DOI 10.1093/treephys/tpaf033. 40-50 % loss of hydraulic conductivity and depleted non-structural carbohydrates in moderately/severely defoliated holm oaks over two years. Assessment: mechanistic, few trees; cite in DISC only to justify why remote sensing canopy loss precedes mortality. (author list beyond four truncated in CrossRef output) — [OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.1093/treephys/tpaf033)
- Lloret F, Siscart D, Dalmases C (2004). Canopy recovery after drought dieback in holm-oak Mediterranean forests of Catalonia (NE Spain). *Global Change Biology* 10(12): 2092-2099. DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2486.2004.00870.x. After the 1994 drought up to 76 % of Q. ilex trees showed complete canopy dieback; stump and crown resprouting was extensive; damage depended on lithological substrate (rooting depth). Assessment: classic non-Italian reference for holm oak dieback and resprouting, relevant for interpreting Landsat-based "dieback" as often non-lethal. Cite in DISC. — [Semantic Scholar abstract](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2486.2004.00870.x)
- Satta GGA, Giannetti F, Deidda A, Brandano A, Lentini A, Piras G, Ruiu PA, Seddaiu S, Scanu B (2025). Detection and Modeling of Drought-induced Forest Dieback in Mediterranean Ecosystems. SSRN preprint. DOI 10.2139/ssrn.5761729. Already in wiki as seddaiu_2025_mediterranean_dieback. CrossRef still lists only the SSRN record (no journal version found in CrossRef as of this search; [unverified] whether a journal version exists elsewhere). Assessment: PREPRINT, flag as not peer reviewed; relevant because it maps ~110,400 ha dieback in Sardinia 2023 vs 2024 (per wiki note) - useful for DISC as the only wall-to-wall 2024 map. Do not cite as established fact.

### Inferences
- A defensible INTRO sentence: holm oak shows drought/heat-triggered crown dieback in Tuscany (2012, 2017), Sardinia (2024) and pathogen-associated decline in Apulia, but the decline is patchy (Alderotti 2023) and partly recoverable via resprouting (Pollastrini 2019, Lloret 2004).
- Because dieback is often crown loss with resprouting, annual Landsat leaf-type/species-cover maps may record transient loss; the paper should distinguish temporary crown loss from true cover loss.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed quantification of 2022 holm oak mortality in Italy found.
- Requested names Pollastrini 2019, Alderotti 2023/2024, Gentilesca 2017, Colangelo: all found except an Alderotti 2024 paper (CrossRef returned only Alderotti 2023, 2025, 2026 abstract). Bussotti and Peñuelas/Lloret Q. ilex primary studies in Spain (Ogaya, Peñuelas) were not systematically searched beyond Lloret 2004 and Peñuelas et al. 2001 (Forest Science 47: 214-218, DOI 10.1093/forestscience/47.2.214; abstract not read, [unverified] content).
- Cork oak (Q. suber) dieback in Sardinia: no suitable peer-reviewed paper found in my searches (a 2025 Bregant et al., Forests 16(7): 1052, DOI 10.3390/f16071052, on Botryosphaeriaceae/Phytophthora and holm oak decline appears to be Sardinia-related but I did not read it; [unverified] scope).

## Q2. Forest cover expansion and land abandonment in Italy

### Takeaway
Italy has undergone strong post-war forest expansion on abandoned farmland and pasture, documented by national inventories, land-cover maps and landscape studies in the Alps and Apennines. These papers explain WHERE new forest appears (low elevation, near existing forest edges), but none separate evergreen from deciduous gain, which is the novelty space of the planned paper.

### Cited Findings
- Garbarino M, Morresi D, Urbinati C, Malandra F, Motta R, Sibona E, Vitali A, et al. (2020). Contrasting land use legacy effects on forest landscape dynamics in the Italian Alps and the Apennines. *Landscape Ecology* 35(12): 2679-2694. DOI 10.1007/s10980-020-01013-9. Corine Land Cover over 28 years plus aerial images over 58 years at 8 Alpine and 8 Apennine sites: forest expansion dominant in both ranges at about 0.6 % per year; in the Apennines strongest at low elevations with abundant abandoned fields; distance to forest edge best predictor in Alps, elevation in Apennines. Assessment: DEEP READ; best match for the land-use driver section; findings from site sample, not wall-to-wall. — [web-search summary of repository abstract](https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/1765594) (full text not read)
- Malandra F, Vitali A, Urbinati C, Weisberg PJ, Garbarino M (2019). Patterns and drivers of forest landscape change in the Apennines range, Italy. *Regional Environmental Change* 19(7): 1973-1985. DOI 10.1007/s10113-019-01531-6. Replicate-landscape design (paired 16 km2 landscapes at 10 sites) across the Apennines, comparing land-cover change by exposure and altitude; forest expansion followed post-WWII abandonment. [Quantitative results not read]. Assessment: cite for DRIV; read full text before quoting numbers. — [Springer, via search excerpt](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10113-019-01531-6)
- Malandra F, Vitali A, Urbinati C, Garbarino M (2018). 70 years of land use/land cover changes in the Apennines (Italy): a meta-analysis. *Forests* 9(9): 551. DOI 10.3390/f9090551. Review of 51 manuscripts / 57 case studies: forest expansion +78 % and cropland -49 % (grassland decline value truncated in my read; [unverified]). Mostly central/southern Italy, 83 % local scale. Assessment: good synthesis, cite for DRIV with caution as meta-analysis of heterogeneous local studies. — [OpenAlex abstract](https://doi.org/10.3390/f9090551)
- Falcucci A, Maiorano L, Boitani L (2007). Changes in land-use/land-cover patterns in Italy and their implications for biodiversity conservation. *Landscape Ecology* 22(4): 617-631. DOI 10.1007/s10980-006-9056-4 (CrossRef lists issued 2006 online; print 2007). Three LULC maps 1960-2000: significant increase of forest, mainly in mountains, increase of artificial areas along coasts, decrease of pastures; forest birds, ungulates, carnivores increase while typical Mediterranean species decrease. Assessment: classic national citation (~730 citations) but old; cite in INTRO/DRIV, supplement with newer data. — [web-search summary](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10980-006-9056-4)
- Gasparini P, Tabacchi G (2022). Changes of Italian forests over time captured by the National Forest Inventories. In: Gasparini et al. (eds) Italian National Forest Inventory - Methods and Results of the Third Survey, Springer Tracts in Civil Engineering, pp. 545-568. DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-98678-0_13. Compares INFC2005 vs INFC2015 (same domain, classification) for area, growing stock, biomass, increment, removals; national-level results clear, regional / forest-category disaggregations often have high standard errors. Assessment: the book is already in wiki, but this specific chapter is the citable source for temporal change; the abstract warns that category-level changes (e.g. evergreen oak types) have large standard errors, which is a key caveat for validating Landsat-based trends. Exact area-change numbers not read [unverified]. — [CrossRef](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-98678-0_13)
- Malavasi M, Carranza ML, Moravec D, Cutini M (2018). Reforestation dynamics after land abandonment: a trajectory analysis in Mediterranean mountain landscapes. *Regional Environmental Change* 18(8): 2459-2469. DOI 10.1007/s10113-018-1368-9. CrossRef record verified; abstract not read, [unverified] content, study area probably central Italian Apennines. Assessment: secondary, candidate if trajectory-analysis framing is useful.

### Inferences
- Land abandonment gives a testable spatial driver (distance to forest edge, elevation, former agricultural land) for "gain zones"; the proposed Corine/land-use correlation can follow Garbarino et al. 2020.
- INFC change estimates are reliable nationally but noisy by forest type, so the paper should not use INFC as strict ground truth for evergreen-oak trends.

### Gaps
- No Italian study found that separates evergreen vs deciduous forest gain after abandonment.
- Coppice abandonment/conversion effects on holm oak: only one weak hit (Sferlazza et al. 2018, iForest 11: 344-351, DOI 10.3832/ifor2424-011, carbon storage in holm oak coppice under traditional management; relevance low). No decisive paper on how coppice abandonment favours Q. ilex over deciduous oaks was found; this remains an expert-knowledge gap.
- Tabacchi / Gasparini INFC2005 methods papers and Corine-based national statistics (ISPRA, Munafo) were not retrieved.

## Q3. Evidence of evergreen expansion into deciduous forests (Italy and analogues)

### Takeaway
Direct, quantified evidence of Q. ilex / evergreen expansion into deciduous forest in Italy is thin in what I could verify. The best analogues are Peñuelas & Boada 2003 (Catalonia) and Conedera et al. 2018 (southern Swiss Alps), both of which attribute shifts to a mix of warming and land-use cessation.

### Cited Findings
- Peñuelas J, Boada M (2003). A global change-induced biome shift in the Montseny mountains (NE Spain). *Global Change Biology* 9(2): 131-140. DOI 10.1046/j.1365-2486.2003.00566.x. Comparison of vegetation 1945 vs present: beech forest shifted up ~70 m and both beech forest and heathland being replaced by holm oak forest at 800-1400 m; isolated beech stands 30 % more defoliated, 41 % lower beech recruitment, holm oak recruitment 3 times higher. Cause: warming plus cessation of traditional land management. Assessment: DEEP READ and cite in INTRO/DISC; the best peer-reviewed analogue for the narrative; Spain, not Italy. — [Semantic Scholar abstract](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2486.2003.00566.x)
- Conedera M, Wohlgemuth T, Tanadini M, Pezzatti GB (2018). Drivers of broadleaved evergreen species spread into deciduous forests in the southern Swiss Alps. *Regional Environmental Change* 18: 425-436. DOI 10.1007/s10113-017-1212-7 (online 2017). Already in wiki (conedera_2018_drivers_evergreen). Propagule pressure and land-use legacy matter more than climate for the studied species. Assessment: caution: species are Hedera, Ilex, Prunus laurocerasus, Trachycarpus, not Quercus ilex, so cite for the principle that spread is not purely climatic.
- Walther G-R (2002). Weakening of climatic constraints with global warming and its consequences for evergreen broad-leaved species. *Folia Geobotanica* 37: 129-139. DOI 10.1007/bf02803195. Record verified in CrossRef; abstract not read, [unverified] content. Assessment: conceptual reference for laurophyllisation; likely already covered by Conedera 2018 in the wiki.
- Archive-style hits (web search): a paper on Italian oak forests states deciduous oak woods dominate the Apennines with an increase in the sclerophyllous evergreen oak component, but I could not identify or verify it. [unverified; do not cite].

### Inferences
- The planned paper can honestly claim a gap: I found no Italy-wide, spatially explicit quantification of evergreen oak gain, consistent with the stated motivation. Caveat: my search was limited to CrossRef bibliographic search, OpenAlex and a few web searches; a targeted Google Scholar search (e.g. Italian vegetation-science journals, Plant Biosystems, Forest@) is still recommended before claiming novelty.

### Gaps
- No Italian paper showing upslope or into-deciduous-forest Q. ilex expansion; no verified Sardinian / Tuscan Q. suber expansion study.
- Q. ilex SDM / future suitability papers for Italy were not found with searches used; noce_2023 (in wiki) covers 20 species but evergreen oaks should be checked there.

## Q4. Mediterranean/Italian climate trends and drought frequency (drivers)

### Takeaway
Italian warming of ~1 K per century is well documented (Brunetti 2006), with recent warming concentrated in maximum temperature; drought severity increases are linked to temperature-driven evaporative demand (Vicente-Serrano 2014 for Iberia, Mombrini 2025 for NW Italy). A good Italy-wide, SPEI-based drought trend paper was not found; this is the weakest part of the search.

### Cited Findings
- Brunetti M, Maugeri M, Monti F, Nanni T (2006). Temperature and precipitation variability in Italy in the last two centuries from homogenised instrumental time series. *International Journal of Climatology* 26(3): 345-381. DOI 10.1002/joc.1251. Positive mean temperature trend of ~1 K per century across Italy, stronger in minimum than maximum temperature over the full record but maximum stronger in the last 50 years; precipitation shows weak decrease (~5 % per century, rarely significant). Assessment: citable baseline (~600 citations) but the series ends around 2000, so it misses the 2012-2024 events; need a recent update [unverified whether one exists]. — [OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.1251)
- Mombrini E, Tamea S, Viglione A, Revelli R (2025). A 60-year drought analysis of meteorological data in the western Po River basin. *Hydrology and Earth System Sciences* 29(10): 2255-2273. DOI 10.5194/hess-29-2255-2025. Piedmont and Aosta Valley, SPI/SPEI at 3 and 12 months: widespread drying trends, SPEI shows steeper trends than SPI because of temperature increase. Assessment: only NW Italy; use in DRIV to justify SPEI over SPI. — [Semantic Scholar abstract](https://doi.org/10.5194/hess-29-2255-2025)
- Vicente-Serrano SM, Lopez-Moreno JI, Begueria S, Lorenzo-Lacruz J, Sanchez-Lorenzo A, Garcia-Ruiz JM, Azorin-Molina C, et al. (2014). Evidence of increasing drought severity caused by temperature rise in southern Europe. *Environmental Research Letters* 9(4): 044001. DOI 10.1088/1748-9326/9/4/044001. Iberian stations: drought severity increased over five decades due to higher atmospheric evaporative demand. Assessment: Iberia only; cite as general mechanism, not for Italy. — [Semantic Scholar abstract](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/9/4/044001)
- Gazol A, Camarero JJ (2022). Compound climate events increase tree drought mortality across European forests. *Science of the Total Environment* 816: 151604. DOI 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2021.151604. 143 of 310 mortality events (46 %) coincided with hot summers plus dry years; compound events not observed before the 1980s; background mortality higher where summer temperature and VPD rose and drought frequency increased. Assessment: strong for justifying "hot drought" framing and a temperature + drought-frequency predictor set. — [OpenAlex abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2021.151604)
- Carnicer J, Coll M, Ninyerola M, Pons X, Sanchez G, Penuelas J (2011). Widespread crown condition decline, food web disruption, and amplified tree mortality with increased climate change-type drought. *PNAS* 108(4): 1474-1478. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1010070108. Increased crown defoliation in southern European forests 1987-2007, parallel mortality increases in drier areas (Iberia). Assessment: general-context citation (~880 citations); not Italy-specific.
- Cramer W, Guiot J, Fader M, Garrabou J, et al. (2018). Climate change and interconnected risks to sustainable development in the Mediterranean. *Nature Climate Change* 8: 972-980. DOI 10.1038/s41558-018-0299-2. Verified record; abstract not read, [unverified] content. Assessment: high-level Mediterranean hot-spot reference (~1500 citations), cite in INTRO for context only.

### Inferences
- Use SPEI (temperature-sensitive) rather than SPI for the driver analysis, supported by Mombrini 2025 and Vicente-Serrano 2014.
- Two-factor driver design (temperature trend + drought frequency, plus compound hot-dry years) is supported by Gazol & Camarero 2022.

### Gaps
- No verified Italy-wide SPEI/drought-frequency trend paper (a press report mentions a 123-year Italian drought study, secondary source only, not verified).
- No verified Italian paper on winter-minimum temperature warming specifically (the driver typically invoked for evergreen spread).

Recommended next steps: Google Scholar searches for "leccio" Italian-language literature (Forest@, L'Italia Forestale e Montana, Sherwood) and the Italian Journal of Forest and Mountain Environments.
