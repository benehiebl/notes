## 1. Satellite/airborne treeline mapping and change detection (Landsat, Sentinel-2, aerial photos, ALS, sub-pixel, accuracy, definitions)

### Takeaway
Alps evidence splits by method: Landsat/RF or greenness-trend studies give forest densification and elevational advances on the order of tens of metres over ~40 yr, with line-position deviations of about one Landsat pixel; aerial-photo + deep learning gives the finest long-term line shifts; ALS gives vertical structure but is almost never repeated at treeline. Accuracy and shift magnitude depend strongly on the definition (forestline vs timberline vs treeline) and no paper found quantifies this sensitivity systematically; no sub-pixel method has been demonstrated at Alpine treeline.

### Cited Findings
- Menegaldo, Sforzini & Tognetti 2025, Vegetation Ecology and Diversity (online 2025), "Tree line dynamics and forest densification in the European Alps revealed by Landsat imagery". Sensor: Landsat multitemporal 1985-2023 + RF classification + manual orthophoto delineation. Area: Senales Valley, South Tyrol, 16 study areas. Result: forest cover +44% on average in all 16 areas; elevational advance in 15 of 16 areas, mean +32 m (Landsat) vs +45 m (orthophoto); manual vs RF tree lines agree within <1 Landsat pixel (30 m) in most cases; föhn/N-NW wind limits S-SE slopes. Limitation: Landsat underestimates advance vs orthophoto by ~13 m (my arithmetic from the two means); 16 sites in one valley; "tree line" here is a forest boundary. ABSTRACT-LEVEL. [doi:10.3897/ved.177152](https://doi.org/10.3897/ved.177152)
- Bolton, Coops, Hermosilla, Wulder & White 2018, Environ. Res. Lett. 13(8):084022, "Evidence of vegetation greening at alpine treeline ecotones: three decades of Landsat spectral trends informed by lidar-derived vertical structure". Sensor: Landsat annual composites (EVI) 1985-2015 + ALS transects. Area: 74 treeline ecotones, Yukon, Canada (not Alps). Result: 27.5% of pixels significant positive EVI trend, 5.6% negative; by ALS structural class greening was highest in non-treed pixels (40.8%) and shrubs (30.5%) vs sparse forest (18.9%) and open/dense forest (13.3%). Limitation (authors): EVI may not capture height growth of trees near treeline; greening mostly herbaceous/shrub, i.e. Landsat greenness does not equal treeline advance. ABSTRACT-LEVEL. [doi:10.1088/1748-9326/aad5d2](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aad5d2)
- Hooshyarkhah, Johnson, Spencer, Ryait et al. 2025, Remote Sens. 17(24):4004, "Mapping Four Decades of Treeline Ecotone Migration: Remote Sensing of Alpine Ecotone Shifts on the Eastern Slopes of the Canadian Rocky Mountains". Sensor: Landsat composites 1984-2023 + SRTM in GEE, NDVI-gradient "ATEI". Result: ATE area +13.32% (1494 to 1693 km2) but Mann-Kendall trend not significant (p>0.05); north aspects +40.2 m mean elevation gain. Limitation: area-based index, no accuracy statistic in abstract; not Alps. ABSTRACT-LEVEL. [doi:10.3390/rs17244004](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17244004)

[doi:10.1038/s41597-026-07293-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07293-1)
- Wang, Ginzler, Eben, Waser et al. 2022 (first author surname "Wang"), Remote Sens. 14(9):2135, "Assessing Changes in Mountain Treeline Ecotones over 30 Years Using CNNs and Historical Aerial Images". Sensor: black-and-white aerial images 1980 and 2010, CNN trained on three ALS-derived structure classes, active learning with 1980 samples. Result: high agreement with visual interpretation for "dense forest", lower for "group of trees"; shows 30-yr woody change can be mapped at fine resolution. Limitation: weak class for sparse tree groups, i.e. exactly the treeline class. Study area is not stated in the abstract (author list suggests Swiss WSL; area UNVERIFIED). ABSTRACT-LEVEL. [doi:10.3390/rs14092135](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14092135)

- Baglioni 2025 (Landsat forestline trend hotspots, Biogeosciences, FULL-TEXT prior), Carrieri 2026 preprint (land abandonment driver), Zhu & Woodcock 2014 and Claverie 2018: see MF.

### Inferences
- Menegaldo 2025 partly rebuts the strict "30 m cannot see 10-20 m shifts" claim in MF: a forest boundary (not individual-tree line) shifted +32 m (Landsat) and +45 m (orthophoto), above one pixel and detectable with a supervised classifier; but the 13 m Landsat bias against orthophotos supports using orthophotos as reference, and the sample (16 areas, one valley) is small.
- Landsat/VHR comparison is the only direct accuracy evidence found; there is no continent-scale accuracy assessment of an Alpine forestline product.
- The Austrian ALS (Land Tirol) literature on treeline specifically was not found (see Gaps); Austrian evidence is mostly Dullinger/Wallentin modelling and Tasser land-use (MF).
- Bolton 2018 shows a systematic limitation relevant to the Alps: greenness trends reflect understory/shrubs more than trees, so a greening hotspot map cannot be read as treeline advance without structural data.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Austrian/Tyrolean ALS CHM treeline study; Wallentin, Bebi, Ørka (treeline) and Coops (Alpine treeline) papers named in the brief were not found as treeline-RS papers (Wallentin 2008 is individual-based modelling, MF).
- Sub-pixel/fractional cover at treeline: no Alpine paper found (Copernicus TCD accuracy for sparse tree cover not found; a 2023 ISPRS Archives conference paper by Sannier et al., [doi:10.5194/isprs-archives-xlviii-m-1-2023-277-2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-archives-xlviii-m-1-2023-277-2023), addresses tree cover area estimation in Europe, content not read).
- Quantified definition sensitivity (forestline vs treeline vs tree species line) is only qualitatively addressed (Nguyen 2022, Baglioni 2025).
- Löw & Koukal full text (accuracy, elevation handling) not read.

## 2. Individual-tree and species mapping at treeline (UAV/VHR + deep learning; small-tree limits)

### Takeaway
Individual-tree detection from 5 cm UAV RGB works for larger trees but misses roughly half the juveniles (Carrieri 2025, MF); species mapping from satellite is weak, whereas aerial/UAV RGB in dense mountain/temperate forest reaches F1 ~0.6-0.9 per species. No published Alpine treeline UAV species classification was found; transfer from lowland benchmarks is unproven.

### Cited Findings
- Beloiu, Heinzmann, Rehush, Waser & others 2023, Remote Sens. 15(5):1463. Sensor: open-source aerial RGB; method Faster R-CNN; area: heterogeneous temperate forests in Switzerland (area UNVERIFIED in abstract). Result: single-species mean F1 0.76 (Norway spruce 0.86, silver fir 0.84, beech 0.75, Scots pine 0.59; pine 0.92 in multi-species model); site/stand structure matters more than illumination. Limitation: upper canopy layer only, closed forest, not treeline. ABSTRACT-LEVEL. [doi:10.3390/rs15051463](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15051463)
- Weinstein, Marconi, Bohlman, Zare & White 2019, Remote Sens. 11(11):1309: semi-supervised RetinaNet using LiDAR-based unsupervised crown delineation to pre-train an RGB detector (NEON, US); DeepForest origin. ABSTRACT-LEVEL. [doi:10.3390/rs11111309](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11111309). Cross-site follow-up: Weinstein et al. 2020, Ecol. Inform. 56:101061, works well for overstory trees across NEON forest types; performance drops for understory (abstract truncated). [doi:10.1016/j.ecoinf.2020.101061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoinf.2020.101061). - Kattenborn, Leitloff, Schiefer & Hinz 2021, ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 173:24-49, review of CNNs in vegetation remote sensing (review; numbers not read). [doi:10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2020.12.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2020.12.010). Kattenborn, Eichel & Fassnacht 2019, Sci. - Teng et al. 2025 (SAM + elevation), Carrieri 2025 (UAV treeline 67% detection, 52% for <=130 cm), Sindewald 2025 (WorldView-3 species 44-47% multi-class): see MF.

### Inferences
- The only quantified treeline small-tree detection limit remains Carrieri 2025 (52% for <=130 cm at 5 cm GSD). Species mapping at treeline is not demonstrated in the Alps; the strongest transferable benchmarks are Beloiu 2023 (F1 0.59-0.92) and Kluczek 2023 (F1 0.87-0.95), both in closed forests.
- Training data sparsity and domain shift (Weinstein cross-site; Carrieri spatial CV drop in MF) are the main risks; the expected gain from a SAM-type foundation model at treeline is untested.

### Gaps
- No Alpine treeline UAV species classification paper found (conifer larch/Swiss stone pine/spruce); no sapling (<50 cm) detection benchmark.
- Weinstein DeepForest MEE published citation not verified.

## 3. Forest drought and stress remote sensing (Alps/Central Europe), snow and phenology in mountains, topography, harmonisation

### Takeaway
Large-area Sentinel-2/Landsat studies document the 2018 drought's unprecedented forest impact in Central Europe, but they are weakly resolved in high-elevation, sparse, snow-affected stands, and no verified Alpine-treeline or Tyrol-specific Sentinel-2 drought-anomaly paper was found. Mountain snow/phenology products are available (MODIS, Landsat, Sentinel-2, webcams) but carry documented uncertainty in complex terrain.

### Cited Findings
- Brun, Psomas, Ginzler, Thuiller, Zappa & Zimmermann 2020, Glob. Change Biol. 26(12):7021-7035, "Large-scale early-wilting response of Central European forests to the 2018 extreme drought". Sensor: Sentinel-2 10 m NDVI time-series statistics linked with visually classified early wilting. Area: Central Europe. Result: early leaf-shedding across 21,500 +/- 2,800 km2, mainly Germany/Czechia; high temperature and low precipitation in August explained patterns; small-medium trees, steep slopes, shallow soils were risk factors; reduced greenness the following spring. Limitation: broadleaf early-wilting signal; Alpine conifer stands not the focus. ABSTRACT-LEVEL. [doi:10.1111/gcb.15360](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.15360)
- Buras, Rammig & Zang 2020, Biogeosciences 17(6):1655-1672. Sensor: MODIS NDVI/EVI. Result: 2018 drought larger and stronger negative impact than 2003 across Central/Northern Europe; pastures and arable more sensitive than forests; climatic dipole (cool and moist Mediterranean). Limitation: MODIS 250 m-1 km, coarse for mountain forest. ABSTRACT-LEVEL. [doi:10.5194/bg-17-1655-2020](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-17-1655-2020)

### Inferences
- Resolution mismatch: Brun 2020 detects early wilting at the scale of large crowns (10 m); treeline trees (<5 m crowns) fall below this, supporting the MF conclusion that stress detection at treeline needs UAV/aerial pixels.
- 2018 was the unprecedented year in Austria/Switzerland (Schuldt, Buras, Senf); but cool-moist Mediterranean and Alpine-high-elevation response is not resolved by these studies, so high-elevation stress claims need local evidence.
- Planet/Sentinel-2 harmonisation: literature found highlights radiometric inconsistency (Frazier 2021) and gives a fusion method (Houborg 2018) but no mountain/forest validation; treat PlanetScope-based mountain stress time series as unproven.
- Snow masking, not just correction, is the mountain-specific risk; Notarnicola and Portenier give uncertainty figures for products but nothing on melt-out detection error at 10-30 m in forest-edge shadows.

### Gaps
- No verified Sentinel-2 anomaly study for Austria/Tyrol subalpine forests (2018/2019/2022) beyond Löw & Koukal 2020; Neycken 2023 and Kloos NOT found (UNVERIFIED). No 2022 drought paper specific to the Alps found (the Buras 2022-greenness item is only an EGU abstract, [doi:10.5194/egusphere-egu23-8927](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu23-8927)).
