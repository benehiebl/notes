## 1. Measured shift rates in the Alps; check of "~0.4 m/yr over the last century" and the "Mdt, F." paper

### Takeaway
No Alps-specific "~0.4 m/yr over the last century" figure was found in any verified source; the closest are a Northern Hemisphere mean (0.354 m/yr, Lu et al. 2020, only reported via a search snippet, UNVERIFIED) and regional Alpine values that range from ~0.3 to >1 m/yr depending on method and site. ## 2. Advancing vs stable; land use vs climate; time lags; Pinus cembra specifics

### Takeaway
Globally ~half of treelines advance, ~half are stable, ~1% recede (Harsch 2009). In the Alps, land abandonment is repeatedly found to dominate (Gehrig-Fasel 2007, Marzini 2025, Tasser 2006), but some recent work attributes acceleration to climate (Nicoud 2025). Treeline response lags warming, notably for Pinus cembra.

### Cited Findings
- Marzini S., Tasser E., Wellstein C., Albrich K., Rammer W., Mina M. (2025) "Future expansion of upper forest-grassland ecotone under land-use and climate change in the Eastern Alps". Landscape Ecology 40(3): 55. DOI 10.1007/s10980-025-02070-8. VERIFIED (Crossref lists 5 authors; OpenAlex lists 6 incl. Marco Mina). iLand simulations: under abandonment, forest expands rapidly in the 21st century regardless of warming because the current ecotone is mainly land-use constrained; shifts larger on S-facing slopes; climate affects composition and stocking at higher elevations. This is a modelling study, not an observation. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10980-025-02070-8)

### Inferences
- The proposal can say: Alpine treeline advance is spatially heterogeneous and frequently confounded with land-use history (Gehrig-Fasel 2007; Cudlín 2017; Marzini 2025), while recent evidence from the French Alps points to climate-driven acceleration (Nicoud 2025). ## 5. Evidence for drought/climate-induced stagnation or retreat at Alpine treelines

### Takeaway
Direct evidence of drought-induced treeline retreat in the Alps is weak/absent in the verified set. Evidence exists for water limitation at lower elevations and for non-drought stagnation causes (winter desiccation, snow-free winters, snow fungi, dispersal/recruitment limitation). A hypothesis of "stagnation under drought" is therefore a testable but weakly supported hypothesis, and satellite evidence for it must be separated from alternatives.

### Cited Findings
- Harsch 2009: recession at only 1% of 166 global sites. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01355.x)
- Vittoz 2008: drought stress visible in the lowest stands of the treeline ecotone in the dry Central Swiss Alps, may limit growth in a warmer climate; but growth/establishment mainly temperature-linked. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1657/1523-0430(06-010)[vittoz]2.0.co;2)
- Obojes 2024: water limitation affects growth at low and mid elevation in a dry inner-Alpine valley; near the forest line species respond differently; P. cembra's temperature response at high elevation smaller than expected. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/ffgc.2024.1332941)
- Obojes N. et al. (2018) "Water stress limits transpiration and growth of European larch up to the lower subalpine belt in an inner-Alpine dry valley". New Phytol. 220(2): 460-475. DOI 10.1111/nph.15348. VERIFIED metadata (lower subalpine, not treeline). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.15348)
- Charra-Vaskou K., Charrier G., Ganthaler A., Améglio T., Mayr S. (2026) "Reduced snow cover at the alpine treeline: resistance and recovery of saplings". New Phytol. 250(3): 1492-1509. DOI 10.1111/nph.70926. VERIFIED. Snow removal experiment at 1700 m (Tyrol authors' group): snow-free saplings (incl. Larix decidua) had more winter dehydration, lower growth, mortality correlated with hydraulic damage. Experimental; shows reduced snow can harm saplings. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70926)
- Barbeito 2013 (snow fungi mortality of P. cembra plantations at treeline) DOI 10.1657/1938-4246-45.4.455: non-climatic mortality mechanism (abstract not retrieved; title verified).
- Wieser 2009: future climate extremes, not gradual warming, likely to control treeline dynamics in the Central Alps. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1051/forest/2009023)
- Frank V., Tasser E., Obojes N., Oberhuber W. (2026) "Decoupling of larch growth from temperature in the Central Eastern Alps is modulated by stand density". Eur. J. For. Res. 145(4). DOI 10.1007/s10342-026-01912-7. Metadata VERIFIED in OpenAlex only; abstract not read; potentially relevant but UNVERIFIED content.
- Non-Alpine counter-example for honest framing: Huang et al. 2023 (Himalaya) suggests increased snow cover may limit advance and even cause decline of old trees. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fecs.2023.100106)

### Inferences
- Pre-register alternatives: stagnation could reflect land-use/grazing history, dispersal limitation, wind/Foehn (Menegaldo 2025), winter desiccation or snow effects, geomorphology (Leonelli/Cudlín) rather than drought. A satellite NDVI/forest-cover signal would not discriminate among these without covariates.
- Treeline ecotone in inner Alps is mostly temperature-limited; drought is documented at lower elevations. Stating that "Alpine forest line is under stress from drought" would be overstated for the upper treeline unless supported by new data.

### Gaps
- No verified Alps study documenting drought-caused treeline retreat. No verified study quantifying treeline stagnation due to the 2018/2022 droughts at the Alpine forest line (Neycken et al. 2023 STOTEN 912: 169068, DOI 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.169068 addresses 2018 drought growth in Swiss forests, not treeline; content not checked).
