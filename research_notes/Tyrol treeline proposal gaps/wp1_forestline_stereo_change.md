## 1. What has already been done with Landsat for forest line / treeline / greening (Alps and elsewhere)

### Takeaway
Landsat in the Alps has been used for (a) greening trends, (b) forest/non-forest classification for a forest-boundary line (one valley next to Tyrol), and (c) Mann-Kendall hotspot detection along a forest line. No study I found applies LandTrendr, CCDC or BFAST to a treeline ecotone, and no sub-pixel Landsat treeline study exists in the Alps. Greening is not advance: it is dominated by infill, shrubs and observation-density artefacts.

### Cited Findings
- South Tyrol (Schnals/Senales Valley, 16 areas): Random Forest on Landsat 5 (1985) and Landsat 8 (2022) gave +32 m mean forest-boundary advance versus +45 m from manual orthophoto delineation (1982/85 vs 2023); 15 of 16 areas advanced; forest cover +44%; lines mostly agree within one 30 m pixel; the RF used 10 trees, a binary forest/non-forest scheme and the plugin's internal accuracy (about 98%, not a line-position error); climate came from a single station [[menegaldo_2025_landsat_treeline_dynamics]] [[treeline_remote_sensing_monitoring]] [W]. DOI 10.3897/ved.177152 (from wiki).
- Landsat underestimates the shift by about 13 m against orthophotos (arithmetic from the two means; one valley, 16 areas) [[menegaldo_2025_landsat_treeline_dynamics]] [W].
- Italian forest line, Landsat vegetation-index series 1984-2023, contextual Mann-Kendall test, forest line from the 10 m Copernicus Tree Cover Density layer: forest expansion mainly close to the forest line and at lower elevations; no displacement measured; GAM trend-vs-elevation not significant; no independent line accuracy (Baglioni et al. 2025, Biogeosciences 22:4349, DOI 10.5194/bg-22-4349-2025) [existing review, full text read earlier] and re-listed in [N] search hit https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/22/4349/2025/.
- French Alps (SW): greening hotspot is mostly forest dynamics, strongest where trees established in the mid-1980s, with trees established later filling gaps (increasing vertical and horizontal cover); combined Landsat, 1983/2018 aerial images, LiDAR and dendrochronology (Bayle et al. 2025, 10.1002/rse2.430). Abstract-level; no numbers retrieved [N]. Source page: https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/alpine-greening-deciphered-by-forest-stand-and-structure-dynamics-in-advancing-treelines-of-the-southwestern-european-alps
- Greening artefact: in alpine zones above 2400 m up to 50% of Landsat NDVImax greening can be explained by growing increases in the number of usable summer observations (Landsat 5/7/8 Collection 2, 1984-2021; MODIS used to simulate sampling; Theil-Sen) [[bayle_2024_landsat_greening_inflated]] [W].
- Swiss national Landsat NDVI study 1984-2018: 61% of pixels significant, 97% of those positive, apparent break near 2010; Landsat 7 excluded (SLC-off); the note explicitly finds no observation-density correction, so it is exposed to the Bayle artefact [[obuchowicz_2024_greening_switzerland]] [W].
- Non-Alpine analogues: Yukon treeline ecotones (74 sites) EVI greening highest in non-treed (40.8%) and shrub (30.5%) pixels, lowest in forest (13.3-18.9%) (Bolton et al. 2018, ERL 13:084022, 10.1088/1748-9326/aad5d2) [existing review, abstract-level]. Canadian Rockies Landsat 1984-2023: ATE area +13.3% but Mann-Kendall not significant (Hooshyarkhah et al. 2025, Remote Sens. 17:4004, 10.3390/rs17244004) [existing note, abstract-level].
- Review of 372 treeline RS papers: pixel classification, greenness trends and aerial photointerpretation dominate; sub-pixel only 1%, object-based 11%; only 19% use multi-scale multi-sensor designs; dynamics studies use about 186 m resolution over about 49 years [[garbarino_2023_treeline_remote_sensing]] [[treeline_remote_sensing_monitoring]] [W].
- Algorithms: a 2022 comparison of LandTrendr and CCDC exists (general land cover/disturbance, not treeline) and Kennedy et al. 2010 is the LandTrendr reference [N, search summary; DOIs not seen, verify]. My search found no treeline paper that uses CCDC, BFAST or LandTrendr [N, negative result].
- Sub-pixel tree cover: Schwarz & Zimmermann 2005 built GLM tree-cover continuous fields for the Alps, but at MODIS resolution [N, search summary, verify]. Landsat VCF/TCC-type products inherit overestimation at low tree cover (documented for taiga-tundra, Landsat TCC trained on MODIS VCF) and underestimation in savannas (Biogeosciences 2022, bg-19-1377-2022) [N, search summary; not Alpine; verify].
- Continental Landsat canopy products: annual 30 m tree canopy height for Europe 2001-2021, canopy extent at >=5 m, RMSE <=4 m vs ALS/GEDI calibration, extent UA/PA >=94% (Turubanova et al. 2023, RSE) [[turubanove_2023_canopy_landsat]] [W]. An RMSE of 4 m is the same order as the entire krummholz-to-tree height range at the Alpine treeline, so this product cannot resolve treeline structure. [inference]
- Sentinel-2 height product: 10 m global canopy height from GEDI+S2, aRMSE 7.3 m, aME -1.8 m balanced across height bins, 2020 snapshot only [[lang_2024_canopy_height]] [W]. Not usable for change detection and not calibrated for 0-5 m vegetation. [inference]
- Greening vs closure elsewhere: Francioni et al. 2026 is a 25-year Italian ICP-Forests understory study (canopy closure plus climate extremes drive understory loss); it is a ground plot study, not remote sensing, and its relevance is only as a densification driver [[francioni_2026_canopy_closure]] [W] (status in the wiki note: unread).
- Land-use covariate: Stubai (Tyrol) iLand simulation: forest edge near 2000 m, forest would expand quickly after grassland abandonment regardless of warming, so the current edge is mainly land-use-set [[marzini_2025_expansion_forest]] [W]. Swiss data: of new forest above 1650 m only 10% was true upward shift, 90% ingrowth (Gehrig-Fasel et al. 2007, 10.1111/j.1654-1103.2007.tb02571.x) [existing review].

### Documented sensor, pixel and harmonisation limits
- Pixel size vs rate: at 0.2-1.6 m/yr, 40 years is 8-64 m; with geolocation error and mixed pixels only the upper end is detectable [existing project notes; arithmetic]. Menegaldo shows +32 m is detectable for a forest-boundary class in one valley [[menegaldo_2025_landsat_treeline_dynamics]] [W].
- Geolocation: USGS Collection 2 re-based Landsat 8/9 ground control to the Sentinel-2 Global Reference Image, giving about 17 m CE90 for Landsat 8/9 and "usually about 10 m" for Landsat 7; Collection 2 also uses NASADEM terrain correction (https://www.usgs.gov/land-resources/nli/landsat/landsat-collection-2) [N, search summary]. I did not retrieve a figure for Landsat 4/5 TM in mountains. [verify] This matters because 1980s-90s TM scenes are the baseline epoch.
- HLS v2.0 starts April 2013 and has no Landsat 5 component, so it does not harmonise the 1984-2012 record; between-sensor VI differences of <4.5% for most indices, largest discrepancy (>20% relative) in terrain shadow (RSE 2025 and companion study, via NASA/MD-SOAR search hits) [N, search summary; verify DOI]. Consequence: TM/ETM+/OLI harmonisation for 1984-2025 must be done by the user (for example Roy et al. 2016 transforms [verify, not searched]).
- Landsat 7 SLC-off (after 2003) causes gaps; the Swiss study excluded it and so has no 2012 data [[obuchowicz_2024_greening_switzerland]] [W].
- Snow, shadow, phenology: Bayle shows that observation-density and late-snowmelt interact; high-elevation pixels are most affected [[bayle_2024_landsat_greening_inflated]] [W]. HLS shadow discrepancy above is consistent with the topographic-shadow problem. No topographic-correction benchmark for Alpine Landsat TM was retrieved [N, gap].
- Definition: Körner & Hoch 2023 (J. Biogeogr. 50:838-845, 10.1111/jbi.14593; title "Not every high-latitude or high-elevation forest edge is a treeline" [N, confirmed by search]) restrict "treeline" to thermally limited, arctic/alpine, globally comparable forest edges; a 2024 correspondence criticises the framework. Under it, the Tyrolean land-use-shaped edge is a forest line or timberline, not a "treeline". Nguyen et al. show that boundary location is highly sensitive to the forest definition (height and cover thresholds) [[nguyen_2022_forest_mapping_explainable]] [[treeline_remote_sensing_monitoring]] [W]. Recommended wording for the proposal: "forest line (closed canopy, >=5 m / >=30% cover as operational definition)" and report sensitivity to the threshold. [inference]
- Deep learning on archives: U-Net+ConvGRU over SWISSIMAGE 1946-2020, labels from 2020 only, F1c 66.7% to 80.9% on old grayscale imagery with a tCA loss; expansion is qualitative only, labels self-annotated [[nguyen_2024_treeline_monitoring]] [[treeline_remote_sensing_monitoring]] [W]. Wang et al. 2022 (Remote Sens. 14:2135, 10.3390/rs14092135) used 1980/2010 B&W aerial images with CNNs; "group of trees" class weak [existing note, abstract-level; search hit confirms] [N].

### Inferences
- A defensible Landsat product is a forest-line elevation or distance change with a stated minimum detectable shift of about one pixel (30 m) plus geolocation error, calibrated against orthophoto lines (Menegaldo's 13 m bias is the benchmark to beat). A greening product must be corrected for observation density (Bayle) and interpreted with structure data (Bolton, Bayle 2025).
- Landsat + classification should use per-year binary or fractional classes with a time-series model that is robust to sampling density. Because no LandTrendr/CCDC/BFAST treeline study was found, applying them is plausible methodology transfer, not precedent. Since the wiki's own Bayle note shows greening trends are fragile, a segmentation approach on classified forest fraction (rather than NDVImax) is safer. [inference]
- Validation against historical orthophotos is the field standard in Menegaldo and Nguyen; this is feasible in Tyrol only if the Land Tirol orthophoto archive supports it (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No Austrian (Tyrolean) peer-reviewed Landsat forest-line change study found; nearest is South Tyrol (Menegaldo, one valley, 16 areas) [existing review confirmed; my 3 targeted searches also returned nothing].
- No accuracy assessment of a Landsat TM-based line in mountains with shadow and snow; no CCDC/BFAST/LandTrendr treeline paper (low-to-medium confidence: search tools are weak for method-by-keyword).
- Landsat 5 TM geolocation in Alpine terrain not retrieved.

## 2. WorldView and other stereo / photogrammetric 3D change at treelines and in mountain forests

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed WorldView/Pléiades/SPOT stereo study of treeline canopy-height or volume change was found. The nearest evidence is Pléiades CHMs over Alpine-type mountain forest (sigma-MAD about 1-4 m), historical aerial photo DSMs for Switzerland, and boreal-alpine ALS and DAP studies. Saplings and krummholz below about 1-2 m are essentially not recoverable from satellite DSMs.

### Cited Findings
- Pléiades CHMs over mountain forest: Piermattei, Marty, Karel, Ressl, Hollaus, Ginzler, Pfeifer 2018, Remote Sens. 10(10):1542 (DOI 10.3390/RS10101542 seen in the Crossref URL). Sites: Ticino (Switzerland) and near Ljubljana (Slovenia). Narrow forward-backward angles (12 degrees) were not a large limitation, tri-stereo and stereo pairs performed similarly, a large across-track angle (19 degrees) noticeably degraded the CHM; site differences were attributed to forest structure; temporal vegetation change adds spurious differences [N, Crossref summary]. One search summary quoted median differences of 0.02 m (sigma-MAD 1.90 m) for Ticino, 0.32 m (sigma-MAD 3.79 m) for the second site, best sigma-MAD 0.80 m (forward-backward pair, Ticino) and 0.29 m; the same summary also wrongly mentioned Salzburg, so the numbers are [verify against the paper]. DTM source: ALS. Not treeline, canopy mostly >10 m. [inference on transferability]
- Small isolated objects: Loghin et al. 2020, Sensors (PMC7248834), compared Pléiades (0.70 m) and WorldView-3 (0.31 m) tri-stereo DSMs for isolated buildings, vehicles and trees; the headline finding is that DSM height depends strongly on object size [N, search summary only; page fetch was blocked; numbers not retrieved; verify].
- Pléiades CHM in plantations: bias 1.4 m, RMSE 3.1 m, tendency to underestimate heights below 20 m in young, open stands (Science of Remote Sensing, Dec 2024) [N, search summary, not Alpine, verify authors and DOI].
- Generic stereo DSM accuracy in mountains: WorldView-2 mountain control-point discrepancy 0.5-1.3 m RMS (ISPRS 2012 paper); NMAD higher over forest and steep slopes; ridge and rough-terrain errors larger for all stereo DEMs [N, search summary; verify].
- ArcticDEM tundra vegetation heights: standard deviation 0.3 m and r up to 0.8 versus LiDAR, with no bias; ArcticDEM follows canopy top in dense forest; shrub-height overestimated for deciduous shrubs and underestimated for sedges in a stereo-derived model (Meddens et al. 2018 and a MontanaView report) [N, search summary; verify]. Not Alpine.
- Shrub/pioneer tree height from point clouds at a boreal-alpine ecotone (Norway): ALS had smaller residuals than aerial and UAV DAP; DAP smoothed small solitary trees; mean vegetation height 0.64 m (ALS) vs 0.76 m (DAP); the authors warn that small systematic differences could undermine monitoring of subtle, slow change (Næsset, Gobakken, Jutras-Perreault, Ramtvedt 2021, Remote Sens. 13(13):2469, DOI 10.3390/rs13132469) [N, Crossref fetch]. This is the single most useful warning for a DSM-differencing design.
- Bi-temporal ALS at a Norwegian tree line: 316 trees measured in 2006 and 2012, heights 0.11-5.20 m, mean growth 0.19 m; height change estimated for domains of at least 1.5 ha with uncertainty (Næsset, Gobakken, McRoberts 2019, Remote Sens. 11(15):1804, DOI 10.3390/rs11151804) [N, Crossref fetch]. This gives a measured detection scale: sub-metre growth is detectable only by aggregating over hectares with a model-dependent estimator, and only with ALS.
- Other repeat-ALS treeline work: three ALS campaigns (2008, 2012, 2018) at 25 sites on the Scandinavian Mountain Range (Noordermeer et al. 2023, Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf.) and ALS calibration and change classification in northern Sweden (Nyström et al. 2012, Remote Sens. Lett., 88% OA for three classes, depends on forest density) [N, search summaries, verify].
- Historical aerial photogrammetry: Switzerland-wide DSMs and vegetation height models from about 32,000 scanned aerial images 1979-2006 (four epochs), 1 m, DSM NMAD <1 m against sealed-surface reference and independent points, closed forest median offset about 1.8 m (NMAD up to 3.77 m); NMAD >2 m above 3000 m and on slopes >45 degrees; treeline ecotone not evaluated as a target; data open on EnviDat (Marty, Piermattei, Waser, Ginzler 2025, ESSD 17:5811-5832, DOI 10.5194/essd-17-5811-2025, 10.16904/envidat.528) [N, page fetch]. Predecessors: Waser et al. 2008 (CIR aerial DSM, tree/shrub cover change 1997-2002), Véga & St-Onge 2008 (canopy growth from DSM time series) [N, via ESSD text, verify].
- Spaceborne lidar: ICESat-2 and GEDI vs ALS at western Tyrol and Vienna Woods (preliminary abstract): terrain heights fit the DTM well, ICESat-2 ATL03 better than GEDI L2A; canopy-height accuracy worse; ICESat-2 tends to underestimate canopy top [N, conference abstract; preliminary; verify]. Finnish evaluation: slope is a major factor in terrain error, canopy height errors >5 m RMSE in ATL08 [N, ISPRS 2023 archives; not Alpine]. No slope-stratified Alpine validation found.
- Dwarf-shrub encroachment (mapping): green alder (Alnus viridis) spread onto gentler slopes in Swiss aerial-photo time series (Caviezel et al. 2017, CATENA [verify]); shrublands cover 679 km2 in Switzerland, about 70% green alder (Svensk et al. 2021, Front. Ecol. Evol. [verify]); WSL/SLF has an area-wide shrub forest map project covering Alnus viridis and Pinus mugo [N, search summaries]. None uses stereo DSM change.

### Technical reasoning for volume and detection limit (my own, not from a paper)
- Canopy volume change per pixel = (DSM_t2 - DSM_t1) x pixel area, summed over the vegetated mask, after co-registration, with both DSMs referenced to a common DTM. This is a canopy envelope volume (m3 of canopy-height integral), not stem volume; biomass needs a height-to-biomass allometry per species, which at the treeline for Pinus cembra, Larix decidua, Picea abies, Pinus mugo and Alnus viridis is a separate (and not yet reviewed) literature. [inference]
- Level of detection for an individual pixel is roughly 1.96 x sqrt(sigma1^2 + sigma2^2). With sigma about 1 m per DSM (steep, forested sigma-MAD in the Piermattei and Marty numbers is 1-4 m) the per-pixel limit is about 2.8 m or worse, so the signal at the forest line (growth of saplings below 1-2 m) is below noise. The aggregated volume signal could still be detectable after spatial averaging if the error is not spatially correlated, but stereo errors are spatially correlated (slope-dependent, geometry-dependent); the ALS literature above needed hectare domains for 0.2 m changes. A defensible detection limit therefore needs an empirical stable-terrain error model (off-vegetation NMAD, binned by slope and aspect) and spatial-autocorrelation-corrected uncertainty. [inference]
- Co-registration: Nuth & Kääb 2011 (The Cryosphere 5:271-290, DOI 10.5194/tc-5-271-2011) provide an analytic method for 3D shift correction and elevation-dependent/geometry-dependent bias checks, designed for glacier thickness change with stable terrain; ICESat is recommended as a global reference [N, search summary]. In the Tyrolean forest the stable-terrain mask (bare rock, scree) is available above treeline, and the Tyrol ALS DTM (below) can serve as the vertical anchor. [inference]
- Geometry: for forest, base-to-height ratio of 0.2-0.3 (convergence 10-15 degrees) gave the best DSMs in a hemiboreal GeoEye-1 study [N, search summary, verify]; large across-track angles degraded Pléiades CHMs (19 degrees; Piermattei). Snow-free and leaf-on-equivalent acquisition: sun elevation and snow influence whether stereo DSMs capture ground or canopy in open boreal canopies [N, search summary, verify]. Alpine imagery that is snow-free in July-September has high cloud and shadow risk; steep north faces get long shadows in stereo geometry. [inference]
- Tools: Ames Stereo Pipeline, SETSM, PCI Geomatica, Metashape and OrientAL are used for stereo/tri-stereo DSMs (Piermattei compared two commercial packages and a TU Wien research tool) [N, search summary]. I did not retrieve a published head-to-head accuracy for WorldView over Alpine forest. [gap]

### Inferences
- WorldView DSM differencing can plausibly quantify: (a) canopy-gap formation and loss (storm, bark beetle) and closed-forest height growth in the closed stand below the forest line (tall, strong signal), (b) mean height change of tall dense krummholz/alder stands over decades if the signal exceeds several metres, and (c) not individual sapling establishment or recruitment. [inference, based on Næsset 2019/2021 and Piermattei]
- The ALS Tyrol DTM is both a boon and a competitor: with two statewide ALS epochs (see section 6) a 3D change analysis from ALS is more accurate than stereo, so stereo adds value mainly for epochs not covered by ALS (pre-2006 is covered by neither; stereo starts about 2009).
- Pre-2006: the only 3D source is historical aerial stereo (Tyrol 1950s-1990s; availability unverified).

### Gaps
- No published WorldView-3/Pléiades/SPOT stereo treeline change study found (medium-high confidence: 5 targeted searches returned only analogues).
- No published error budget for WorldView DSMs on steep Alpine slopes under sparse conifers; no sapling/krummholz detectability threshold from satellite stereo.
- Pléiades/WorldView numbers above need verification against the papers.
- Biomass allometry for the five treeline species/shrub types not searched.

## 3. Data sources and availability for Tyrol

### Takeaway
Tyrol has two statewide ALS campaigns and an orthophoto archive. A WorldView multi-temporal stereo archive over Tyrol is not documented in anything I found; ESA's Third Party Mission archive of WorldView stops at 2020.

### Cited Findings
- Land Tirol ALS: first statewide flights 2006-2010, updates 2010 (Ötztal and glacier areas), 2012-2013 (settled areas, North Tyrol), 2015 (East Tyrol settled areas), 2016 (small areas); second statewide campaign 2017-2023; point density generally 1-8 points/m2 and at least 8 ground points/m2 in the second campaign; DGM/DOM 50 cm GeoTIFF and 1 m XYZ free of charge; DGM/DOM are mosaics of flights from different years; first and last echo recorded; historical terrain models, classified LAS/LAZ point clouds ordered from geodatenbestellung@tirol.gv.at (processing fee quoted at EUR 86.09/hour; fee may have changed); the Laser- und Luftbildatlas shows hillshades and flight dates of current and historical DGM/DOM; no licence name and no accuracy figures on the page (https://tirol.gv.at/sicherheit/geoinformation/geodaten-tiris/laserscandaten) [N, page fetch].
- Land Tirol orthophotos: current 20 cm orthophotos updated every 3-4 years; Laser- und Luftbildatlas shows current and historical orthophotos; a WMS with historical orthophotos via data.gv.at is mentioned; a shapefile "Orthofoto Befliegungsjahre" lists years; free download by 1:2000 sheets (https://tirol.gv.at/sicherheit/geoinformation/geodaten-tiris/orthofotos) [N, search summary of page]. The years and whether 1950s-1970s imagery is held were not found. [gap; ask Land Tirol]
- ESA Third Party Missions: the WorldView ESA archive collection runs 2009-02-07 to 2020-12-01; access requires an EO sign-in and request approval (normally about 2 days); WorldView-1 to 4, GeoEye-1, QuickBird-2 archive collections opened in May 2021 under an agreement with European Space Imaging; Category-1 users in ESA/EU member states; terms version 1.7 dated January 2022 [N, search summaries of ESA pages]. Whether Tyrol footprints exist, and which are stereo pairs, was not checked and must be queried in the EO catalogue. Data after 2020 would need a commercial purchase or another route.
- Pléiades: Austria (FFG) is a partner in the Pléiades programme with a quota for institutional non-commercial use through CNES (about 2013 presentation); the FFG-funded HighSens II (2013-2014) built a Pléiades processing chain; French DINAMIS serves French institutions and European scientists [N, search summaries]. I found no current Austrian national WorldView or Pléiades licence. [gap; contact FFG/CNES/DINAMIS/Land Tirol]
- NGA Commercial Archive and Planet: not searched; do not cite. [verify]
- Existing multi-temporal stereo archive over Tyrol: none found. Possibilities (not verified): Land Tirol aerial image archive for stereo pairs with historical analogue photographs; BEV/Bundesheer products; ESA TPM WorldView 2009-2020 footprints.
- Canopy-height products: Lang et al. 2024 (10 m, 2020) [[lang_2024_canopy_height]] [W]; Turubanova 2023 (30 m annual, 2001-2021) [[turubanove_2023_canopy_landsat]] [W]; CHMv2 (DINOv3) appears as an arXiv entry (arXiv 2603.06382) [N, appeared in a search; not read; verify].
- Sentinel-2 as complement: 10 m from 2015/2017; Löw & Koukal 2020 Austrian Sentinel-2 phenology/disturbance, no treeline accuracy (Remote Sens. 12:4191, 10.3390/rs12244191) [existing review, abstract-level].

### Inferences
- A realistic Tyrol design has three 3D epochs: ALS 2006-2010, ALS 2017-2023 (volume and height change at fine accuracy) and stereo 2009-2020 (optional). Stereo would then be a validation/extension, not the core source, which changes how the proposal should justify a "WorldView" component. [inference]
- Because the ALS DGM is a mosaic of flights from different years, DSM differencing needs per-tile flight-date metadata.

### Gaps
- Years and coverage of Land Tirol historical orthophotos and aerial images; availability of the ALS first-epoch DOM (surface model) in addition to the DGM; ESA/WorldView stereo footprints over Tyrol; licence terms for research use of Land Tirol data; Maxar's current branding and commercial archive terms (not searched).

Confidence is limited by search depth (about 25 calls in this pass, plus the earlier ~15 per note).
