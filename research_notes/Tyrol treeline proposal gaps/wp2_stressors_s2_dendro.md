## 1. Which stressors are documented vs only hypothesised at the Tyrolean treeline?

### Takeaway
Documented at or near the Tyrolean treeline: thermal/nutrient growth limitation, a post-mid-1980s break of the ring-width/July-temperature link at Patscherkofel (cause unresolved), winter frost-drought embolism in Picea (not in P. cembra in one study), spring-frost/deacclimation risk (lab/in-situ physiology, mostly P. cembra), snow-removal damage in saplings of Larix/Picea at 1700 m, and drought effects in larch/spruce at a Tyrolean subalpine treeline forest under rain exclusion (Oberleitner 2022). Not documented for adult trees at the Tyrolean treeline: climate-driven browning/mortality in 2003, 2015, 2018, 2022, frost rings, freeze-thaw embolism in P. cembra/Larix, snow-fungus mortality (only Swiss plantation evidence), compound sequences. Most of the "stressor list" is hypothesis.

### Cited Findings

**Summer drought / heat**
- Larch sap flow and radial growth are water-limited up to ~1715 m in the Vinschgau dry valley (S. Tyrol); maximum growth ~2000 m; temperature response only above ~2000 m; no soil-water link at the 2000 m site; a cool wet year (2014) removed elevation differences — [[obojes_2018_water_stress_growth_larch]] (doi 10.1111/nph.15348).
- Five conifers, 24 sites, 1000–2320 m, Vinschgau: all species water-limited at low elevation incl. previous autumn; spruce most drought-sensitive at mid elevation; P. cembra's temperature response near the forest line weaker than expected; larch grew faster recently; a dry period since 2003 coincided with growth declines at low/mid elevation; authors attribute the weak forest-line temperature signal to pasture-depressed forest line (interpretation, untested) — [[obojes_2024_growth_limits_elevational]] (doi 10.3389/ffgc.2024.1332941).
- Larch in 7 paired open/closed stands (~1723 m, N and S Tyrol): May–Aug temperature positive, but after ~2000 BAI no longer follows warming, more so in dense stands; warm summers help only with July moisture; driest site (Matsch) most drought sensitive — [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]]. Caveat: subalpine ~1700 m, not treeline; management history from landowner reports; mowing/grazing vs thinning collinear (r = 0.995).
- NEW. Rain-exclusion at a subalpine treeline forest in the Austrian Alps (Larix decidua and Picea abies, 6 trees per species per treatment, 3 years): drought reduced growth from the start and the growth effect intensified with repeated drought; sap flow dropped only in the third year; spruce effect grew more than larch — Oberleitner et al. 2022, Plant Cell Environ 45:2617–2635, doi 10.1111/pce.14369 (CR; findings from the Innsbruck group web summary, not the paper). Elevation not stated on the page I read [verify; site is probably the Innsbruck/Kranebitten-type site, unconfirmed]. A 2025 IMC conference abstract (not peer reviewed) reports a longer, eight-year version with reduced sap flow and growth in treatment years. This conflicts in tone with Wieser 2019 (below) and is the most relevant adult-ish tree evidence for treeline drought; it is a 6-tree experiment, i.e. plausibility, not regional exposure.
- Saplings of Picea and Larix at the treeline: three years of top-soil rain exclusion hardly changed sap flow — Wieser et al. 2019, Forests 10:777, doi 10.3390/f10090777 (SPA/SFD; abstract read). Contradictory with Oberleitner 2022 (saplings vs trees, soil layer, outcome variable: sap flow vs growth). Do not cite either alone.
- P. cembra at treeline (Sellrain, Haggen): soil warming no effect on growth; N addition and understory removal increased growth; 33% roofing no soil-water shortage — Gruber et al. 2018, doi 10.3389/fpls.2018.00711 (SPA). Whole-tree water use of P. cembra at Patscherkofel treeline responded to soil warming (Wieser et al. 2015, doi 10.1093/treephys/tpv009; SFD, metadata only).
- NEW. Stable water-use efficiency at the Patscherkofel treeline for P. cembra, Picea, Larix: tree-ring δ13C/δ18O 1975–2010 showed roughly constant iWUE; soil water not limiting; BAI rose — Wieser et al. 2016, Front Plant Sci 7:799, doi 10.3389/fpls.2016.00799 (CR; abstract via web summary). The only stable-isotope treeline study from Tyrol I found; ends 2010, so it does not cover 2015/2018/2022.
- 2003 heat wave radial growth response at a low-elevation inner-Alpine site (Pichler and Oberhuber 2007; SFD, metadata only); Inner Alpine conifer drought swings: Affolter et al. 2010 (CR doi 10.1007/s10342-009-0327-x; Valais chronologies; colline chronologies most precipitation-sensitive — web summary). Neither is treeline.
- 2018 as a hotter drought (more extreme than 2003 in DACH): [[schuldt_2020_drought_forest]]; Alpine canopies: see Section 2 (Sturm 2022 found weaker or opposite signal at high elevation).
- Verdict: for P. cembra no verified drought-limitation at the treeline; for larch/spruce only experimental or valley-gradient evidence below ~2000 m. Hypothesis status: "drought co-limits at the upper forest line" is untested for adult P. cembra.

**Divergence and elevational growth limits**
- NEW (abstract read via PMC summary). Patscherkofel P. cembra: five chronologies, moving response functions 1866–1999; July temperature the dominant long-term stable driver, but since the mid-1980s growth at timberline and treeline departs from the July trend; authors suggest extreme events (low winter precipitation, late frost) and/or increasing drought stress, the latter supported only by a small increment increase — Oberhuber et al. 2008, Trees 22:31–40, doi 10.1007/s00468-007-0166-7 (CR). Oberhuber 2004 (Tree Physiol 24:291, doi 10.1093/treephys/24.3.291) abstract not read by me; the revised proposal already attributes the divergence to it. Pfeifer et al. 2005 (doi 10.1007/s00334-005-0001-2 CR): web summary says growth limited by cool June–Aug and previous Sep–Oct temperature and low March precipitation [secondary summary, verify]. Note: this is the best documented candidate for a "snow/frost/drought" event signal at a Tyrolean treeline and the user's Oberhuber 2004 attribution should be 2004 + 2008 (two papers).
- Frank 2026 frames larch decoupling as stand-density modulated [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]]; Märker 2025 (High Tatras spruce, not Tyrol) shows non-stationary climate response and emerging drought sensitivity in the largest size class [[maerker_2025_drought_spruce]]. Wiki note ambiguity: its static-correlation bullet reports "negative correlations with SPEI3" while the interpretation uses positive SPEI correlations as drought sensitivity; check sign convention in the paper before citing.
- Age/size dependence of climate signal (Carrer and Urbinati 2004, doi 10.1890/02-0478; SPA/MF, content not read).

**Winter stress: snow change, frost drought, freeze–thaw embolism, winter desiccation**
- Snow trends: station snow depth −8.4 %/decade (mean), −5.6 % (max, duration), strongest in spring and below 2000 m, few stations above 2000 m [[matiu_2021_snow_depth_trends]]; Austria 1 km model 1961–2020 −42 d snow cover duration but significant only below ~2000 m, no change in duration above 2000 m [[olefs_2020_snow_depth_changes]]. Consequence: a decline of snow at the treeline (1900–2300 m) is not established by these two sources; the stressor "reduced snow" is an exposure assumption. Also note both are not independent of the driving station data.
- Snow removal at 1700 m (Praxmar, Sellrain), two winters, 100 saplings: 53 vs 0 freeze–thaw events; spring PLC 77.8 % vs 31.3 %; summer mortality up in all five species; Larix high mortality with no recovery strategy; Picea resisted via refilling; P. cembra not tested — [[charra_2025_snow_treeline]]. Wiki note flags ambiguity (one vs two winters; the record I saw says Nov 2021–Oct 2022 for the measured winter).
- Winter embolism: Picea up to 100 % PLC at 2020 m (Mayr 2002), only at timberline and not in P. cembra at the same sites (Mayr 2003, Plant Physiol 131:780, doi 10.1104/pp.011452) [SFD, abstract read]; refilling in late winter via branch water uptake (Mayr 2014; SFD). Larch is leafless in winter, so needle desiccation does not apply; shoot/bud desiccation untested.
- Frost physiology: leaf frost resistance −26 to −58 °C in winter; developing shoots damaged at −4.8 °C (P. cembra), −4.4 °C (Picea), −6.3 °C (Larix); premature deacclimation shown experimentally in P. cembra; trees up to 9.3 K colder than air at night — [[neuner_2014_frost_resistance]] (narrative review, partly own/unpublished data). In-situ P. cembra dehardening: Buchner and Neuner 2011 (doi 10.1093/treephys/tpr103; SFD, metadata only).
- Deciduous safety margins: constant with elevation (Lenz 2013, doi 10.1111/nph.12452) vs decreasing (Bigler and Bugmann 2018, doi 10.1038/s41598-018-27893-1), both deciduous angiosperms, not conifers [SFD]. Spring-frost exposure increased at higher elevations in Switzerland (Vitasse 2018, doi 10.1016/j.agrformet.2017.09.005; metadata only [SFD]).
- NEW. A "false spring" in the High Tatras 2024 (11-day cold spell at −8.3 °C after exceptionally early bud development) caused needle yellowing/defoliation and reproductive failure in P. cembra — Lukasová et al. 2025, Reg Environ Change 25(4), doi 10.1007/s10113-025-02477-8 (CR, abstract partly read). Slovakia, a single event, not Alps. It is the only verified P. cembra late-frost damage record I found; it shows the mechanism but not Tyrol.
- Frost in wood: a frost during early ring formation produced a non-lignified "blue" layer in treeline conifers (Körner, Lenz and Hoch 2023, Alpine Bot 133:63, doi 10.1007/s00035-023-00293-6; SFD, abstract read). Frost rings in Alpine Larix/P. cembra: no verified study (SFD gap; my searches also found none).
- Snow fungi: Barbeito 2013 (doi 10.1657/1938-4246-45.4.455) attributes P. cembra plantation mortality at Dischma/Stillberg (~5 % survival after 30 years per web summary) to Gremmeniella abietina and Phacidium infestans; infection tracked late snowmelt (Gremmeniella) and early melt near mature trees (Phacidium). Critical point: this is NOT Herpotrichia; no verified Herpotrichia study at the Tyrolean treeline was found. Snowmelt date was the strongest predictor of mortality in the 30-year plantation (Barbeito 2012, doi 10.1890/11-0384.1) [SFD, abstract read]. NEW (not read): Probst et al. 2024, Environ Microbiome 19, doi 10.1186/s40793-024-00564-7 (CR), fungal/bacterial communities on snow-free vs snow-covered subalpine P. cembra; relevant for Tyrol sampling but I read only the title.

**Larch defoliation (budmoth)**
- Larch budmoth (Zeiraphera griseana) cyclic outbreaks ~8–9 years across the Alps, reconstructed from >5000 ring series (Büntgen et al. 2009, New Phytol 182:929, doi 10.1111/j.1469-8137.2009.02825.x CR; summary via web): synchronised Alpine-wide outbreaks have ceased in recent decades. Rozenberg et al. 2020 (Front For Glob Change 3:86, doi 10.3389/ffgc.2020.00086, CR): outbreaks shift with elevation in French Alps; a web summary says reconstruction was better with latewood density than ring width [verify in text]. Vitali et al. 2023 (Tree Physiol 43:706, doi 10.1093/treephys/tpad014 CR, abstract read): 700-year isotope chronology, 79 outbreaks, δ2H enrichment and δ18O/δ13C depletion in outbreak rings, δ2H–δ18O decoupling. Obojes 2018 reports a budmoth effect at some sites [[obojes_2018_water_stress_growth_larch]]; Frank 2026 includes outbreak years as covariate [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]].
- "Larix decemlineata": I could not identify any such organism (Leptinotarsa decemlineata is the Colorado potato beetle). Possible intended agents (larch sawfly, larch casebearer Coleophora laricella, larch needle cast) were not searched; treat as unresolved and ask the user.
- No satellite detection of larch budmoth defoliation in the Alps was found (two searches); the closest is a hyperspectral study on Erannis jacobsoni in Mongolia/China larch (Xi et al. 2021, Remote Sens 14:124, doi 10.3390/rs14010124, CR; title only) and tree-ring/isotope reconstructions.

### Inferences
- Priority-ranking by evidence (Tyrol): (1) thermal/nutrient limitation, (2) unexplained post-1980s divergence at Patscherkofel, (3) winter frost-drought in Picea, (4) spring frost/deacclimation in P. cembra (physiology only), (5) summer drought in larch/spruce below ~2000 m or under experimental exclusion, (6) reduced snow cover at treeline elevation (consequence shown in saplings; exposure trend not shown), (7) snow fungi (Swiss plantation only), (8) Herpotrichia/Alnus/frost rings (nothing verified).
- Budmoth is the one biotic stressor with a century-scale dendro signature in Alpine larch; it will confound Sentinel-2 larch signals only if an outbreak (~9-year cycle; last synchronous Alpine-wide peaks noted as ceasing) occurs in 2015–2025; no Tyrolean outbreak record for this decade was found, so check forest-health reports (BFW) [verify].
- Divergence at Patscherkofel (data to 1999) must be re-tested with post-2000 data including 2003/2015/2018/2022; this is feasible and novel.

### Gaps
- No published Tyrolean treeline study of 2003/2015/2018/2022 impacts on P. cembra/Larix/Picea (also noted in SPA/SFD). Confidence that the gap exists: moderate (Crossref and web searches only; German-language BFW/Land Tirol reports not searched).
- Kühtai, Ötztal, Gschnitz: no verified stress study. Soil frost and root uptake, P. cembra/Larix freeze–thaw vulnerability curves, Herpotrichia in Tyrol, frost rings: not found.
- Kirdyanov, Carrer, Büntgen "treeline-wide chronology" items in the brief: only Büntgen 2009 and Carrer and Urbinati 2004 were checked; others not searched.

## 2. Sentinel-2 (and Landsat, S1, ERA5-Land, SPARTACUS/SNOWGRID, Planet, UAV) for stress/disturbance/browning/phenology in sparse treeline canopies

### Takeaway
S2 works for closed-canopy disturbance and anomaly detection (Löw 2020: 83.4 % of field disturbances detected; Brun 2020: 10 m NDVI early-wilting maps), but nobody has validated it for sparse conifer canopies at the Alpine forest line. At 10 m, treeline tree islands and krummholz are mixed pixels; snow, shadow and grass phenology will dominate the signal. Alpine pixels showed no or opposite response in 2018 in Switzerland. The defensible target is stand-level (forest-line, ≥ closed-canopy patches) seasonal-anomaly and phenology metrics, with UAV as the bridge to tree level.

### Cited Findings

**Prior S2/Landsat work**
- Austria-wide S2 phenology and disturbance (Savitzky–Golay dynamic filter, TOA data, 2017–2019): 83.4 % of ~1500 field disturbances detected, 95.2 % of no-disturbance points correct; RGVI best index for conifers; only 2 years baseline; validation in northern Austria bark-beetle region; Alpine snow and shadow "not evaluated" — [[löw_2020_phenology_austria_forest]].
- Continental disturbance 2018–2020 (Landsat 30 m, ERA5-Land): unprecedented, persistent for ≥2 years, soil moisture and VPD explained 11.5 % of variance; omission 36.9 %; salvage logging inflates — [[senf_2021_impacts_2018_forests]]; context [[schuldt_2020_drought_forest]].
- NEW. Brun et al. 2020 (Glob Change Biol 26:7021, doi 10.1111/gcb.15360, CR, abstract read): 10 × 10 m S2 NDVI time-series statistics plus visual early-wilting labels; 21,500 ± 2,800 km² early leaf shedding in 2018, concentrated in Germany/Czechia; lasting reduced greenness next spring; small/medium trees, steep slopes, shallow soils risk factors. Mostly deciduous/broadleaf signal; Swiss forests less affected.
- NEW. Sturm et al. 2022 (Glob Change Biol 28:2956, doi 10.1111/gcb.16136, CR; I read a long web extract): S2 NDWI (August 2017/18/19), >1 million 10 m forest pixels, Switzerland. NDWI fell ≥10 % in 2018 in ~4.3 % of forest; ~2.7 % still declining 2019; conifers more damaged (functional type the main driver); fewer severe declines at high elevation and many alpine pixels showed NDWI increases (authors tentative); south slopes, ridges and edges less resistant; validation producer's accuracy for damaged forest ~18 % (only severe damage detected). Most relevant direct evidence that 2018 produced no obvious S2 browning signal at high elevation in the Alps; note August composites only.
- Forest-health review: 107 papers 2015–2020, Landsat dominant, mostly reactive not early-warning — [[torres_2021_forest_health_remote_sensing]].
- Landsat greening is partly sampling artefact: in >2400 m non-forest Alps up to 50 % of Landsat NDVImax greening explained by increasing observation counts — [[bayle_2024_landsat_greening_inflated]] (non-forest pixels, tree cover <5 %; forest not tested, so inflation at the forest line is unknown). Landsat Swiss greening 1984–2018 [[obuchowicz_2024_greening_switzerland]]. Forest-line Landsat trends (Baglioni 2025: tau hotspots, not displacement; forest-line reference from the 10 m Copernicus layer, no independent accuracy) per MF; Menegaldo 2025: +32 m (Landsat) vs +45 m (orthophoto) in 16 Senales sites [[menegaldo_2025_landsat_treeline_dynamics]]; Garbarino 2023 on multi-scale treeline RS (doi 10.1002/rse2.351) [[garbarino_2023_treeline_remote_sensing]].
- Tree-level UAV: Carrieri 2025, 5 cm RGB, F1 0.76 (0.68 with spatial CV), detects 52 % of trees ≤130 cm (doi 10.5194/bg-22-6393-2025) [[carrieri_2025_hr_imagery_treeline]] / MF. Planet 3 m cannot resolve juvenile crowns (MF). SIF from UAV unproven for small conifers (MF).

**Phenology and snow**
- NEW. Snow melt-out dates from 10 m S2 NDSI/fractional snow plus NDVI phenology: green-up and NDVI peak timing follow melt-out (eight S2 years, Spanish Pyrenees, grassland, preprint EGUsphere-2025-6131 [verify status/authors]); Theia snow collection operational S2/Landsat-8 snow maps (Gascoin et al. 2019, Earth Syst Sci Data 11:493–514, doi 10.5194/essd-11-493-2019, CR). Snow-season onset is poorly seen optically (cloud coincides with snowfall; CESBIO note). Barrou Dumont et al. 2025, The Cryosphere (1986–2023 melt-out record, SPOT/Landsat/S2) found via web search only [verify]. None of these addresses snow under or on conifer canopy.
- Larch phenology from MODIS 250 m in Aosta Valley (Migliavacca/Cremonese group per web summary; third-derivative method, ~6/4 days error SOS/EOS; ~7 days earlier budburst per °C) [verify authors/DOI; not retrieved].
- No Sentinel-2 study separating Larix from evergreen conifers at the Alpine forest line found. Related wiki: S2 tree species work and phenology as classification features ([[sentinel_2]], [[phenology]], [[sattstools]]). Principle (inference): larch is leafless in winter and flushes later, giving a unique NDVI/NDMI amplitude, so larch fractions drive seasonal amplitude; anomaly baselines must be stratified by larch fraction.
- Winter-phase evergreen index choice: PPI outperformed NDVI/EVI2 for evergreen conifer GPP phenology in the Copernicus phenology framework (web summary; reference [verify]).

**Climate/snow drivers**
- SPARTACUS 1 km gridded T/P (Hiebl and Frei 2016, doi 10.1007/s00704-015-1411-4 per SFD/MF), SNOWGRID-CL 1 km [[olefs_2020_snow_depth_changes]], ERA5-Land ~9 km. NEW (web summary, [verify specifics]): ERA5-Land snow bias in the Alps grows with elevation (sign differs between studies: Monteiro and Morin 2023 vs Central European evaluation); a South Tyrol study reports ERA5-Land precipitation +40 mm/month and temperature −2.1 °C bias. Implication: do not use raw ERA5-Land for treeline climate–growth without bias check against stations (e.g. Patscherkofel, Obergurgl).
- Topographic correction: C-correction best on slopes >25° in one Chinese S2 study; NDVI/SR over-corrected >15°; residual effects when DEM resolution mismatches sensor (web summaries, not Alpine primary papers) [verify]; ESA HQ-S2 project produced topographically corrected S2/L8 series for the Alps [verify]. No Alpine-forest-line evaluation found.

### Inferences
- Pixel purity: treeline sample sizes will be small. Use S2 only for closed-canopy ≥ some tree-cover threshold (the 50 % in the proposal is arbitrary, no literature basis) and report the sensitivity to the threshold.
- Likely signals detectable: (a) larch season-start/season-end shifts and spring-snow-free date; (b) severe stand-level browning or mortality; (c) multi-year anomalies in a modelled baseline using red-edge/NDMI/NIRv with Whittaker/CCDC-type smoothing. Unlikely detectable: single-tree frost-needle damage, snow-fungus patches in saplings, small embolism events.
- 2018: published S2 evidence suggests little browning at high elevation; a null S2 anomaly at the treeline in 2018/2022 is a plausible result and should be framed as informative, not as failure.
- Landsat adds 1984–2014 baseline (needed for non-stationary baseline and 2003) but observation-count inflation requires count-corrected metrics. HLS would harmonise L8/S2 [verify].
- Sentinel-1: wiki concept [[sentinel_1_sar]] only; I found no Alpine treeline S1 stress study. Wet-snow detection from S1 backscatter is established in general (not verified here) and could supply melt-out dates under cloud; mark as option, not evidence.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Austrian subalpine/treeline S2 drought-anomaly paper (also MF); no S2 larch-vs-evergreen separation at treeline; no S2 or Landsat detection of larch budmoth; no S2 evaluation of fractional snow under sparse conifers; no S2–tree-ring combination at an Alpine treeline found (see Section 3). Confidence in these gaps: moderate (about 10 web and 10 Crossref queries; Google Scholar/Scopus not available).

## 3. Combining remote sensing with tree rings; dendro methods and data

### Takeaway
No Sentinel-2 + tree-ring study at an Alpine treeline was found. Satellite–ring links elsewhere are weak or inconsistent (NDVI vs ring width mismatch in Norway spruce, D'Andrea 2022), and the temporal overlap is only 2015–2025 (10 growth seasons), too short for stable correlation statistics unless sites are many. The design should treat S2 as stand-level exposure/phenology data and rings as the response variable, using event-year contrasts (2018, 2022, 2015, 2003 via Landsat) rather than year-by-year correlation.

### Cited Findings

**S2/Landsat–ring combination evidence**
- NEW. D'Andrea et al. 2022, Forests 13:1417, doi 10.3390/f13091417 (CR): 180 trees, six lowland Norway spruce plots in Central Europe, no significant correlation of NDVI and annual ring width; the authors state NDVI is a poor indicator for low radial growth (web summary; sensor not confirmed as S2).
- NEW (web, supplement only): Urban et al. 2026 pair Landsat NDVI 2000–2025 with ring and isotope data of Scots pine near Mainz; preprint-level, not Alpine [verify].
- Landsat NDVI vs ring width inconsistent in montane evergreen forests (Wen et al. 2021, Trees 36:379, doi 10.1007/s00468-021-02211-x, CR; title only). Not Alpine.
- Mechanistic reason for mismatch (inference): rings record carbon allocation to stem over the season and previous-year effects; NDVI/NDMI record canopy greenness/water at the date of acquisition.
- Spatial scale: ring cores are individual trees (typ. 10–20 per site); a 10 m pixel contains grass, rock, shadow and several crowns; S2 geolocation and GPS error of trees in steep terrain are of the same order as pixel size [inference].

**Dendro design and methods**
- Wood anatomy of P. cembra: maximum latewood radial cell-wall thickness in a 40 µm band best reconstructed temperature, annual to multidecadal; old-growth stand Dé da Tamangur, Engadin (Switzerland), not Tyrol — Lopez-Saez et al. 2023, Sci Total Environ 855:158605, doi 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2022.158605 (CR; abstract via Bern repository). Authors state P. cembra has been little used for reconstructions because ring width and MXD give weak signals. (The proposal draft cites it for juvenile anatomy; it is old-tree evidence.)
- Blue intensity: larch BI in the Southern Rhaetian Alps (Cerrato et al. 2025, Clim Past 21:609, doi 10.5194/cp-21-609-2025 CR; abstract partly read); P. cembra ring record late spring–late summer temperature, Rhaetian Alps (Cerrato et al. 2019, Dendrochronologia 53:22, doi 10.1016/j.dendro.2018.10.010, CR, abstract not seen). I found no published blue-intensity or MXD series of P. cembra in Tyrol and no direct BI-vs-MXD test for P. cembra [moderate confidence]. BI vs MXD cross-species: Björklund 2013 (doi 10.5194/cpd-9-5227-2013, Scandinavian pine, discussion paper); Wang et al. 2020 (doi 10.1016/j.dendro.2020.125771, black spruce Canada) [titles only].
- MXD temperature sensitivity along elevation gradients is more stable than ring width (Hartl et al. 2022, Dendrochronologia 73:125958, doi 10.1016/j.dendro.2022.125958, CR, title only; species/region not read [verify]). Relevant to divergence: MXD/BI may recover a temperature signal when ring width diverges.
- Larch budmoth: latewood density reportedly better than ring width in the French Alps (Rozenberg 2020, above); isotope fingerprint (Vitali 2023).
- Tyrolean isotopes: Wieser 2016 (above); Arosio et al. 2020 (Front Earth Sci 8:523073, doi 10.3389/feart.2020.523073, CR, abstract read): larch cellulose markedly more depleted in δ2H than P. cembra (−113.4 vs −65.4 ‰; Swiss/Tyrol high-altitude Holocene samples), so species cannot be pooled in δ2H analyses.
- Pointer years and event analysis: neuwirth 2007 central European pointer years 1901–1971 (doi 10.1016/j.dendro.2006.05.004; MF), Bigler et al. 2006 Valais Scots pine drought years: single extreme drought years short-term reversible, multi-year drought initiates prolonged decline (Ecosystems 9:330, doi 10.1007/s10021-005-0126-2, CR; web summary). Vitasse et al. 2019 (doi 10.1111/gcb.14803; SFD, abstract read): 2,844 Swiss series, larch and spruce strongly and enduringly reduced by spring drought; no P. cembra.
- Legacy: growth reduced 3–4 years after drought globally (Anderegg 2015, Science 349:528, doi 10.1126/science.aab1833; SFD, abstract read); no treeline stratification.
- Age structure and recruitment: juvenile series too short for pointer-years; ring-based establishment dating needs pith-height correction (SPA/MF); Vittoz 2008 and Li and Yang 2004 for P. cembra microsites (metadata only).
- Climate predictors: SPEI (used by [[obojes_2024_growth_limits_elevational]]), VPD (Obojes 2018 modelled VPD; DRC decreased linearly with VPD), SPARTACUS/SNOWGRID; moving windows (31-year in Märker 2025).
- Open vs closed canopy: Frank 2026 paired design (open managed vs closed abandoned) is the template [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]]; Gruber 2018 shows understory competition reduces P. cembra growth.

**Existing chronologies and data access**
- NEW (ITRDB/NOAA files seen via search): Obergurgl P. cembra (AUST002, ~2000 m, 1566–1971, Giertz), Obergurgl larch (AUST004, 1604–1972), Stubai Milderaun Alm P. cembra (AUST008, 1822–1975, 1850 m, Schweingruber). Critical consequence: all end before 1976, so none covers the Sentinel-2 era or even the Landsat era; useful only as long reference/age-trend and for RCS, not for 2015–2025 events. Fresh coring is mandatory. Note two of the three ITRDB codes were labelled "PICE/LADE" in headers I saw; AUST002 header read "PICE", which conflicts with my web summary listing it as P. cembra (probably Picea) [verify species before use].
- Eastern Alpine conifer chronology (Nicolussi et al.; Kaunertal treeline history) exists [[nicolussi_2005_holocene_treeline_variability]]; whether the post-1990 treeline series are public is not confirmed [verify; likely by request to Innsbruck Dendro lab]. Oberhuber/Wieser Patscherkofel series (five P. cembra chronologies in Oberhuber 2008) are group-held; data access by request [verify].
- Regional context networks: Frank and Esper 2005 high-elevation multi-species Alpine network (doi 10.1016/j.dendro.2005.02.004; MF, metadata only). Kirdyanov (Siberia), Carrer (Italy) not searched.

### Inferences
- Recommended combination (all inferences from the above): (1) stand-level S2 anomaly metrics aggregated per site over the polygon of cored trees plus 20–30 m buffer, (2) rings from 20–30 trees per site (more for BAI/ MXD/BI), (3) compare event-year contrasts (2018, 2022 vs reference years) with permutation/mixed models, with site as random effect and use of species-stratified larch fraction as covariate, (4) validate S2 phenology (SOS from NDVI/NDMI) with phenocams and dendrometers rather than with rings, (5) isotopes as optional drought-versus-defoliation discriminator (δ18O/δ2H decoupling in outbreak years; WUE under drought) on a subsample.
- Rings answer "what was the growth response", S2 answers "what was the canopy exposed to / how did the canopy look"; a null correlation is likely and must be pre-registered as an acceptable outcome. 10 seasons imply at best |r| > ~0.63 for p < 0.05 per site (arithmetic, df = 8); multi-site pooling needed.
- Larch is the best candidate for S2–dendro linkage (deciduous, strong phenology signal, budmoth/frost/drought signatures in density/isotopes). P. cembra evergreen signal is weak at 10 m and weakly climate-sensitive in rings.

### Gaps
- No published S2 + ring combination at an Alpine treeline; no assessment of spatial mismatch between tree coordinates and 10 m pixels; no blue-intensity chronology of Tyrolean treeline P. cembra; no documented frost-ring detection protocol for Alpine conifers. Confidence: moderate-high for the first (many queries), moderate for the rest.
- Recent (2000–2025) public Tyrolean treeline chronologies unverified.

## 4. Compound / sequential stress (winter snow deficit → spring hydraulic damage → summer drought)

### Takeaway
Only one study provides a sequential link: Charra-Vaskou 2026 (saplings, 1700 m, no P. cembra), where spring PLC and cell mortality correlated with summer mortality. Interaction effects (statistical, i.e. non-additive) on growth or mortality of adult treeline conifers are untested. The draft proposal's statement that interaction is untested is correct.

### Cited Findings
- Spring PLC/PLD predicted summer mortality (adjusted efficiency 0.895 vs 0.604 for species/treatment-only model) [[charra_2025_snow_treeline]]; Larix high mortality in both snow-free and snow-covered treatments, no recovery strategy.
- Rain-exclusion repeated drought amplifies effects year on year (Oberleitner 2022, above): recurrent summer drought, not winter–summer.
- Spring drought causes enduring growth reductions in larch and spruce in Swiss trees (Vitasse 2019; SFD); summer heat × moisture interaction in larch growth [[frank_2026_larch_growth_temperature_density]]; previous-autumn water supply matters at low elevation [[obojes_2024_growth_limits_elevational]]; previous-year Aug/Sep SPEI3 effects emerging in large spruce at High Tatras treeline [[maerker_2025_drought_spruce]].
- Oberhuber 2008 proposed winter-precipitation/late-frost/drought as causes of the post-1985 divergence at Patscherkofel, never tested separately.
- Drought legacy in lowland Picea hydraulic traits (Knüver 2022, doi 10.1111/plb.13444; SFD) is lowland.
- Hotter drought concept [[drought_mortality]]; snow concept [[snow_cover_treeline]].
- NSC: no depletion at treeline (Hoch and Körner 2012; SFD) but after-drought NSC at treeline conifers untested.

### Inferences
- Testable with rings + loggers: lagged and interaction terms in mixed models of BAI/MXD on (winter snow depth or snow-cover days) × (summer SPEI/VPD of the following season) with year-lag structure, restricted to years with known snow deficits (e.g. winter 2016/17, 2021/22 [verify with SNOWGRID] ) and to larch, where the hypothesised carry-over is strongest.
- Rings cannot separate freeze–thaw embolism from other causes without anatomical (vessel-less conifer: tracheid lumen and cell-wall) or hydraulic data; wood anatomy of narrow ring cells (e.g. reduced earlywood lumen after cavitation) is hypothetical.

### Gaps
- No test of interactions in adult trees; no P. cembra snow manipulation; no 2018 vs 2022 comparison of compound sequences. High confidence.

## 5. Explicit research gaps with confidence

1. S2-detectable drought/heat browning at the Alpine forest line (2018, 2022): not published; Sturm 2022 hints at no/opposite signal at high elevation. Confidence high that no Tyrolean paper exists in the databases I could reach; moderate overall.
2. Post-2000 climate response of Tyrolean treeline P. cembra/larch including 2003–2022 extremes; divergence cause unresolved since Oberhuber 2008. Confidence moderate-high.
3. Snow-cover trend at treeline elevation (1900–2300 m): stations scarce, model trend non-significant above 2000 m; S2 melt-out dates (2015–2025) could provide a new 10 m record but cover only 10 years. Confidence high.
4. Freeze–thaw embolism and winter desiccation in P. cembra and Larix adults: not found. High.
5. Frost-ring or blue-ring detection protocols for Alpine conifers; late-frost damage after earlier phenology in Tyrol: only Lukasová 2025 (Tatras) and physiological thresholds. Moderate-high.

8. Blue-intensity/MXD for Tyrolean P. cembra; BI vs MXD comparison. Moderate.
9. Spatial mismatch quantification between trees and pixels at 10 m in steep terrain. High.
