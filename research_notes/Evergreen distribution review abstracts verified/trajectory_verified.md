# Time-series change / trajectory analysis of annual categorical or fractional forest-cover maps: verification of candidate papers and gap closing (2026-10-16)

Access note (honest): Abstracts were retrieved via OpenAlex (abstract_inverted_index), Semantic Scholar (abstract or auto-generated "tldr"), Crossref (no abstracts returned for any of these DOIs) and Google Scholar. Google Scholar via curl returned HTTP 403, but WebFetch of scholar.google.com worked; it only gives truncated ~125-character snippets, so it confirms existence/authors only. ScienceDirect (403) and nature.com (login redirect) were blocked, so for Elsevier/Nature papers without an OpenAlex abstract no verbatim abstract could be obtained. No full texts were read; no paper below is tagged FULLTEXT-PARTLY-READ. Tags: [ABSTRACT-READ] = full abstract text read; [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY] = only Scholar snippet or Semantic Scholar machine-generated summary (not the authors' words); [METADATA-ONLY] = bibliographic data only. Year note: OpenAlex lists online year for some (Olofsson 2013 listed 2012; Abercrombie 2015; Verbesselt 2009; Bogaert 2021); cite the volume year from the earlier note unless re-checked.

## A. Verification of candidate papers

### Takeaway
Of the 28 earlier candidates, 17 abstracts were read; 9 only have a snippet/auto-summary; 2 are metadata-only. Several earlier "from memory" descriptions were wrong or over-specific (notably Balzter 2000, Bogaert scale, Abercrombie scale). The papers truly worth a deep read are few (see end of section).

### Cited Findings (per paper)

**Change area / uncertainty**
- Olofsson et al. 2013, RSE 129:122-131, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2012.10.031 [METADATA-ONLY]. OpenAlex: 4 authors (Olofsson, Foody, Stehman, Woodcock); no abstract from any API. Earlier description ("adjusted areas with standard errors") remains [still unverified]. Cited ~1000 times (Semantic Scholar).
- Olofsson et al. 2014, RSE 148:42-57, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2014.02.015 [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]. Authors confirmed (Olofsson, Foody, Herold, Stehman, Woodcock, Wulder). Semantic Scholar tldr (machine summary): "provides practitioners with a set of 'good practice' recommendations for designing and implementing an accuracy assessment of a change map and estimating area based on the reference sample data." Scholar snippet starts "The remote sensing science and application communities have developed increasingly reliable, consistent, and robust". Mean-field: still the standard reference; applies to a change map vs. reference sample, not to uncertainty of 40 annual maps without reference data.
- Pontius & Lippitt 2006, CaGIS 33(2):159-171, doi:10.1559/152304006777681706 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "This paper presents methods to test whether map error can explain the observed differences between two points in time among categories of land cover in maps. ... Sensitivity analysis reveals that if the accuracy of the maps were less than 77 percent, then error could explain virtually all of the observed differences between the maps." Data: central Massachusetts, 1971 and 1999, seven categories; four transitions to "built" not explained by 15% error. Uses confusion matrices (random or stratified). Assessment: bi-temporal only; 40 maps need pairwise application and confusion matrices per date (not available historically). Concept useful as argument; deep read optional (short).
- Aldwaik & Pontius 2012, LUP 106(1):103-114, doi:10.1016/j.landurbplan.2012.02.010 [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]. Scholar snippet: "a quantitative method to analyze maps of land categories from several points in time for a single site"; three levels interval, category, transition, tests stationarity; case: seven categories, Plum Island Ecosystems, Massachusetts (summary by fetch tool, not verbatim). Fits the user's categorical multi-date maps well (transition-level targeting; stationarity of rates).
- Stehman & Foody 2019, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2019.05.018 [METADATA-ONLY, not re-fetched this round].

**Annual-map temporal consistency (HMM / post-processing)**
- Hermosilla et al. 2018, CJRS 44(1):67-87, doi:10.1080/07038992.2018.1437719 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "Post-processing aimed to reduce spurious class transitions is undertaken integrating change information, land cover transition likelihoods, and year-on-year class membership likelihoods. Validation was assessed for a single year (2005) using independent data for an overall accuracy of 70.3% (± 2.5%)." Data: Canada, 650 million ha forested ecosystems, annual BAP composites 1984-2012 (29 years), Virtual Land Cover Engine (VLCE). Key caveats for the user: accuracy validated for one year only; OA 70.3% means plenty of class error; they note "general stability of land cover over time". Closest precedent for the workflow; deep read.
- Abercrombie & Friedl 2016, IEEE TGRS 54(2):703-713, doi:10.1109/TGRS.2015.2463689 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "Much of this apparent land cover change arises from errors in classification and does not indicate real change in land cover or land use." HMM applied "as a postprocessing step to supervised classification"; evaluated on MODIS MCD12Q1 (Collection 5.1); abstract states coarse resolution "(i.e., 300-500 m)" as the problem setting. Correction to earlier note: operates on MODIS 500 m labels, not Landsat; accuracy numbers not in abstract. Deep read for the HMM framing.
- Bogaert, Lamarche & Defourny 2022 (OpenAlex dates the record 2021), IEEE TGRS 60, doi:10.1109/TGRS.2021.3123738 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Given names confirmed via OpenAlex: Patrick Bogaert, Celine Lamarche, Pierre Defourny. "HMM's allow filtering out errors and recovering the actual sequence of LCLUC, which are typically overestimated when directly estimated from the classified images." Data: three 300-m annual series 2003-2019, 756 x 756 km areas in Brazil, China, Mali; Viterbi training; handles cloud-caused missing observations; feasible "as long as the legend ... involves a reasonable number of classes like the six main IPCC land categories". Correction: 300 m, six classes; the user has 30 m, many species classes, so the emission/transition matrix will be larger and sparse. Deep read (most transferable on estimating matrices from the maps themselves).
- Gong, Fang, Yang & Ge 2017, ISPRS IJGI 6(10):292, doi:10.3390/ijgi6100292 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Full names: Wenbing Gong, Shenghui Fang, Guang Yang, Mengyu Ge. "HMM that incorporated information from spatial and temporal neighboring observations to improve the time series land cover maps initially produced by a support vector machine (SVM)"; Landsat and HJ images; spatial weights "could eliminate the vast majority of illogical land cover transition". No accuracy numbers in abstract; small case study. Skim only; open-access, cite as a minor example of spatio-temporal HMM.
- Tsendbazar et al. 2021, RSE 266:112686 (volume not re-verified), doi:10.1016/j.rse.2021.112686 [ABSTRACT-READ] (new): framework for validating annual global land cover maps incl. "stability in map accuracy" and temporal consistency; applied to CGLS-LC100 2015-2019. Useful for argument that accuracy stability over years must be reported.

**Trajectory / segmentation algorithms**
- Kennedy, Yang & Cohen 2010, RSE 114(12):2897-2910, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2010.07.008 [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]. Semantic Scholar tldr (machine): "LandTrendr ... a new approach to extract spectral trajectories of land surface change from yearly Landsat time-series stacks, appears to be a feasible and robust means of increasing information extraction from the Landsat archive." Actual abstract not obtained. Foundation; applicable to a per-pixel fraction series only as an index; not designed for categorical series.
- Kennedy et al. 2018, Remote Sens 10(5):691, doi:10.3390/rs10050691 [ABSTRACT-READ]. LT-GEE vs LT-IDL "agreed in most cases"; six U.S. study areas; "translation ... faithful". Pure software paper; skim only, cite when using LT-GEE. Authors per OpenAlex (7 listed incl. Yang, Gorelick, Braaten, Cavalcante, Cohen, Healey; more not displayed).
- Pasquarella et al. 2022, JAG 110:102806, doi:10.1016/j.jag.2022.102806 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "We review two temporal segmentation approaches ... LandTrendr ... and CCDC ... We examine differences in the way these approaches use the temporal and spectral domains and compare model specifications and outputs." It is a review/comparison, with no accuracy numbers; earlier description "shows how parameter choices change outputs" is an over-claim, abstract says compare specifications and outputs. Worth reading for choosing between LT and CCDC (short).
- Zhu & Woodcock 2014, RSE 144:152-171, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2014.01.011 [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]. Scholar snippet: "A new algorithm for Continuous Change Detection and Classification (CCDC) of land cover using all available Landsat data". Semantic Scholar tldr (machine): "producer's accuracy of 98% and user's accuracies of 86% in the spatial domain and temporal accuracy of 80%" for change detection (numbers belong to the original study area; not verbatim author text). Skim; operates on spectral observations, not maps.
- Cohen et al. 2017, Forests 8(4):98, doi:10.3390/f8040098 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "seven different algorithms ... six different Landsat scenes ... conterminous US. The maps agreed very well in terms of the amount of undisturbed forest ... for the ~30% of forest mapped as disturbed in a given year by at least one algorithm, there was little agreement about which pixels were affected." Disturbance (not cover-type) context; supports sensitivity tests. Skim; open access.
- Verbesselt et al. 2010, RSE 114(1):106-115, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2009.08.014 [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]. Scholar snippet only: "A wealth of remotely sensed image time series covering large areas is now available to the earth science community." Content beyond that [still unverified]. Spectral series, not maps; cite as background only.
- Kennedy et al. 2014, Front Ecol Environ 12(6):339-346, doi:10.1890/130066 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "this ecologically relevant perspective of change allows the novel characterization of important dynamic processes, including disturbances, long-term trends, cyclical functions, and feedbacks". Conceptual; 22 authors; cite for framing, not method.
- Hermosilla et al. 2015, RSE 170:121-132, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2015.09.004 [METADATA-ONLY]; no abstract retrievable; earlier description [still unverified].
- Zhu 2017, ISPRS 130:370-384 [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]: Semantic Scholar tldr says recent studies use higher Landsat frequency and algorithms discussed include "thresholding, differencing, segmentation, trajectory classification, and regression". Woodcock et al. 2020, RSE 238:111558 [ABSTRACT-READ via Semantic Scholar]: "a paradigm shift away from change detection, typically using two points in time, to monitoring". Both background citations.
- Song et al. 2018, Nature 560:639-643, doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0411-9 [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]. Scholar snippet: "Land change is a cause and consequence of global environmental change". Semantic Scholar tldr: satellite data for 1982-2016 reveal change patterns "indicative of a human-dominated Earth system". Details (AVHRR, fractional cover, sample-based area) [still unverified].

**Trend tests / hotspots**
- Hamed & Rao 1998, J Hydrol 204(1-4):182-196, doi:10.1016/S0022-1694(97)00125-X [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]. Scholar snippet: "One of the commonly used tools for detecting changes in climatic and hydrologic time series is trend analysis." Rest [still unverified]; very widely cited (~3500).
- Yue et al. 2002, Hydrol Process 16(9):1807-1829, doi:10.1002/hyp.1095 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "the existence of serial correlation alters the variance of the estimate of the Mann-Kendall (MK) statistic ... the commonly used pre-whitening procedure ... leads to potentially inaccurate assessments of the significance of a trend." Monte Carlo AR(1) simulation; streamflow in Ontario; recommends removing trend before estimating autocorrelation. Earlier summary correct.
- Neeti & Eastman 2011, Trans GIS 15(5):599-611, doi:10.1111/j.1467-9671.2011.01280.x [ABSTRACT-READ]. CMK "uses the principle of spatial autocorrelation ... The procedure removes serial correlation through a prewhitening process." Mean annual NDVI 22 years (1982-2003), West Africa: "~11% of the study area showed significant (p < 0.001) trends which increased to 16%" with CMK. Designed for NDVI with cloud contamination; not categorical data.
- Getis & Ord 1992, Geogr Anal 24(3):189-206, doi:10.1111/j.1538-4632.1992.tb00261.x [ABSTRACT-READ]. "Gi and Gi* statistics enable us to detect local 'pockets' of dependence that may not show up when using global statistics." Examples: SIDS counties North Carolina, San Diego housing prices. Skim; cite once.

**Space-for-time / forecasting**
- Blois et al. 2013, PNAS 110(23):9374-9379, doi:10.1073/pnas.1220228110 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "Predictions relying on space-for-time substitution were ~72% as accurate as 'time-for-time' predictions. However, space-for-time substitution performed poorly during the Holocene when temporal variation in climate was small relative to spatial variation." Late Quaternary pollen, eastern North America; conclusion supports "judicious use". Earlier summary ("fails under no-analog climates") was not stated in abstract; corrected. Read (short, directly relevant).
- Damgaard 2019, TREE 34(5):416-421, doi:10.1016/j.tree.2019.01.013 [ABSTRACT-READ via Semantic Scholar]. "this practice is problematic, especially in nonstationary environments. ... it is more judicious to report the observed spatial patterns and only discuss which ecological processes are in concordance with the observed spatial pattern." Opinion/critique; skim.
- Balzter 2000, Ecol Model 126(2-3):139-154, doi:10.1016/S0304-3800(00)00262-3 [ABSTRACT-READ via Semantic Scholar]. "An overview of 22 applications of Markov chain models is presented, using data from four sources examining different grassland communities ... time steps of one year are preferable to three months. The rank order of dominant species is found to be the most reliable prediction achievable". CORRECTION: earlier claim that it introduces non-stationary/covariate-dependent/spatial extensions is NOT supported by the abstract; it is a grassland, stationary-model assessment. Useful for one result: annual time step and rank order of dominants most predictable. Not a forest/climate-covariate anchor.
- McClintock et al. 2020, Ecol Lett 23(11):1706-?, doi:10.1111/ele.13610 [ABSTRACT-READ] (new): tutorial/review of HMMs in ecology: "formally disentangling state and observation processes"; ecosystem states in scope but most applications are animal movement. Page range not verified.
- Elith & Leathwick 2009 [METADATA-ONLY, not re-fetched].
- Dormann et al. 2007, Ecography 30(5):609-628, doi:10.1111/j.2007.0906-7590.05171.x [ABSTRACT-READ]. "The violation of the assumption of i.i.d. residuals may bias parameter estimates and can increase type I error rates"; compares six approaches with simulated data; (17 authors). Beale et al. 2010, Ecol Lett 13(2):246-264, doi:10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01422.x [ABSTRACT-READ]: simulation; "generalized least squares family of models and a Bayesian implementation of the conditional auto-regressive model" performed well, OLS "poorly controlled Type I error"; "Removing large-scale spatial trends in the response led to poor performance." Both are about regression with spatial autocorrelation, not spatial CV (see B7).

## B. Gap-closing searches

### Takeaway
No paper was found that does what the user plans (trajectory/hotspot analysis of 40 annual 30 m species-dominance maps with HMM forecasting using climate covariates); the nearest verified precedents are listed per gap. Tool limits: OpenAlex relevance search (primary); Google Scholar only via WebFetch, snippets only (one query tried, returned unrelated trajectory/biomass papers, nothing new that was needed).

### Cited Findings

**(1) Trajectory/change of annual tree-species or forest-type maps with Landsat**
- Padial-Iglesias, Ninyerola, Serra, Gonzalez-Guerrero, Espelta 2022, Forests 13(3):475, doi:10.3390/f13030475 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "A rigorous map production at 30 m resolution, including detailed filtering methods and uncertainty management at pixel scale"; Iberian Peninsula 185 km-wide transect, 1987-2002-2017, 30 explanatory variables; boosted regression trees; for broadleaf evergreen forests drivers were "precipitation, temperature and solar radiation"; proximity to previous forests key for all types. HIGHEST relevance (evergreen broadleaf expansion + drivers + 30 m maps + filtering), but only three time points and land-cover classes, not species. Deep read; check how filtering and uncertainty were done.
- Thompson, Nelson, White, Wulder 2015, CJRS, doi:10.1080/07038992.2015.1065708 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Six tree species, Saskatchewan >39 million ha, 30 m, multiyear Best-Available-Pixel composite; "the BAP composite was found to have little effect on model outcomes" (single-date species map, not time series); relevant to composite-date heterogeneity. Skim.
- Mancino et al. 2013, iForest, doi:10.3832/ifor0909-007 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Basilicata (southern Italy) 1984-2010, two-date NDVI differencing; 91.8% accuracy for forest expansion; forest cover +19.7% (70154 ha), consistent with National Forest Inventory; "considerable forest expansion also occurred on degraded soils in drought-prone Mediterranean areas". Italian regional anchor; bi-temporal only.
- Rejected: Hauser et al. 2020 (mangroves) is on point methodologically (post-classification temporal optimization, 94-96% spatial and 87-92% temporal accuracy, mangrove hotspots) but is a small tropical study; keep as optional example of temporal gap-filling [ABSTRACT-READ, doi:10.3390/rs12223729].

**(2) Mann-Kendall / Theil-Sen / hotspot on categorical or fractional series**
- Aubard, Paulo, Silva 2019, Remote Sens 11(5):525, doi:10.3390/rs11050525 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Landsat NDVI 1984-2017 at 30 m for stable cork and holm oak stands in Portugal; "Mann Kendall and Contextual Mann-Kendall test ... Theil-Sen estimator"; validated against forest inventories and MODIS; July-August NDVI to focus on the tree layer. Directly on Quercus ilex with exactly the MK/CMK/Theil-Sen toolbox, but spectral index in stable land cover, not maps. Read.
- Forkel et al. 2013, Remote Sens 5(5):2113, doi:10.3390/rs5052113 [ABSTRACT-READ]. "performance decreases with increasing inter-annual variability"; "an overestimation of breakpoints ... can result in wrong or even opposite trend estimates". Useful caution when using LandTrendr/breaks. Skim.
- No paper found applying MK/Theil-Sen/Getis-Ord to categorical annual species maps; the hotspot hits in OpenAlex were low-quality regional LULC case studies (e.g., Majuli island, Kolkata, Jharia); rejected.

**(3) Markov / HMM / transition forecasting of forest composition with climate covariates**
- No verified paper using HMM with climate covariates on forest species dominance was found. Markov hits: Sustainability 2016 (Markov-CA forest land-use, doi:10.3390/su8030236) and Forests 2021 Pacific Northwest succession (doi:10.3390/f12121770): [METADATA/ABSTRACT-SNIPPET only], regional case studies; not recommended as anchors. McClintock 2020 and Balzter 2000 above remain the best generic anchors. Thom & Seidl 2021 (Ecosystems, doi:10.1007/s10021-021-00674-0) [ABSTRACT-READ]: 28 years, 3759 plots, BRT on 30 covariates, "forest change accelerated with increasing temperature" but "Legacies and climate were the most important drivers" with diverging impacts; field-plot analogue for driver attribution, not maps.

**(4) Uncertainty of change from classified maps / temporal filtering**
- Verified: Pontius & Lippitt 2006, Olofsson 2014, Abercrombie 2016, Bogaert 2022, Hermosilla 2018, Gong 2017, Tsendbazar 2021 (above). No additional paper found for annual species-dominance maps.

**(5) Observation-density effects on classified Landsat maps**
- Nothing new and on point. Search returned off-topic items (Landsat 7-8 NDVI continuity, Roy et al. 2016 doi:10.1016/j.rse.2015.12.024 [ABSTRACT-READ]: reflective band/NDVI continuity between sensors; relevant to sensor harmonisation only). Gap remains; Bayle 2024 stays the main reference.

**(6) LandTrendr/CCDC for evergreen or Mediterranean forest**
- Viana-Soto et al. 2020, Remote Sens 12(9):1499, doi:10.3390/rs12091499 [ABSTRACT-READ]. LandTrendr on Tasseled Cap Wetness/Angle 1994-2018, two large fires in Spanish Mediterranean pine forests; K-means clustering of recovery ratios into four trajectory classes; GWR: recovery "highly sensitive to post-fire climate in the mid and long-term". Good trajectory-typology + driver precedent (disturbance recovery, not species change). Read.
- Giannetti et al. 2020, Remote Sens 12(22):3720, doi:10.3390/rs12223720 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Mediterranean coppice clearcuts 2001-2013; LandTrendr and new TTM overall accuracy >0.94; Global Forest Change producer's accuracy 0.00-0.38. Shows GFC misses Mediterranean coppice disturbance; evergreen oak coppice in Italy (Tuscany authors). Skim/cite.
- Khoury & Coomes 2020, Glob Change Biol, doi:10.1111/gcb.15268 [ABSTRACT-READ]: 18-year NDVI of 3,100 forest pixels in ten Spanish forest types vs SPEI; drought-tolerant species show weak covariance with SPEI. Cite for drought-response by forest type.
- No LandTrendr/CCDC study on holm-oak (Q. ilex) dieback in Italy found in OpenAlex; Oak decline in southern Italy 2025 (doi:10.3897/ved.160170) [SNIPPET only, field/climate parameters] not read.

**(7) ML attribution of vegetation change to climate and land-use drivers with spatial CV**
- Padial-Iglesias 2022 and Thom & Seidl 2021 (BRT, above) are the only relevant verified examples; neither is described as using spatial cross-validation in the abstract. Dormann 2007 and Beale 2010 give regression-side guidance. No verified paper pairing SHAP/ML attribution with spatial CV for forest-cover change was found; hits were irrelevant (Iraq, South Asia temperature; rejected). Gap.

### Inferences
- The user's design could cite: Padial-Iglesias 2022 (driver analysis of broadleaf evergreen expansion), Hermosilla 2018 + Bogaert 2022 (temporal filtering), Aubard 2019 (MK/CMK/Theil-Sen on Q. ilex), Viana-Soto 2020 (trajectory clustering + climate drivers).
- Because Bogaert/Abercrombie HMMs are used for filtering, forecasting with climate covariates would be a novel extension that must be justified and hindcast-validated by the user; no precedent found (state this as a gap, not as established).

### Gaps
- No Italian Landsat species-level trajectory study found; no observation-density paper for classified maps; no HMM-with-climate forest forecasting precedent; no ML+spatial-CV attribution precedent.
- Rejected (reasons): CA-Markov/regional LULC and hotspot case studies (Majuli, Kolkata, Jharia, Nyungwe: weak methods, not forest-type or not peer-reviewed quality); Iraq/South Asia SHAP papers (off-topic); Landsat ET/crop/urban hits (off-topic); 2026 Scientific Reports "forest disturbance-agent attribution" (doi:10.1038/s41598-026-72550-7) is a theoretical framework, not read, not relevant to cover maps.
