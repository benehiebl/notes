- Q. ilex is ecologically a different EVE from laurophylls: in Insubria it sits on dry calcareous sites (Berger & Walther 2006, dissertation text), whereas laurophylls (Laurus, Ilex, Cinnamomum, Prunus laurocerasus, Trachycarpus) track moist, shaded sites. A single "EVE" class conflates these niches (consistent with the stratification advice in the report).

- Niinemets & Valladares 2006, "Tolerance to shade, drought, and waterlogging of temperate Northern Hemisphere trees and shrubs", Ecol Monogr 76(4):521-547, DOI 10.1890/0012-9615(2006)076[0521:TTSDAW]2.0.CO;2 [ABSTRACT-READ, OpenAlex; page numbers from my knowledge are not verified, take them from CrossRef before citing]. ### (3) CO2 effect on evergreen vs deciduous

#### Takeaway
There is a concrete mechanistic literature: Niinemets, Flexas & Peñuelas 2011 (hypothesis) and Soh et al. 2019 (empirical iWUE); but no study tests it on EVE spread in Europe.

#### Cited Findings
- Niinemets, Flexas & Peñuelas 2011, "Evergreens favored by higher responsiveness to increased CO2", Trends Ecol Evol 26(3):136-142, DOI 10.1016/j.tree.2010.12.012 [ABSTRACT-READ, Europe PMC]. "...photosynthesis and the intrinsic efficiency of the use of plant water responds more strongly to elevated levels of CO2 in leaves with more robust structures. This provides an additional explanation for the current apparent expansion of evergreen sclerophylls in many Earth ecosystems..." A review/hypothesis paper (not a test). Worth reading; directly addresses the CO2 driver named by Alessi 2021.
- Soh, Yiotis, Murray & Parnell 2019, "Rising CO2 drives divergence in water use efficiency of evergreen and deciduous plants", Sci Adv 5(12):eaax7906, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aax7906 [ABSTRACT-VIA-SEARCH + CrossRef teaser: "A rapid rise in CO2 has made evergreen trees more waterwise than deciduous trees, especially in cooler parts of the world."]. Per search summary: 244 woody species, eight biomes, about 25 years of CO2 rise, evergreen iWUE rose faster than deciduous, difference larger toward boreal. Worth reading; iWUE is not growth or range expansion.
- Contrary evidence: a palaeobotanical experiment (Cambridge/Paleobiology 2005, "Contrasting seasonal patterns of carbon gain in evergreen and deciduous trees of ancient polar forests") found deciduous trees reached similar annual carbon budgets under elevated CO2 [ABSTRACT-VIA-SEARCH only, not read; polar forests, not relevant for Italy]. Treat as unverified.
- Walther 2007 and Berger 2007 attribute expansion to winter temperature; the Alessi 2021 abstract lists "increase in atmospheric CO2" among suggested drivers, with no test. 

#### Inferences
- CO2 is a plausible but untested driver; a correlative trajectory analysis cannot separate it from warming.

#### Gaps
- No European field or modelling study partitioning CO2 vs warming for EVE spread found (Hickler 2012 abstract does not report this).

### (4) North-Italian sites (Insubrian lakes, Garda, Euganean Hills, Liguria, Friuli)

#### Takeaway
Insubria (including Lago di Garda) is covered by Berger & Walther 2006 (22 sites, Lago d'Orta to Garda) and Fehr & Burga 2016; Euganean Hills, Liguria and Friuli yielded no change study.

#### Cited Findings
- Berger & Walther 2006: see Q1; Garda is the eastern end; Q. ilex dominates the eastern, drier, calcareous sites. The dissertation text cites Brullo & Guarino (1998) for an overview of Laurus, Ilex aquifolium and Q. ilex distribution around Lago di Garda [reference seen in the dissertation, paper not retrieved].
- Insubria review: "Forest ecosystems and global change: the case study of Insubria", Annali di Botanica 2013, DOI 10.4462/annbotrm-10092 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Mentions rural abandonment and invasive exotic species and the need for "reliable long-term bio-indicators" and "to predict the shift of Insubric species". No EVE numbers. Skim only.
- Applied Vegetation Science 2024, DOI 10.1111/avsc.12765 (Trachycarpus impacts on deciduous forests on the southern slope of the Alps, ten sites, Swiss-Italian border) [ABSTRACT-READ]: reduced native richness in mesic-moist forests with high palm density; refers to the "ongoing and inevitable 'laurophyllisation'". Also Biol Invasions 2022 (DOI 10.1007/s10530-022-02874-8), 200 individuals, 21 populations in Ticino, Lombardy, Piedmont, "the expansion of T. fortunei is active" at the colonisation front [ABSTRACT-READ]; Global Ecol Conserv 2024 (DOI 10.1016/j.gecco.2024.e03303) on arthropods and litter decomposition [ABSTRACT-READ]; Forest Ecol Manage 2024 (DOI 10.1016/j.foreco.2024.121743) "Invasive palms have more efficient and prolonged CO2 assimilation compared to native sub-Mediterranean vegetation": T. fortunei had "similar assimilation rates" to competitors year-round, higher Amax and Anet in October when deciduous leaves senesce [ABSTRACT-READ]. These are recent impacts/physiology papers on one alien palm in Insubria; useful for the "winter carbon gain" mechanism, but author lists were not verified here (only titles/DOIs from OpenAlex).
- Liguria: Barberis et al. 1992 "Notes on Quercus ilex L. in Liguria (NW Italy)" (DOI 10.1007/978-94-017-2836-2_3): WebSearch summary states Q. ilex is absent from early succession and appears later, often under old pine cover [ABSTRACT-VIA-SEARCH; 1992]. Not about recent expansion.
- Friuli/Trieste: Annali di Botanica article "Climatic factors and establishment of Quercus ilex communities in Trieste province (NE Italy)" (rosa.uniroma1.it/rosa04/annali_di_botanica/article/view/9172) [METADATA-ONLY via search; authors/year/DOI not verified]. Describes Q. ilex-hornbeam stands near Duino where deciduous oaks otherwise dominate; not an expansion study.
- Euganean Hills and Garda: no study of change found (Google Scholar snippets and WebSearch).

#### Gaps
- All North Italian sites except Insubria lack temporal data.

### (5) Observed EVE expansion rates anywhere in Europe

#### Takeaway
Only a handful of real numbers exist: Delzon (Q. ilex lateral spread 22-57 m/yr), Perring 2020 (ivy occurrence +14% per site), Kucharski 2019 (ivy fruiting stands almost 10-fold), Carraro 1999 (130 relevés; no rate given in abstract).

#### Cited Findings
- Delzon et al. 2013: see Q1.
- Perring, De Frenne, Hertzog, Blondeel et al. 2020, "Increasing liana frequency in temperate European forest understories is driven by ivy", Front Ecol Environ 18(10):550-557, DOI 10.1002/fee.2266 [ABSTRACT-READ, CrossRef]. "...resurvey database of 1814 (quasi-)permanent plots from across 40 European study sites, with a median between-survey interval of 38 years..." "Ivy (Hedera helix) is largely responsible for driving this increase ..., as its proportional occurrence has grown by an average of 14% per site." "Enhanced warming rates, increased shade, and historical management transitions explain only some of the variation." The best Europe-wide quantified expansion of one evergreen broadleaf; an evergreen liana. Include. A follow-up commentary exists ("Lianification or liana invasion - is there a difference?", Front Ecol Environ 19:377-378, DOI 10.1002/fee.2393) [METADATA-ONLY].
- Kucharski et al. 2019, Folia Forestalia Polonica 61(3):211-221, DOI 10.2478/ffp-2019-0020 [ABSTRACT-READ, CrossRef]. "474 stands of naturally growing Hedera have recently been identified. Ivy was found to reproduce generatively on 121 of those locations. There has been an almost 10-fold increase in the number of fruiting ivy specimens since the mid-1970s"; attributed to temperature and solar radiation. Central Poland, near the eastern limit; one species; local. Minor.
- Abrahamczyk 2024: P. laurocerasus in 19 of 24 transects, mostly established since 2000 (Q1 item 18).
- Q. ilex in France: after the 1950s, forestry stopped systematic felling and colonisation accelerated (WebSearch summary of Delzon et al.; the abstract itself does not say this; [still unverified]).
- Montseny: holm oak recruitment three times higher than beech (Peñuelas & Boada 2003).

#### Gaps
- No Europe-wide quantified rate for sclerophyllous Q. ilex or laurophylls such as Laurus/Ilex area expansion.
