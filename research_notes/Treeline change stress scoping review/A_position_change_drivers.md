## Q1. Concept vocabulary and best definitional sources

### Takeaway
There is no single source that defines all terms. The strongest conceptual frame is Körner and Hoch 2023 (potential climatic treeline = fundamental niche edge of the life form tree; all deviations = realized niche edge), challenged by Malanson 2023 as too narrow. Operational thresholds (tree height, canopy cover) are not given in any abstract I read. Boundary type and threshold must be stated per study.

### Cited Findings
- Treeline (strict, climatic) vs other forest edges: "Founded in the ecological niche concept, the application of the fundamental niche edge to treeline permits defining the potential climatic limit of tree growth, while the realized niche edge captures all deviations for reasons related to other, more local, abiotic factors, biotic interactions, disturbances and human interventions." — Körner & Hoch 2023, J Biogeogr 50(5):838-845, doi:10.1111/jbi.14593 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Note: the abstract does not use the words "forest line" or "timberline"; the proposal's sentence that the treeline "must not be confused with forest line or timberline" is therefore from the full text (unread here) or from the earlier wiki note.
- Contested definition: Malanson 2023 argues the Körner-Hoch definition "restricts treeline and the treeline ecotone to narrow but interesting ecophysiological questions", leaves "the extensive zone of krummholz and tree-species seedlings now often referred to as the treeline ecotone ... without terminology", and proposes defining "alpine treeline" as "a zone or line within the broader alpine treeline ecotone" — Malanson G (2023) Inclusions and exclusions in treeline definitions. J Biogeogr 51(1):54-56, doi:10.1111/jbi.14729 [ABSTRACT-READ]. (CrossRef lists year/volume as 51(1) 2023.)
- Thermal limitation (global): growing-season mean air temperature 6.6 ± 0.3 °C as global thermal-treeline indicator; "around two-thirds of uppermost tree distribution records significantly deviated from it", drought the primary driver in 51% of cases — Xie Y et al. 2024, Glob Change Biol 30(4):e17260, doi:10.1111/gcb.17260 [ABSTRACT-READ]. Caveat: quasi-observational Google Earth dataset, global; not Alpine-specific. Companion: Xie Y et al. 2025, PNAS 122(33):e2504685122, doi:10.1073/pnas.2504685122 ("heat deficits ... consistently restrict the presence of treeline; moisture gradients drive the taxonomic variation") [ABSTRACT-READ]. Earlier basis (Körner & Paulsen 2004, 6.7 °C) is in prior notes.
- Treeline form / krummholz: treelines "with a diffuse form were more likely to have advanced than those with an abrupt or krummholz form" — Harsch M et al. 2009, Ecol Lett 12(10):1040-1049, doi:10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01355.x [ABSTRACT-READ]. (Prior wiki notes cite this paper without a DOI in the text; this is the verified DOI.)
- Ingrowth/infilling vs true upward shift: "10% of the new forest areas were identified as true upward shifts whereas 90% represented ingrowth" above 1650 m, with "both land use and climate change as likely drivers" — Gehrig-Fasel J, Guisan A, Zimmermann NE 2007, J Veg Sci 18(4):571-582, doi:10.1111/j.1654-1103.2007.tb02571.x [ABSTRACT-READ via OpenAlex]. Frei et al. 2023: "Newly established spruce mostly represented infilling below the previous treeline" — Forests 14(2):412, doi:10.3390/f14020412 [ABSTRACT-READ via OpenAlex].
- Equilibrium / lag: "tree advances lag behind the upslope shift of the treeline isotherm, on average, by more than 200 m" — Körner & Hiltbrunner 2024, Reg Environ Change 24(3):98, doi:10.1007/s10113-024-02259-8 [ABSTRACT-READ (excerpt)]. Lag-vs-densification framing: Shi H et al. 2022, Agric For Meteorol 318:108917, doi:10.1016/j.agrformet.2022.108917 ("Climate warming will widen the lagging gap of global treeline shift relative to densification") [METADATA-ONLY]. Range-pinning/Allee concept: Büntgen U et al. 2024, Glob Change Biol 30(4):e17288, doi:10.1111/gcb.17288 [METADATA-ONLY].
- Land-use legacy / abandonment: Tasser et al. 2007: abandonment occurred in every region over 150 years, "between 8 and 67% of previously used land is now abandoned", seed dispersal and continued use strongest influences on regrowth; snow gliding/avalanches damage young trees — Agric Ecosyst Environ 118(1-4):115-129, doi:10.1016/j.agee.2006.05.004 [ABSTRACT-VIA-SEARCH]. Meta-analysis: Anselmetto N, Weisberg PJ, Garbarino M 2024, Landsc Urban Plan 243:104973, doi:10.1016/j.landurbplan.2023.104973: "We observed a lack of studies in the northeastern region of the Alps"; population density, road density and the agricultural vs industrial workforce share most correlated with reforestation [ABSTRACT-READ (excerpt)]. Legacy structure: Marengo G et al. 2025, Landsc Urban Plan 259:105357, doi:10.1016/j.landurbplan.2025.105357 (western Alps, 1954-2017; "land-use legacy" types) [ABSTRACT-READ (excerpt)]. Cadastral legacy of greening: Bayle A et al. 2025, Prog Phys Geogr 49(6):753-772, doi:10.1177/03091333251378970: greening "aligns with the historical structure of cadastral parcels" [ABSTRACT-READ].
- Avalanche / geomorphic disturbance: Kulakowski, Bebi & Rixen 2010 (Davos, 1954 to ~1999): "Change in land use was the most important variable contributing to changes in landscape forest structure, followed by changes in the disturbance regime, then changes in GDD" — Oikos 120(2):216-225, doi:10.1111/j.1600-0706.2010.18726.x [ABSTRACT-READ]. Bebi P, Kulakowski D, Rixen C 2009, For Ecol Manage 257(9):1883-1892, doi:10.1016/j.foreco.2009.01.050 [METADATA-ONLY, abstract not retrieved]. Leonelli et al. 2011 (AMBIO 40(3):264-273, doi:10.1007/s13280-010-0096-2): treeline "mainly controlled by human impacts and geomorphological factors" in three western Italian valleys [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY]. Leonelli et al. 2009, Phys Geogr 30(4):338-352, doi:10.2747/0272-3646.30.4.338 [METADATA-ONLY]. Gorsic S et al. 2025, Geomorphology 489:109985, doi:10.1016/j.geomorph.2025.109985 ("Unabated forest expansion despite increased rockfall frequency", Täschgufer, Switzerland) [METADATA-ONLY, abstract not retrieved].
- Greening vs structural change: "most of the alpine greening in this area can be attributed to forest dynamics, including forest ingrowth and treeline upward shift" and greening highest where trees were already established in the mid-1980s — Bayle A et al. 2025, Remote Sens Ecol Conserv 11(4):375-393, doi:10.1002/rse2.430 [ABSTRACT-READ]. This resolves the proposal's "journal unconfirmed" note: the journal is RSEC, volume 11(4), 2025; the paper is French Alps (SW), not a Tyrol result.
- Browsing: no treeline-specific browsing quantification found in the Alps (see Q5). Wieser et al. 2019 (prior notes) attributes seedling re-establishment to reduced grazing.

### Inferences
- A reader needs one sentence stating which boundary is measured. Best pairing for the Introduction: Körner & Hoch 2023 for the strict thermal concept, Malanson 2023 as the counter-position, and Harsch 2009 for form (diffuse/abrupt/krummholz).
- "Minimum detectable shift" has no definitional source in the treeline literature I found. It remains the project's own construct (pixel size plus geolocation error); Menegaldo's Landsat-vs-orthophoto difference (+32 vs +45 m) is the only empirical anchor.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Austrian source for numeric thresholds of Waldgrenze/Baumgrenze/Baumartengrenze found; Körner's book "Alpine Treelines" (2012) was not checked, nor was the full text of Körner & Hoch.
- Bebi 2009, Leonelli 2009, Motta 2006, Vittoz 2008 abstracts not retrieved (publisher pages blocked); do not quote numbers from them.

## Q4. Austrian / Tyrolean context: ÖWI, alm abandonment, browsing, avalanches

### Takeaway
Official statistics show forest gain concentrated in the alm and subalpine zone, a ~10% decline in alm fodder area in 2000/03-2009 (strongest in Tirol, among others), and high game influence. None of these are forest-line position series; all are coarse, differently defined, and none is peer reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- Tyrolean forest area growth: "Seit den 60 er Jahren wächst die Waldfläche in Tirol langsam aber sicher. Jährlich kommen ca. 800 ha Wald, vor allem in der Almregion und der subalpinen Stufe dazu." — Land Tirol, Waldinventur page, https://www.tirol.gv.at/umwelt/wald/waldzustand/waldinventur [fetched, verbatim; undated]. The page states the Austrian Forest Inventory has surveyed on a systematic sample grid since 1961 and does not mention the upper forest line. A figure of 521,000 ha / 41.2% forest (Waldbericht 2011) and ~6,000 ha added since 2000/02 came only from a search-tool paraphrase [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY].
- National ÖWI trend (search paraphrase of a BFW publication; not checked on the source): annual gain slowing 7,700 ha (1992/96) to 3,400 ha (2016/18) [SNIPPET/TLDR-ONLY].
- Alm statistics: Groier M (2011) "Die neue Almstatistik 2009", Ländlicher Raum 12/2011 (BMLFUW online journal; PDF read via pdftotext). Verbatim: "Im Untersuchungszeitraum 2000/03-2009 ist die Almfutterfläche insgesamt um knapp 10 % stärker zurückgegangen als die Anzahl der Almbetriebe, während die Gesamtalmfläche annähernd konstant blieb. Regional gesehen sind die stärksten Abnahmen vor allem in den Bundesländern Tirol, Steiermark, Vorarlberg und Kärnten". Also: alm numbers and area declined since the 1952 survey, fodder area most, and the last earlier survey was 1986 [FULLTEXT-PARTLY-READ]. The sentence is ambiguous (decline relative to the number of farms vs absolute) and the authors warn that the 1952 and 2009 data are only conditionally comparable ("nur bedingt verglichen") and that survey methods changed.
- Land Tirol publishes Almstatistik for 2007 and 2009 (tirol.gv.at downloads) [search results only; not opened]. No continuous Tyrolean series of alm numbers or alm area was found.
- Game influence monitoring (WEM): BFW report Schodterer H, Kainz C (2022) Wildeinflussmonitoring 2019-2021, BFW-Praxisinformation 55, 69 pp. (summary page on waldwissen.net, fetched): Tirol average "hat sich über die ersten fünf Perioden etwas verschlechtert" and is about equal between periods 5 and 6; Schutzwald result relatively constant [FULLTEXT-PARTLY-READ of web summary]. Land Tirol's current page states a "most recent survey period 2022-2024" with strong game influence at 44.0% of examined points in Tirol (sentence truncated in the fetch tool's output) [fetched, partial verbatim].
- Avalanches: see Q1 (Kulakowski 2010; Bebi 2009 metadata only; Tasser 2007 notes snow gliding damage).
- Post-abandonment reforestation: Tasser 2007 (Q1) is the key Eastern-Alps source (South Tyrol Passeier plus municipal scale), 8-67% of previously used land abandoned over 150 years.

### Inferences
- The "800 ha per year, mostly alm region and subalpine zone" statement supports "forest gain concentrated near the upper forest limit" but cannot be turned into a forest-line rate in m/yr.
- The Almfutterfläche decline gives a Tirol-specific, 2000-2009 abandonment signal at coarse (Bundesland) resolution, plausible for the proposal's WP3 land-use predictor but not a spatial layer.

### Gaps
- ÖWI forest-line or altitude-resolved change in a peer-reviewed publication: not found.
- WEM sampling design (regeneration-area plots vs ecotone), point-level release and subalpine-zone results: not found.
- Browsing impact at the Tyrolean forest line in the literature: not found. Only unrelated Alpine regeneration studies (e.g. Frei et al. 2024 Forestry, silver fir; Unkule 2022) surfaced; they are montane, so not listed.

## Q5. Tests of proposal claims marked [verify] or load-bearing in this theme

- "Strong game influence at 41.8% of Tyrol survey points 2019-2021 (waldwissen.net)": the 41.8%/2019-2021 figure could not be reproduced on any page I fetched. The waldwissen summary does not contain it; the current Land Tirol page gives 44.0% for 2022-2024. Recommendation: cite the BFW report (Schodterer & Kainz 2022) directly, and update to 2022-2024 if citing the Land Tirol page. The 41.8% figure came only from a search-tool paraphrase. Also note the reference in the proposal to waldwissen.net as source is for 2019-2021 overall WEM, not for 41.8%.
- "WEM samples regeneration areas, not the ecotone": not confirmed or refuted (sampling details not on the pages read). Keep as [verify].
- "No Tyrol-wide abandonment time series found": consistent with this round; the closest are the national Almstatistik 2009 (Groier 2011) with Bundesland-level change 2000/03-2009 and the Land Tirol Almstatistik PDFs for 2007/2009 (unopened). Not a spatial layer.
- "Exact forest-line elevation (1900-2300 m) from the Tyrolean inventory [verify]": unresolved. Related anchors: uppermost P. cembra >2 m at about 2500 m in the Defereggen (Körner & Hiltbrunner 2024; that is a tree line, not a forest line), and afforestation sites at 1600-2100 m (Schindlbacher 2026).
- "Swiss ingrowth was 90% into former pasture above 1650 m (Gehrig-Fasel 2007)": the abstract supports "90% represented ingrowth" above 1650 m and states both land use and climate change as likely drivers; whether the ingrowth went into "former pasture" specifically is posed as a question in the abstract, not as a result. Suggested rewording: "90% of new forest above 1650 m was ingrowth rather than true upward shift". The label "land-use-dominated" for Gehrig-Fasel is stronger than the abstract.
