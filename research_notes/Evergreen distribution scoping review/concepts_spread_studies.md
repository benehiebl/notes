## Q1. Which papers define or introduce the concepts; resolution of unmatched items

### Takeaway
"Mediterranization" as a measured process is used in Hernández et al. 2017 (NE Spain, Navarra) for Q. ilex gain in a submediterranean zone; this is almost certainly the earlier unmatched "Hernández / Peñuelas mediterranization" item (the Peñuelas part is most likely a conflation with Peñuelas & Boada 2003). "Laurophyllisation" has a dedicated chapter by Walther (2001) plus the Ticino cluster; I could not verify who coined it. Jump 2009 and Trindade 2020 could not be identified with confidence. No paper defining "tropicalization of forests", "mediterraneisation of Italian forests" or "laurofillizzazione" was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Hernández et al. 2017 = the "mediterranization" paper.** Hernández L, Sánchez de Dios R, Montes F, Sainz-Ollero H, Cañellas I (2017) Exploring range shifts of contrasting tree species across a bioclimatic transition zone. Eur J For Res 136(3):481-492. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10342-017-1047-2. [ABSTRACT-VIA-SEARCH] (Semantic Scholar record + search summary; the Springer abstract itself was not fetchable; CrossRef has no abstract). Content per both summaries: Navarra (N Spain), spanish national forest inventory 1971 vs 2010, kriging, four species (Q. subpyrenaica, Q. ilex, Pinus sylvestris, Fagus sylvatica). Presence probability rose for all four across Navarra; within the submediterranean transition zone three declined while Q. ilex expanded; quoted phrase from the search summary: results "supported a previous hypothesis of a 'mediterranization'"; authors conclude climate warming is the major driver with moderate human influence. Limits: presence probability from interpolated inventory plots, two dates, climate attribution not formally tested against land use (not in the summary). Supports: Introduction "spread" (the only inventory-based statement of Q. ilex gaining on cooler-adapted species at a bioclimatic transition) and "drivers" (authors favour warming, contrast with Conedera 2018). Needs abstract verification at uibk.
- **Walther G-R (2001) Laurophyllisation, a sign of a changing climate?** In: Tasks for Vegetation Science, pp. 207-223. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-015-9686-2_13. [METADATA-ONLY]. And Walther G-R, Carraro G, Klötzli F (2001) Evergreen broad-leaved species as indicators for climate change. In: "Fingerprints" of Climate Change, pp. 151-162. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4419-8692-4_10. [METADATA-ONLY]. These are the dedicated laurophyllisation concept chapters (titles confirm). The earlier review listed Walther 2001 (Carraro) as METADATA-ONLY; the chapter titles are now CrossRef-confirmed. Who first used the word "laurophyllisation" is NOT verified (the earlier review points to Gianoni, Carraro & Klötzli 1988 and Klötzli & Walther eds. 1999 as the cluster).
- **Jump 2009**: no hit. CrossRef free-text search for "Jump 2009 Mediterranean holm oak" returned nothing relevant. The earlier review already notes the real Jump paper is Jump, Hunt & Peñuelas 2006 (GCB 12:2163). A possibly intended alternative is Hampe A, Jump AS (2011) Climate relicts: past, present, future. Annu Rev Ecol Evol Syst 42:313-333, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-102710-145015, [ABSTRACT-READ], but that is only my guess and about refugia/rear edges, not EVE spread. Treat Jump 2009 as unidentified.
- **Trindade 2020**: CrossRef search "Trindade 2020 evergreen oak" gave nothing relevant. Unidentified; ask the user for title/journal.
- **Non-equilibrium / dispersal limitation.** Svenning J-C, Skov F (2004) Limited filling of the potential range in European tree species. Ecol Lett 7(7):565-573. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2004.00614.x. [ABSTRACT-READ]. "mean R/P = 38.3% (+/-30.3% SD)" for 55 tree species; low filling "primarily" read as dispersal limitation; "we expect European tree species to show only limited tracking of near-future climate changes." Limits: atlas data, bioclimatic envelope potential ranges, all trees (not evergreens specifically). Supports: concept "non-equilibrium range"; companion to Alessi 2019 (which uses Beals' index).
- **Propagule pressure.** Simberloff D (2009) The role of propagule pressure in biological invasions. Annu Rev Ecol Evol Syst 40:81-102. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ecolsys.110308.120304. [ABSTRACT-READ]: evidence "implicates propagule pressure - propagule sizes, propagule numbers, and temporal and spatial patterns of propagule arrival - as important in both [establishment and spread]". Supports: definition behind Conedera 2018's driver ranking. Invasion-biology review, not forest-specific.
- **Thermophilization.** Gottfried M et al. (2012) Continent-wide response of mountain vegetation to climate change. Nat Clim Change 2:111-115. https://doi.org/10.1038/nclimate1329. [METADATA-ONLY here]. Bertrand 2011, Lenoir 2008, Zellweger 2020 already in the earlier review (herbs/understorey). For EVE the term is used loosely; no EVE-specific definition found.
- **Forest transition / land abandonment.** Meyfroidt P, Rudel TK, Lambin EF (2010) Forest transitions, trade, and the global displacement of land use. PNAS 107(49):20917-20922. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1014773107. [ABSTRACT-READ]; defines "forest transitions" as "a shift from net deforestation to net reforestation" but is tropical/trade focused; cite only for the term. For Italy use Garbarino 2020, Malandra 2018, Vitali 2018 (already in earlier review). Mather 1992 / Rudel 2005 original forest-transition papers were not retrieved.
- **Mesophication** (opposite process in eastern US deciduous forests): Nowacki GJ, Abrams MD (2008) BioScience 58(2):123-138. https://doi.org/10.1641/b580207. [METADATA-ONLY]. Not an EVE concept; mention only if the paper discusses shade-casting shifts.
- **Tropicalization / "evergreen encroachment" in forests**: no relevant forest paper found (CrossRef hits were marine, bamboo, urban). Drop these terms or define them explicitly as own wording.

### Inferences
- The conceptual vocabulary in the literature is: laurophyllisation (Ticino/Insubria, climbers, shrubs, palm), "mediterranization" (Iberian transition zones, Q. ilex), "biome shift" (Peñuelas & Boada 2003; also used in the 2024 Ticino palm paper title), "range filling/non-equilibrium" (Svenning & Skov).
- The paper should state in the text that "evergreen encroachment" and "tropicalization" are own wording.

### Gaps
- Coining paper of "laurophyllisation" unverified; Gianoni 1988 / Klötzli & Walther 1999 not accessible.
- Hernández 2017 abstract not verbatim-read; Jump 2009 and Trindade 2020 unidentified.

## Q2. Evidence of Q. ilex / EVE expansion by region (charted studies)

### Takeaway
New observed-expansion evidence is thin. Beyond the earlier review, the only new inventory-based Q. ilex expansion is Hernández 2017 (Navarra); Urli 2013 (Spain, 10 yr) found NO shift for Mediterranean species at their core. For Italy, the Alps/Prealps, Slovenia/Croatia, Austria, and Balkans I found NO peer-reviewed observed-change study of Q. ilex; only palaeo and modelling. Conclusion: the "Italian expansion" statement must rely on Alessi 2019/2021, Conedera 2018 (Ticino) and be framed as a gap.

### Inferences
- The only evidence for deciduous-to-Q. ilex replacement from a time-comparison design is Peñuelas & Boada 2003 and Hernández 2017 (both NE Spain) plus Delzon 2013 (France). Urli 2013 shows the opposite (no change) for core-range Mediterranean species, so spread is region and range-position dependent. The Italian paper can legitimately say that Italian evidence is lacking.
- Vayreda 2016 and Padullés Cubino 2024 both attribute Spanish composition change mainly to land-use legacy/forest densification rather than warming, consistent with Conedera 2018 (Ticino).

### Gaps
- No observed Q. ilex or EVE change for Italy, the Alps or Austria beyond Ticino cluster; Italian-language grey literature not retrievable.
